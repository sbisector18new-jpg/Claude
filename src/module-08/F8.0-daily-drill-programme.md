Module 8 is **80 planning marks — the largest single block in the paper**, larger than Labour Laws.
And Blueprint §10.2 item 9 says it must be *"never a block; distributed daily practice from week 1."*

Those two facts together create the problem this programme solves. A module that is never a block is a
module with no obvious moment to start, which is exactly why it gets postponed — and postponing the
largest block in the paper is the most expensive mistake available to you.

## 0. The Honest Position on This Programme

Two things you should know before relying on it.

**What this programme is.** A **scheduler over question banks that already exist**. Across Modules 6, 7,
9 and 8 this manual now contains **several hundred practice questions** with worked explanations. The
calendar in §3 assigns them to specific days in an order designed to interleave subjects and revisit
material at spaced intervals.

**What it is not.** It is not several hundred *new* questions written for the calendar. Authoring a
fresh drill set for every one of 72 days at the standard of the chapter question banks is not something
I can do honestly in one sitting, and a padded set of low-quality items would be worse than none —
you would be practising against questions that do not resemble the paper.

::: note | NOTE · WHAT THAT MEANS IN PRACTICE
For roughly the **first six weeks** the calendar draws on the chapter banks, which is more than enough
material. From **week 7** it moves to **second-pass and error-log re-drilling**, which is not a
weakness — spaced repetition of questions you got wrong is more valuable than fresh questions you get
right.

Where you need genuinely new volume beyond that, the honest answer is **previous years' papers and a
commercial question bank**, used as raw material. Blueprint §22 permits coaching material as
*supplementary* — this is exactly the case it was written for.
:::

## 1. The Thirty-Minute Template

Blueprint §11.1 divides your evening hour into 10 minutes of active recall, 20 minutes of
consolidation, and 30 minutes of conversion practice. **This programme owns that last 30 minutes.**

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| **0 – 2** | Read the day's cell in the §3 calendar. Open the named question set. Note the start time |
| **2 – 22** | **Attempt the questions. Timed. No looking anything up.** Write answers on paper, not in your head |
| **22 – 28** | Check. **Read the explanation for every question, including the ones you got right** |
| **28 – 30** | **Log every error** in the §5 format. This step is the one that compounds |

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE TWO RULES THAT MAKE THE THIRTY MINUTES WORK
**Rule 1 — the evening subject must differ from the morning subject.** Blueprint §11.1 requires it, and
the calendar in §3 enforces it. Interleaving different material improves retention substantially more
than massing the same subject, and it also guarantees English and Quant get daily attention without
ever needing a dedicated block of weeks.

**Rule 2 — time yourself from day one.** The RT gives you **120 questions in 120 minutes**. An untimed
correct answer is worth very little, because the constraint that decides your score is not whether you
*can* solve a mensuration problem but whether you can solve it in **ninety seconds**. Practising
untimed builds a skill you cannot use.
:::

### 1.1 Your per-question time budget

The paper averages one minute per question, but the average conceals the strategy. Some formats must be
done faster than average so that others can be done slower.

| Format | Target time | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Vocabulary in context** | **20–30 seconds** | You either know the word or you do not. Deliberating does not help |
| Grammar, error spotting | 30–40 seconds | Pattern recognition |
| **Para jumbles** | **60–75 seconds** for a set | Find the opener, then chain |
| **Reading comprehension** | **5 minutes for the whole passage set** | Read once properly, then answer all five |
| Arithmetic, ratio, percentage | 45–60 seconds | Mostly one-step once set up |
| **Mensuration, geometry** | **75–90 seconds** | Formula plus arithmetic |
| **Data interpretation** | **3 minutes for a set of three** | One reading of the table serves all three |
| Labour law, accounting recall | 20–30 seconds | Recall or move on |

**The arithmetic that matters:** if you spend 25 seconds on each of 10 vocabulary questions instead of
60, you bank **six minutes** — which is four extra mensuration problems. Speed on the cheap formats is
what buys time for the expensive ones.

### 2.2 Quantitative Aptitude — 15 questions in 2023

Mensuration of a hemisphere and of a melted disc · circle geometry · quadratic roots · probability ·
ratio and ages · mixtures · **three** data-interpretation questions · heights and distances · mean of
weights.

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE TWO FINDINGS THAT SHOULD CHANGE YOUR STUDY PLAN
**Finding 1 — vocabulary is half the English block.** Ten of twenty questions. No other single format in
the entire paper carries that concentration. It is also the format most improved by **daily drip
exposure** rather than by study sessions, which is the strongest argument for this programme's
existence. §4 gives you the drip.

**Finding 2 — there was only ONE pure reasoning question.** One, out of 120. Blueprint §4.4 records it

That second finding is worth pausing on, because "General Mental Ability" appears in the official
syllabus and every commercial book for this exam devotes hundreds of pages to reasoning — syllogisms,
blood relations, coding-decoding, seating arrangements. On the 2023 evidence that is a **very poor use
of your hours**. Module 8 therefore treats reasoning as **one compact chapter (F8.8)**, not as a third
of the module. If you have a reasoning book, use it as a doorstop until the core is safe.

## 4. The Speed Material

Two kinds of thing belong in a daily drill and nowhere else: **numbers you should never compute**, and
**words you should already know**.

### 4.1 Numbers you should never compute

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · SQUARES TO 30, AND THE FRACTION EQUIVALENTS
Every one of these should be instant recall. Computing them mid-question wastes the seconds you need
for the actual problem.

**Squares 1 to 30:**

| n | n² | n | n² | n | n² |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | 121 | 18 | 324 | 25 | 625 |
| 12 | 144 | 19 | 361 | 26 | 676 |
| 13 | 169 | 20 | 400 | 27 | 729 |
| 14 | 196 | 21 | 441 | 28 | 784 |
| 15 | 225 | 22 | 484 | 29 | 841 |
| 16 | 256 | 23 | 529 | 30 | 900 |
| 17 | 289 | 24 | 576 | | |

**Cubes 1 to 12:** 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343, 512, 729, 1000, 1331, 1728

**Fraction, decimal and percentage equivalents** — the single highest-value table for arithmetic speed:

| Fraction | Percentage | Fraction | Percentage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | 50% | 1/8 | 12.5% |
| 1/3 | 33⅓% | 1/9 | 11⅑% |
| 1/4 | 25% | 1/11 | 9¹/₁₁% |
| 1/5 | 20% | 1/12 | 8⅓% |
| 1/6 | 16⅔% | 1/16 | 6.25% |
| 1/7 | 14²/₇% | 1/20 | 5% |

**Why this table matters more than it looks.** A question asking for "an increase of 16⅔%" is asking you
to **multiply by 7/6**. Recognising the fraction turns a decimal calculation into a one-step
multiplication. Almost every percentage question in an aptitude paper is built on these equivalents.

**Powers of 2:** 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024 — needed for F9.5's number systems too.
:::

### 4.2 The vocabulary drip — ten words a day

Vocabulary is **half the English block** and is the only topic in the paper that responds better to
**five minutes daily for twelve weeks** than to twelve hours in one week. Words need repeated exposure
at intervals; they cannot be crammed.

**The protocol:**

1. Take **ten words** a day from the list in **F8.1 §5**
2. For each, write the word, its meaning, and **one sentence of your own using it**
3. The next day, before taking ten new words, **cover the meanings and test yourself on yesterday's ten**
4. Every Saturday, test yourself on all sixty from that week
5. Any word you fail twice goes into the **error log** and is re-tested weekly

Ten words a day for twelve weeks is **840 exposures over 720 words** — which, at the observed weight of
ten vocabulary questions in the paper, is the best-returning five minutes in this entire manual.

## 5. The Error Log

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE SINGLE HIGHEST-VALUE HABIT IN THE PROGRAMME
Everything else in this document is scheduling. **This section is the part that actually raises your
score.**

A question you got wrong and understood is worth several questions you got right. But that only holds
if you **return to it**, and you will not return to it unless it is written down somewhere you look.

Keep one page per subject, in this format:

| Date | Source | What I chose | Correct | **Why I got it wrong — in my own words** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 Aug | F8.5 Q14 | 62.5% | 60% | Used 5/8 instead of 3/5. Did not check the fraction |
| 18 Aug | F8.1 Q7 | *reticent* | *recalcitrant* | Guessed from a similar-sounding word |

**The fourth column is the whole point.** "Silly mistake" is not a diagnosis and teaches nothing. The
diagnosis must be specific enough to be actionable — *"I applied the percentage before deducting"*,
*"I read 'not correct' as 'correct'"*, *"I did not convert km/h to m/s"*.

Because when you have twenty entries you will find that **four or five root causes produce most of your
errors**, and those four or five are what you fix. That is a far smaller and more tractable problem
than "get better at Quant".
:::

### 5.1 The weekly review, fifteen minutes on Saturday

1. Re-attempt every error from the week **without looking at the answer**
2. Any error you repeat gets a **star** — starred items are re-tested every Saturday until clean
3. Count your root causes and name the **top three** for the week
4. Write one line: *what will I do differently next week?*

::: think | THINK LIKE UPSC · THE THREE ERROR CAUSES THAT WILL DOMINATE YOUR LOG
Predicted from the trap patterns across this manual, and worth watching for from day one.

1. **Missing the word "not".** Blueprint §6 records that about **20%** of the 2023 paper used the "which
   one is **not** correct" format. Blueprint §8's remedy is a two-second habit: **re-read the last six
   words of the stem before you fill any bubble**
2. **Unit and conversion failures.** km/h to m/s (F9.1 §2.2), percentage before deduction (F6.3 §5.3),
   the wrong power of ten in pH (F9.3 §3.2). These are not knowledge failures, and they are the cheapest
   to eliminate
3. **Formula swaps within a matched pair.** Years'-purchase versus capitalisation (F6.4), mirror versus
   lens (F9.2), n(n−1) versus n(n−1)/2 (F9.7). The examiner offers the other member of the pair,
   correctly computed
:::

## 6. Attempt Strategy for the Real Paper

Restating the two calculations from Blueprint §1.2 and §1.5, because the drill exists to serve them.

**The marking scheme.** +2.5 for a correct answer, **−0.8333** for a wrong one, 0 for a blank. A blind
guess among four options has an expected value of **exactly zero** — not negative.

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE ATTEMPT RULE
**If you can eliminate even one option, you must attempt the question.** Eliminating one leaves an
expected value of +0.28; eliminating two, +0.83.

Over-cautious candidates leave 20 to 25 such questions blank and give away **six to eight marks of pure
expected value**.

Leave a question blank only in two situations: you have **no basis whatever** for elimination, or you
feel actively **pulled towards a familiar-looking option** — which usually means you have half-recognised
a trap.

**Your target: about 100 attempts at 80% accuracy**, which gives roughly 183 of 300. Attempting more
only pays if accuracy holds — 110 attempts at 70% scores *less* than 100 at 80%.
:::

### 6.1 The three-pass method

Do not work through the paper in order. Three passes:

| Pass | What you do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **First** | Answer everything you **know immediately**. Skip anything needing calculation or deliberation | about 45 min |
| **Second** | Return to the **calculable** ones — mensuration, DI, arithmetic. You now know how much time you have per question | about 55 min |
| **Third** | **Eliminate and guess** on the rest. Fill every bubble where you can rule out one option | about 20 min |

The reason this works: the paper mixes formats of wildly different cost, and the order is not sorted by
difficulty. A 20-second vocabulary question sitting at number 95 is worth the same 2.5 marks as a
90-second mensuration problem at number 12. **A single pass in order means you may never reach the cheap
marks.**
