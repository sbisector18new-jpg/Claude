## 1. Session 1 — The Format ★★★☆☆

Vocabulary questions come in four shapes, and all four appeared in some form in the papers analysed:

| Shape | What it asks |
|---|---|
| **Meaning in context** | A word is used in a sentence; choose the closest meaning |
| **Fill in the blank** | Choose the word that best completes the sentence |
| **Synonym or antonym** | Choose the word closest or opposite in meaning |
| **Confusable pair** | Choose the correct one of two similar words |

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · TWENTY TO THIRTY SECONDS, AND THEN MOVE
F8.0 §1.1 sets your target at **20 to 30 seconds** per vocabulary question, and the reason is worth
stating plainly: **deliberating does not help.** Either the word is in your vocabulary, or the techniques
in §2 will get you to an answer in twenty seconds, or neither will — and a further ninety seconds of
staring changes nothing.

Ten vocabulary questions at 25 seconds is **four minutes**. The same ten at 70 seconds is **twelve
minutes** — and the eight minutes lost are five mensuration problems you will not reach.

**This is the format where speed is worth more than certainty.** Answer, mark it, move.
:::

## 2. Session 2 — Five Techniques for Words You Do Not Know ★★★★★

You will meet words you have never seen. These five techniques, in this order, will get you to an answer
far more often than guessing.

### 2.1 Connotation — is the sentence praising or criticising?

**The most powerful technique, and the fastest.** Before considering meaning at all, ask whether the
sentence carries a **positive** or **negative** charge. Then eliminate every option of the wrong charge.

> *"The employer's conduct in concealing wage records was **egregious**."*

You may not know *egregious*. But "concealing wage records" is plainly **negative**, so any positive
option — commendable, generous — is gone immediately. That often leaves two, and a 50% guess has an
expected value of +0.83 marks.

**Signposts of charge:** *unfortunately, failed, despite, concealed, refused* signal negative;
*successfully, commendably, achieved, ensured* signal positive.

### 2.2 Contrast and continuation signals

The connective tells you whether the missing word **agrees** with the rest of the sentence or **opposes**
it.

| Signal | Relationship | Words |
|---|---|---|
| **Contrast** | The blank **opposes** the other half | but, although, though, however, whereas, despite, notwithstanding, **far from**, rather than |
| **Continuation** | The blank **agrees** | and, moreover, furthermore, indeed, similarly, likewise |
| **Cause and effect** | The blank follows from the other half | because, therefore, consequently, since, hence |

> *"**Far from** being **______**, the penalty was widely regarded as lenient."*

"Far from" signals contrast, and the other half says *lenient*. So the blank must mean the **opposite of
lenient** — severe, harsh, **draconian**. The sentence has told you the answer without your knowing any
of the options.

### 2.3 Word roots

Covered fully in §3. A word you have never seen may still decompose: *malfeasance* contains **mal-**
(bad), *benevolent* contains **bene-** (good).

### 2.4 Part of speech

The grammatical slot often eliminates options outright. If the blank follows *"a matter of"*, it needs a
**noun** — so *principle*, not *principal* used adjectivally, and certainly not *principally*.

### 2.5 Collocation — what actually goes with what

Some words simply pair with others by convention. *Protestations* are **vehement**; a point is
**moot**; credulity is **strained**; a fort is **held**. If an option produces a phrase no one would
ever say, it is wrong even if its dictionary meaning fits.

::: think | THINK LIKE UPSC · THE ORDER TO APPLY THESE IN
Under time pressure, run them in this sequence and stop as soon as one option survives alone:

1. **Charge** — positive or negative? Eliminate the wrong charge
2. **Signal** — contrast or continuation? Does the blank agree or oppose?
3. **Root** — can I decompose any option?
4. **Part of speech** — does the slot need a noun, verb or adjective?
5. **Collocation** — does the phrase sound like English?

Steps 1 and 2 alone resolve a large share of questions in **under fifteen seconds**, and they require no
vocabulary at all. That is why they come first.
:::

| **voc, vok** | call, voice | *vociferous, evoke, equivocal* |

::: memory | MEMORY TECHNIQUE · TWO ROOTS THAT PAY FOR THEMSELVES
**greg = flock.** A *gregarious* person likes the flock. To *segregate* is to separate from the flock. And
**egregious** — literally *out of the flock* — meant "standing out", and came to mean standing out for
being **outstandingly bad**. One root, three exam words.

**scrib = write**, with the prefix deciding everything: to **pre**scribe is to write *for* — to
recommend. To **pro**scribe is to write *against* — to forbid. **They are near-opposites and they are
routinely offered against each other.** A doctor prescribes a medicine; a law proscribes a practice.
:::

## 4. Session 4 — Confusable Pairs ★★★★☆

The exam's cheapest question type, and the easiest to lose. Each pair differs by one or two letters and
entirely in meaning.

| Pair | Meanings |
|---|---|
| **affect / effect** | affect = to influence (verb) · effect = a result (noun), or to bring about (verb) |
| **principal / principle** | principal = chief, or a sum of money · **principle = a rule or belief** |
| **stationary / stationery** | stationary = not moving · **stationery = writing materials** |
| **eminent / imminent** | eminent = distinguished · **imminent = about to happen** |
| **prescribe / proscribe** | prescribe = to recommend · **proscribe = to forbid** |
| **complement / compliment** | complement = to complete · compliment = to praise |
| **discreet / discrete** | discreet = tactful · **discrete = separate, distinct** |
| **elicit / illicit** | elicit = to draw out · **illicit = unlawful** |
| **ingenious / ingenuous** | ingenious = clever · **ingenuous = naive, artless** |
| **council / counsel** | council = an assembly · counsel = advice, or a lawyer |
| **censure / censor** | censure = to criticise formally · censor = to suppress content |
| **adapt / adopt** | adapt = to adjust · adopt = to take up |
| **precede / proceed** | precede = to come before · proceed = to go forward |
| **accede / exceed** | accede = to agree or yield · exceed = to go beyond |
| **moral / morale** | moral = ethical · morale = spirit, confidence |
| **respectfully / respectively** | respectfully = with respect · **respectively = in the order given** |

::: trap | COMMON UPSC TRAP · THE THREE PAIRS MOST OFTEN TESTED
1. **principal / principle.** *Principle* is the **rule** — and it is the one with the noun-only meaning.
   "A matter of principle." Mnemonic: a **rule** ends in **-le**, like princip**le**
2. **prescribe / proscribe.** Near-opposites, from §3.2. **Pro-scribe forbids**
3. **stationary / stationery.** **Station-e-ry** contains **e** for **envelopes**; station**a**ry
   contains **a**, and means **at rest**

Note also that several of these words appear naturally in the administrative register this examination
favours — *censure*, *accede*, *proscribe*, *ex parte* — which is not a coincidence. The setter draws
from the vocabulary of governance.
:::

::: world | FROM YOUR OWN WORLD · FOUR OF THESE ARE PART OF YOUR FUTURE JOB
**Ex parte**, **prima facie**, **bona fide** and **sine qua non** are not decorative Latin — they are
working terms in the quasi-judicial process F6.1 §1.4 described.

A **Section 7A** determination made without the employer appearing is an **ex parte** order. A **prima
facie** case is what justifies opening an inquiry. A **bona fide** dispute is treated differently from an
evasion. And a hearing is a **sine qua non** of natural justice — the condition without which the order
cannot stand.

So this row of the table is simultaneously English vocabulary and Module 3 procedure. Learn them once
and they pay twice, including in the interview.
:::

### 8.1 Level 1

| 3 | d | **convincing.** *Cogent* argues compellingly |

| 5 | a | **courage in adversity.** *Fortitude* is moral endurance, not physical strength — option (b) is the trap |

| 7 | b | **extremely careful** |

| 9 | c | **using more words than needed.** From *verb* = word |

|---|:-:|---|
| 16 | b | **accede** = to yield or agree. *Exceed* is to go beyond — the confusable pair from §4 |

::: think | THINK LIKE UPSC · THE THREE SHAPES IN THIS BANK
1. **The synonym offered as an antonym.** Q18, Q23 and Q30 each place three near-synonyms of the given
   word among the options. When a question asks for the **opposite**, the danger is not ignorance but
   inattention — underline the word "opposite" before reading the options
2. **The sentence that answers itself.** Q31 and Q37 need no vocabulary. A contrast signal plus the other
   half of the sentence determines the charge of the missing word. These are the fastest marks in the
   English block, and they are free
3. **The cross-matched pair.** Q34 gives you four combinations of two words and two meanings. Only one
   is right, and knowing *either* word settles it
