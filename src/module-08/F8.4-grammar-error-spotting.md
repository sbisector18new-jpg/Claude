::: note | NOTE · THE HONEST WEIGHTING OF THIS CHAPTER
The 2023 English block was **10 vocabulary + 5 para jumbles + 5 comprehension** (PYQ-DERIVED, Blueprint
§4.4). On that evidence, **grammar was not separately tested at all**.

So why a chapter at all, and why 30 questions?

Because grammar is the **supporting format**. It is the layer underneath the three formats that *were*
tested: a fill-in-the-blank vocabulary item is often decided by which option is grammatically possible in
the slot (F8.1 §2.4), and a para jumble is solved by pronoun reference and connective logic, which is
grammar under another name (F8.2 §3.1). Every mark this chapter protects is a mark in another chapter.

**What follows from that is a decision about depth.** This chapter is written to be **finished quickly**
— dense rule tables and heavy practice, with no teaching of parts of speech from zero. The patterns are
finite, they recur, and once recognised they are recognised for good. Budget **two evenings**, not four,
and do not let it displace the vocabulary drip.

## 1. Session 1 — The Three Formats ★★★☆☆

### 1.1 What you will be shown

| Format | What it looks like | What it is really testing |
|---|---|---|
| **Error spotting** | A sentence in four labelled parts; name the part containing the error | Whether you can locate an error, not merely feel one |
| **Sentence improvement** | One part underlined; choose the best replacement, or *no improvement* | The same rules, in reverse |
| **Fill in the blank** | A gap needing a preposition, a verb form or a connective | Prepositions and tense, mostly |

### 1.2 The method for error spotting

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · CHECK IN THIS ORDER, AND STOP WHEN YOU FIND IT
Do not read the sentence for meaning and wait for something to feel wrong. Feeling is unreliable,
because a wrong sentence you have heard a hundred times feels right. Run the checklist instead — it takes
about twenty seconds:

1. **Find the true subject, and check the verb agrees with it.** Ignore everything between them
2. **Check the tense sequence** — especially if one clause is past and another is not
3. **Check every preposition** against §4
4. **Check every pronoun** for a clear antecedent and correct case
5. **Check the comparative** — *than* with a comparative, *to* with *superior / inferior / senior /
   preferable*
6. **Check for a redundancy** — *return back, discuss about, more better, cousin brother*

**Rule one, since there is nearly always exactly one error:** when you find a definite error, mark it and
move on. Do not audit the remaining parts. That discipline is what brings this format inside its
30–40 second budget (F8.0 §1.1).
:::

### 1.3 The trap that defines the format

The sentences are built so that the error is in the part that reads most naturally. *"The list of members
have been placed before the Commissioner"* is wrong, and it sounds right, because *members* is sitting
next to *have*. Every question in §7.1 is built on this principle: **the noun nearest the verb is
usually not the subject**.

## 2. Session 2 — Subject–Verb Agreement ★★★★★

### 2.1 The twelve rules

| # | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **An intervening phrase does not change the number of the subject** | The **list** of members **has** been placed |
| **2** | *as well as, along with, together with, in addition to, besides, accompanied by* — **the verb follows the first subject only** | The employer, along with his managers, **was** present |
| **3** | *either… or*, *neither… nor*, *not only… but also* — **the verb agrees with the nearer subject** | Neither the employer nor his representatives **were** present |
| **4** | *each, every, everyone, everybody, either, neither, none, no one, anybody, somebody* — **singular** | **Each** of the schemes **has** been framed |
| **5** | **Two subjects joined by *and* take a plural verb** — unless they name one thing | Bread and butter **is** his only meal |
| **6** | **A number of** = plural · **the number of** = **singular** | A number of claims **are** pending · The number of claims **has** fallen |
| **7** | **A collective noun is singular** when it acts as one body, plural when its members act individually | The committee **is** unanimous · The committee **are** divided |
| **8** | **Money, distance, time and weight taken as a single amount are singular** | Ten years **is** a long time · ₹50,000 **is** a heavy penalty |
| **9** | **Plural-looking singulars:** news, mathematics, physics, economics, statistics *(the subject)*, politics, measles, innings, summons | The **news** of the recovery **was** welcomed |
| **10** | **Singular-looking plurals:** police, people, cattle, gentry, vermin, poultry, clergy | The **police have** registered a case |
| **11** | **One of the + plural noun + who / that + plural verb** — the relative refers to the plural noun | He is one of the officers **who are** willing to dissent |
| **12** | ***There is / there are* agrees with what follows it** | There **are** three schemes under the Act |

::: trap | TRAP · THE FOUR THAT ARE MISSED MOST OFTEN
1. **Rule 2 versus Rule 3.** *Along with* leaves the number alone; *neither… nor* moves it to the nearer
   subject. Two phrases that look similar and behave in opposite ways, and the examiner will put them in
   adjacent questions. See Q2 and Q28
2. **Rule 6.** *A number of* and *the number of* differ by one article and take different verbs. Q5 and
   Q22 both use it
3. **Rule 11.** *One of those who* takes a **plural** verb, which sounds wrong to almost everyone. The
   subject of the relative clause is *those / the officers*, not *one*. Q25
4. **Rule 9.** *Statistics* is singular as a subject of study (*statistics is a branch of mathematics*)
   and plural as a set of figures (*the statistics were revised*). Both are correct, in different senses
:::

### 2.2 The mechanical drill

Cover the verb, ask **"who or what?"** once, answer in one word, then restore the verb. *"The list … has."*
*"The number … has."* *"The police … have."*

This works because the checklist step is mechanical and the intuition is not. Practise it on ten
sentences and it becomes automatic.

::: check | CHECK YOURSELF · SESSION 2

3. Give the verb for: *a number of claims*, *the number of claims*
4. Why does *one of the officers who* take a plural verb?
5. Which of *police, news, cattle, mathematics* take plural verbs?
:::

## 3. Session 3 — Tense and Sequence ★★★★☆

### 3.1 The rules that get tested

| Rule | Right | Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **since / for** with a period reaching the present take the **present perfect** | He **has been** in service since 2015 | He **is** in service since 2015 |
| **Past principal clause » past subordinate clause** | He said that he **would** submit the report | He said that he **will** submit |
| **Exception: a universal truth stays in the present** | He said that water **boils** at 100 °C | He said that water **boiled** |
| **The earlier of two past actions takes the past perfect** | The employer **had left** before the inspector **arrived** | The employer **left** before the inspector **had arrived** |
| ***no sooner… than* · *hardly / scarcely… when*** | No sooner **had** the order been passed **than** he appealed | No sooner… **when** · Hardly… **than** |
| **After *if, unless, until, when, before, after, as soon as*, a future is expressed by the present** | If the employer **remits** the arrears, no penalty will follow | If the employer **will remit** |
| ***It is time / it is high time* + past tense** | It is time the employer **paid** the arrears | It is time the employer **pays** |
| ***wish, as if, as though* + *were*** | He speaks as if he **were** the Commissioner | as if he **was** |
| ***would rather* + bare infinitive** | He would rather **resign** than accept a transfer | would rather **to resign** |

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE THREE HIGHEST-FREQUENCY TENSE ERRORS
1. **"Since" with a simple present.** *He is working here since 2015* is the commonest Indian English
   error in the language, and it is wrong in every register. *Since* fixes a starting point, which
   requires a **perfect** tense: **has been working**
2. **The reported-speech shift.** *He said that he will submit* must be *would submit*. A past reporting
   verb pulls the reported verb back one step — unless what is reported is timelessly true
3. ***No sooner… than* and *hardly… when*.** These are fixed pairs and the examiner swaps them. *No
   sooner* takes **than**; *hardly* and *scarcely* take **when**. Both invert: *No sooner **had** the
   order been passed…*
:::

### 3.2 The perfect infinitive

After *hoped, intended, expected, wanted, meant* about something that **did not happen**, use the perfect
infinitive: *I had hoped **to have finished** the inquiry by March*. Rare, but distinctive when it appears.

::: check | CHECK YOURSELF · SESSION 3
1. Correct: *He is in service since 2015*
2. What happens to a reported verb when the reporting verb is past? What is the exception?
3. Complete: *No sooner had the order been passed ______*
4. Give the verb form after *it is time* and after *as if*
:::

## 4. Session 4 — Prepositions and Idiomatic Usage ★★★★★

Nothing here is deducible. It is a memory list, and it is the highest-yield memory list in the English
block after the vocabulary itself.

### 4.1 The pairs that change meaning with the preposition

| Verb | One preposition | The other |
|---|---|---|
| **differ** | **from** a thing | **with** a person (**on** a point) |
| **consist** | **of** its parts | **in** its essence |
| **die** | **of** a disease | **from** a cause · **for** a cause |
| **accompanied** | **by** a person | **with** a thing |
| **confide** | **in** a person (trust) | **to** a person (disclose) |
| **entrust** | a person **with** a task | a task **to** a person |
| **provide** | **for** a contingency | a person **with** a thing |
| **agree** | **to** a proposal | **with** a person |
| **charge** | a person **with** an offence | a sum **to** an account |
| **enter** | a room — **no preposition** | **into** an agreement |
| **responsible** | **to** a person | **for** a matter |
| **liable** | **for** a sum | **to** a penalty |

### 4.2 The fixed prepositions worth memorising

**Take *to*:** superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior, subsequent, preferable, entitled, averse,
addicted, akin, indifferent, consent, accede, yield, object.

**Take *with*:** comply, dispense, part, cope, acquaint, tamper, vie, deal, correspond *(a person)*,
familiar, conversant, entrusted *(a task)*.

**Take *of*:** deprive, accuse, convict, suspect, apprised, cognisant, dispose *(get rid of)*, beware.

**Take *from*:** abstain, refrain, prohibit, exempt, absolve, derive, recover *(an amount)*.

**Take *for*:** eligible, liable *(a sum)*, compensate, account, atone, opt, apologise *(to a person for a
thing)*.

**Take *on / upon*:** insist, depend, rely, impose, embark, congratulate *(on)*, act.

**Take *in*:** invest, succeed, persist, involved, absorbed, interfere *(in a matter)* — but **interfere
with** a thing or a person.

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE VERBS THAT TAKE NO PREPOSITION AT ALL
This is the most reliably examined item in the whole session, because the wrong version is what everyone
says out loud.

**Never followed by a preposition:** **discuss** (not *discuss about*), **describe**, **order**,
**resemble**, **accompany**, **emphasise**, **comprise** (not *comprise of*), **reach** (not *reach at*),
**await** (not *await for*), **lack** (not *lack of*, as a verb), **attack**, **investigate**,
**demand**, **request**, **join**, **sign**, **stress**, **precede**.

And the reverse — **verbs that always need one**: *listen **to**, reply **to**, wait **for**, hope **for**,
dispose **of**, aim **at**, insist **on**, dispense **with**, comply **with***.

**Two that are worth their own line:** *comprise* means *is made up of*, so **"comprises of" says "is
made up of of"**. And **prefer** takes **to**, never *than*: *he prefers the pension **to** the
withdrawal benefit*. *Preferable* likewise takes *to*.
:::

::: check | CHECK YOURSELF · SESSION 4

## 5. Session 5 — Pronouns, Modifiers and Parallelism ★★★★☆

### 5.1 Pronouns

| Rule | Right | Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **After a preposition, the object form** | between you **and me** · for you and **me** | between you and **I** |
| **After *than* or *as*, the subject form** (a verb is understood) | He is senior to me, but I am older **than he** *(is)* | older **than him** *(in formal use)* |
| ***Each, every, everyone, anyone, none* take a singular pronoun** | Everyone must sign **his** own declaration | Everyone must sign **their** own |
| **A reflexive cannot be a subject, and needs an antecedent** | The Commissioner and **I** examined the file | The Commissioner and **myself** examined |
| ***Who* for the subject, *whom* for the object** | the officer **who** signed · the officer **whom** we heard | |
| **A pronoun needs one unmistakable antecedent** | *The employer told the inspector that the employer's records were missing* | *…that **his** records were missing* — whose? |

### 5.2 Misplaced and dangling modifiers

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE PARTICIPLE MUST BELONG TO THE SUBJECT THAT FOLLOWS IT
When a sentence opens with a participial phrase, **the subject of the main clause must be the thing doing
the participle**.

> **Wrong:** *Having examined the registers, the discrepancy was obvious.*
> The discrepancy did not examine anything.
>
> **Right:** *Having examined the registers, the inspector found the discrepancy obvious.*

The repair is always the same: **make the doer the subject.** Q23 is exactly this shape, and the option
that reads most smoothly is the one with no doer at all.

**And the placement of *only*.** It modifies whatever follows it, so it changes the meaning of the
sentence depending on position: *Only the Commissioner can condone* (nobody else) · *The Commissioner can
only condone* (he can do nothing else) · *The Commissioner can condone only the delay* (nothing else can
be condoned). Where an option moves *only*, ask which meaning the sentence needs.
:::

### 5.3 Parallelism

Items joined by *and*, *or*, or a correlative pair must be the **same grammatical kind**.

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| The report was not only late **but also it was incomplete** | not only late **but also incomplete** *(adjective, adjective)* |
| He is responsible for the inquiry **and to submit** a report | for the inquiry **and for submitting** a report |
| She likes **auditing** and **to prepare** accounts | **auditing** and **preparing** |

**Correlatives keep their partners:** *not only… but also* · *either… or* · *neither… nor* · *both… and*
· *not… but* · *no sooner… than*. Mixing partners (*neither… or*, *both… as well as*) is an error.

### 5.4 Two constructions that are always wrong

- ***The reason is because***. *Reason* already means *because*. Write **the reason is that**
- **The double negative.** *Hardly, scarcely, barely, seldom* are already negative, so *hardly no
  evidence* and *cannot hardly* are wrong. Use **hardly any**

## 6. Session 6 — Articles, Degree and Redundancy ★★★★☆

### 6.1 Articles

- ***A* or *an* follows sound, not spelling:** **a** university, **a** one-rupee coin, **a** European —
  **an** hour, **an** honest officer, **an** MP, **an** LLB
- ***The*** before superlatives, unique things, rivers, ranges, oceans, plural country names, and a
  singular noun standing for a whole class: *the Ganga, the Himalayas, the United States, the tiger is
  disappearing*
- **No article** before abstract nouns, material nouns, meals, languages or games in their general sense:
  *honesty pays · gold is costly · he plays cricket · he teaches English*
- **One article for one person, two for two:** *the Secretary and Commissioner* is one officer; *the
  Secretary and **the** Commissioner* is two

### 6.2 Degree of comparison

| Rule | Note |
|---|---|
| **No double comparative or superlative** | *more better*, *most unkindest* are wrong |
| ***Superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior, preferable* take *to*** | never *than* |
| **Absolute adjectives take no degree** | *unique, perfect, complete, extreme, ideal, universal* — no *more unique* |
| ***Comparatively* and *relatively* take the positive degree** | *comparatively easy*, not *comparatively easier* |
| ***Elder / eldest*** for family, attributively | *my elder brother*; but *older than* |
| ***Later / latest*** = time · ***latter / last*** = position | |
| ***Farther*** = distance · ***further*** = additional | |
| ***Fewer*** = number · ***less*** = quantity | **fewer defaults**, **less money** |
| **One of the + superlative + plural noun** | one of the **best officers** |

### 6.3 The standard redundancies

Each of these says the same thing twice, and each appears in error-spotting banks:

**return back · revert back · repeat again · discuss about · comprise of · cousin brother · more
preferable · both the twins · reason is because · sufficient enough · new innovation · final conclusion ·
mutual cooperation · descend down · rise up · join together · free gift · past history · advance
planning**

::: think | THINK LIKE UPSC · WHY THIS SESSION IS SHORT ON PURPOSE
Articles and degree are the parts of English grammar with the longest rule lists and the smallest
examination yield. A full treatment of the article would run to twenty pages and win you, on the 2023
evidence, nothing.

What is in §6 is the residue that actually appears in question banks: the sound rule for *a / an*, the
*to* comparatives, absolute adjectives, *fewer* against *less*, and the redundancy list. **That is the
whole of it, and it is enough.**

Blueprint §10.2's instruction that Module 8 must never become a block applies inside the module too:
this is the point at which grammar stops earning and the vocabulary drip resumes.
:::

## 8. Answers and Explanations

### 8.1 Level 1 — Error spotting

| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|:-:|---|
| 1 | b | **has been placed.** The subject is **the list**, not *members* — §2.1 rule 1. The intervening phrase is there precisely so that a plural noun sits next to the verb |
| 2 | c | **were present.** With *neither… nor* the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject, here *representatives* — rule 3. Contrast Q28, where *along with* leaves the number alone |
| 3 | a | **has been framed.** *Each of* is singular — rule 4 — whatever follows it |
| 4 | d | **to discuss the matter.** *Discuss* takes no preposition (§4.2). The commonest spoken error in the list, which is why it is set |
| 5 | c | **has risen.** *The number of* is **singular**; *a number of* would be plural — rule 6. One article decides the verb |
| 6 | a | **Hardly had the inspection begun when.** *Hardly* and *scarcely* pair with **when**; only *no sooner* takes *than* (§3.1). Compare Q26 |
| 7 | b | **he would submit.** A past reporting verb (*stated*) pulls the reported verb back — §3.2's second point. The report is not a universal truth, so the exception does not apply |
| 8 | d | **to return the registers.** *Return back* is a redundancy (§6.3) — *return* already contains *back* |
| 9 | c | **was produced.** *Every one of* is singular — rule 4. Note that *every one* here is two words, meaning each individually; *everyone* would refer to people |
| 10 | a | **Between you and me.** *Between* is a preposition, so it takes the object form — §5.1. The temptation towards *I* comes from over-correction |

### 8.2 Level 2 — Prepositions and improvement

| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|:-:|---|
| 11 | d | **differ from.** Things differ **from** each other; you differ **with** a person, on a point — §4.1 |
| 12 | b | **entrusted with.** A **person** is entrusted **with** a task; a **task** is entrusted **to** a person. Here the officer is entrusted, so *with*. This pair is worth memorising in both directions |
| 13 | c | **consists in.** *Consist **in*** = has its essence in; *consist **of*** = is made up of. The gravity of an offence is its essence, not its parts |
| 14 | a | **entitled to.** One of the fixed *to* list in §4.2, alongside *superior, inferior, senior, preferable, averse* |
| 15 | b | **comprises.** *Comprise* means *is made up of*, so *comprises of* says *is made up of of*. Note (a) is a real construction — *is comprised in* — but it means *is included in*, which reverses the sense |
| 16 | d | **than accept.** *Would rather* takes the **bare infinitive** on both sides of *than* — §3.1 |
| 17 | c | **paid.** *It is time* and *it is high time* take the **past** form, this being a subjunctive of unreality rather than a past tense |
| 18 | a | **is.** *Neither of* is singular — rule 4. *Of the two* does not make it plural |
| 19 | c | **insisted on.** *Insist* takes **on**; *insist to* and *insist about* do not exist |
| 20 | b | **but also incomplete.** *Not only… but also* must join **like with like**: here adjective with adjective, *late* with *incomplete* — §5.3. Repeating the subject and verb breaks the parallel |

### 8.3 Level 3 — Recruitment Test standard

| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|:-:|---|
| 21 | a | **The police have.** *Police* is a singular-looking plural, with *people, cattle, gentry, clergy, vermin, poultry* — rule 10. All three wrong options use a singular verb |
| 22 | d | **The news… were** is wrong; *news* is a plural-looking **singular** — rule 9. Note that (a), (b) and (c) are all correct, and each illustrates a different rule: *the number of* singular, *a number of* plural, and money as a single amount singular. **The stem asks which is NOT correct** |
| 23 | c | Only (c) supplies a **subject that did the examining** — §5.2. (a), (b) and (d) all make the discrepancy or an impersonal *it* the subject, so the participle dangles. (b) is the smoothest-reading of the wrong options, which is exactly why it is offered |
| 24 | b | **senior to.** *Superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior, preferable* take **to**, never *than* — §6.2. (c) adds a second error: *senior* is already comparative, so *more senior* is a double comparative |
| 25 | a | **who are.** The relative clause describes *the few officers*, not *one* — rule 11. This is the agreement rule that sounds wrong to nearly everyone and is therefore examined |
| 26 | d | **than the employer filed.** *No sooner* takes **than** (compare Q6's *hardly… when*), and the second clause takes the **simple past**, not the past perfect — the past perfect already sits in the first clause |
| 27 | c | In **indirect** speech there is no inversion and no auxiliary fronting: *asked… why he had not remitted*. The reporting verb is past, so the reported verb goes back one step to the past perfect |
| 28 | a | **was present.** *Along with* does **not** change the number of the subject — rule 2, and the deliberate contrast with Q2. The subject is *the employer*, singular |

::: think | THINK LIKE UPSC · THE FIVE SHAPES IN THIS BANK
1. **The hidden subject.** Q1, Q3, Q5, Q9, Q18, Q25, Q28. **Seven of thirty questions** turn on finding
   the true subject through an intervening phrase. Nothing else in this chapter comes close in frequency
2. **The matched pair, swapped.** Q2 against Q28 (*neither… nor* against *along with*); Q6 against Q26
   (*hardly… when* against *no sooner… than*); Q5's *the number of* against Q22's *a number of*. The
   examiner sets both members of a pair and relies on you knowing only one
3. **The redundancy you say out loud.** Q4 and Q8. *Discuss about* and *return back* are so common in
   speech that they read as correct. Learn §6.3 as a list of forbidden phrases, not as a rule
4. **The "not correct" stem.** Q22 and Q30. About **20% of the 2023 paper** used this format (Blueprint
   §6), and here the three *correct* options are the distractors. **Re-read the stem before you mark**
5. **The smooth wrong option.** Q23(b). Where a dangling modifier is the issue, the option that avoids
   naming a doer will always read most easily, because it hides the problem rather than fixing it
:::

## 9. One-Minute Revision

::: check | ONE-MINUTE REVISION · CHAPTER F8.4
**WEIGHT** not separately tested in 2023 · it is the **supporting layer** under vocabulary blanks and para
jumbles · **target 30–40 seconds** · budget two evenings, not four

**CHECKLIST** **1** true subject vs verb **2** tense sequence **3** every preposition **4** every pronoun
**5** the comparative **6** redundancy. **Stop at the first definite error**

**AGREEMENT** intervening phrase changes nothing · *along with / as well as / besides* » **first subject
only** · *either–or, neither–nor* » **nearer subject** · *each, every, either, neither, none* » **singular**
· **a number of = plural, THE number of = singular** · collective singular when acting as one ·
money/time/distance as one amount **singular** · **news, mathematics, statistics, innings, summons =
singular** · **police, people, cattle, gentry, clergy = plural** · **one of those who + PLURAL verb** ·
*there is / are* follows what comes next

**TENSE** *since / for* » **present perfect**, never simple present · past reporting verb » reported verb
back one step, **except a universal truth** · earlier past action » **past perfect** · **no sooner… THAN ·
hardly / scarcely… WHEN** · after *if / until / when / as soon as*, the future is expressed by the present
· *it is time* + **past** · *as if / wish* + **were** · *would rather* + **bare infinitive**

**PREPOSITION PAIRS** differ **from** a thing / **with** a person · consist **of** parts / **in** essence ·
die **of** disease / **from** a cause · accompanied **by** a person / **with** a thing · entrust a person
**with** / a task **to** · provide **for** a contingency / a person **with** · responsible **to** a person /
**for** a matter · liable **for** a sum / **to** a penalty

**TAKE *TO*** superior · inferior · senior · junior · prior · **preferable** · entitled · averse · accede
**TAKE *WITH*** comply · dispense · cope · acquaint · tamper · vie · conversant
**NO PREPOSITION** **discuss · describe · order · resemble · accompany · emphasise · comprise · reach ·
await · attack · investigate · request · sign**
**PREFER TAKES *TO*, NEVER *THAN***

**PRONOUNS** *between you and **me*** · *older than **he** (is)* · everyone » **his** · a reflexive is
never a subject · *who* subject, *whom* object

**MODIFIERS** an opening participle must belong to the **subject that follows** · *only* modifies whatever
comes after it

**PARALLELISM** *not only… but also* joins like with like · keep correlative partners: *neither… nor*,
*both… and*, *either… or* · **the reason is THAT**, not *because* · no double negative after *hardly*

**DEGREE** no double comparative · **absolutes take no degree: unique, perfect, complete, ideal** ·
*comparatively* + positive · **fewer counts, less measures** · elder/older · later/latter · farther/further

**REDUNDANCIES** return back · revert back · repeat again · discuss about · comprise of · cousin brother ·
more preferable · reason is because · sufficient enough · new innovation · descend down · past history
