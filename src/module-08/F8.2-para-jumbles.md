::: note | NOTE · WHY THIS FORMAT IS BETTER NEWS THAN IT LOOKS
Para jumbles were **5 of the 20 English questions** in 2023 (PYQ-DERIVED, Blueprint §4.4) — a quarter of
the English block.

Candidates dislike them, and that dislike is misplaced. Unlike vocabulary, para jumbles do **not** depend
on what you happen to know. They are governed by **mechanical rules of cohesion**: a pronoun must follow
its noun, "however" must follow the thing it contradicts, an abbreviation must follow its full form.
Learn the rules in §2 and §3 and the format becomes closer to logic than to language.

**One practical note on this chapter's practice set.** Every jumble in §6 is built from material in
Modules 3, 6, 7 and 9 — provisions and reserves, ocean acidification, the mesh formula, AS 15. So the
drill is simultaneously English practice and a revision pass. That is deliberate.

## 1. Session 1 — The Format ★★★☆☆

Four to six sentences, labelled **P, Q, R, S** (sometimes 1 to 6), presented out of order. You choose the
sequence that forms a coherent paragraph. Variants ask only for the **opening** or the **concluding**
sentence.

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · SIXTY TO SEVENTY-FIVE SECONDS, AND NEVER BUILD THE WHOLE ORDER
F8.0 §1.1 budgets **60 to 75 seconds** for a jumble. You cannot afford to construct and verify a complete
sequence from scratch.

**So do not.** The method that fits the time budget is:

1. **Find the opener** (§2). This alone often eliminates two options
2. **Find one mandatory pair** — two sentences that must be adjacent, in a known order (§3)
3. **Eliminate every option that does not contain both** the right opener and that pair

Very often **one option survives** after step 3 and you never need to check the rest of the order. That is
the entire technique, and §4.2 works it through.
:::

## 2. Session 2 — Finding the Opener ★★★★★

An opening sentence has to stand on its own, because there is nothing before it to refer back to. That
gives you a **test by elimination**: any sentence that **depends on something earlier cannot be the
opener**.

### 2.1 What disqualifies a sentence from being the opener

| Disqualifier | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Demonstratives** pointing backwards | **this, that, these, those, such, the same** |
| **Pronouns** with no antecedent yet | **it, he, she, they, them, its, their** |
| **Contrast connectives** | **however, but, yet, nevertheless, on the other hand, by contrast** |
| **Consequence connectives** | **therefore, thus, hence, consequently, so, accordingly** |
| **Addition connectives** | **moreover, furthermore, also, in addition, besides** |
| **Exemplification** | **for example, for instance, thus** |
| **Comparatives implying a prior item** | **another, the other, similarly, likewise** |

### 2.2 What marks a sentence as the opener

- It **introduces the subject by its full name**, not by a pronoun
- It often uses the **indefinite article** — *a* or *an* — for a first mention
- It tends to be the most **general** statement; the others add detail
- If a **full form and an abbreviation** both appear in the set, the sentence with the **full form** comes
  first

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE FULL-FORM RULE, AND THE ARTICLE RULE
Two rules that settle a surprising number of sets outright.

**The full-form rule.** "The Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952" must
precede any sentence calling it "the Act" or "it". You introduce a thing before you abbreviate it.

**The article rule.** **"A provision" precedes "the provision".** *A* marks a first mention; *the* marks
something already introduced. If two sentences mention the same noun and one uses *a* while the other
uses *the*, the **indefinite one comes first**.

Both rules work in the same direction and both are mechanical — no comprehension required.
:::

## 3. Session 3 — Chaining the Middle ★★★★★

Once the opener is fixed, the remaining sentences link by the same cohesion devices.

### 3.1 The six chaining rules

| Rule | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Pronoun rule** | A pronoun **follows** its noun. If P names *soaps* and S says *they*, then **P before S** |
| **Demonstrative rule** | *This / that / these* points at the **immediately preceding** idea, which usually makes the pair **adjacent** |
| **Connective rule** | *However* follows what it contradicts · *therefore* follows its cause · *moreover* follows what it adds to · *for example* follows the general statement |
| **Chronology rule** | Dates, and *first / then / next / finally*, run forward |
| **Cause before effect** | The reason precedes the consequence |
| **General before specific** | The broad statement precedes the illustration or detail |

### 3.2 Mandatory pairs — the workhorse of the method

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · WHAT A MANDATORY PAIR IS, AND WHY IT SAVES THE MOST TIME
A **mandatory pair** is two sentences you can be certain are **adjacent and in a fixed order**, usually
because the second contains **this**, **that**, **these** or **therefore** referring directly to the
first.

Why it matters more than finding the opener: **a mandatory pair eliminates options fast**. If you are
certain that **S comes immediately after Q**, then every option in which S does not directly follow Q is
gone — often three of the four.

**The strongest signals of a mandatory pair:**

| Signal in the second sentence | What it points to |
|---|---|
| **"This" + noun** — *this band, this limitation, this makes* | The **immediately** preceding sentence |
| **"These are…"** | A list just announced |
| **"That range", "the former", "the latter"** | Something just named |
| **"therefore", "consequently"** | The cause immediately before |
| **"nevertheless", "however"** | The statement immediately contradicted |

Find one of these and you have converted a four-option question into a one- or two-option question in
about fifteen seconds.
:::

## 4. Session 4 — The Closer, and Elimination ★★★★★

### 4.1 What marks the concluding sentence

- **Conclusion connectives:** *therefore, thus, consequently, hence, as a result, in conclusion, finally*
- A **summary or evaluation** rather than new information
- The **broadest** statement, if the paragraph runs from specific to general
- A statement of **consequence** where the rest of the paragraph explains the cause

Note that *therefore* can mark either a middle sentence or the closer. What settles it is whether
anything else in the set still needs to be said.

### 4.2 The elimination method, worked

> **P.** It was enacted to provide for the institution of provident funds for employees.
> **Q.** The Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act was passed in 1952.
> **R.** Three schemes have been framed under it.
> **S.** These are the Provident Fund Scheme, the Pension Scheme and the Insurance Scheme.
>
> Options: (a) P Q R S · (b) Q P S R · (c) Q P R S · (d) R S Q P

**Step 1 — the opener.** P begins "It was", a pronoun with no antecedent, so P is out. R says "under it",
also dependent. S begins "These are", pointing at something already listed. **Only Q can open** — and it
carries the full name of the Act, per §2.2's full-form rule. That eliminates **(a)** and **(d)**
immediately.

**Step 2 — a mandatory pair.** S says "**These are**" and lists three schemes. R is the sentence that
announces three schemes. So **R must come immediately before S**. That eliminates **(b)**, which has S
before R.

**One option survives: (c) Q P R S.** We never needed to verify where P sits.

::: think | THINK LIKE UPSC · WHY THIS BEATS READING FOR SENSE
You could have solved that set by reading all four sentences and constructing the natural paragraph. It
would have taken two minutes and you would have been right.

The elimination route took about twenty seconds and used **two structural facts**: a pronoun cannot open,
and "these are" must follow its list. **Neither required understanding the provident fund at all.**

Under a 120-questions-in-120-minutes constraint, that difference is the whole game. Read for structure
first; read for sense only if structure leaves two options standing.
:::

## 5. Session 5 — Two More Worked Examples ★★★★☆

**Example 1**

> **P.** Momentum, however, is conserved in every collision.
> **Q.** Collisions are classified as elastic or inelastic.
> **R.** In an inelastic collision kinetic energy is not conserved.
> **S.** In an elastic collision both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved.

**Opener:** P has "however" — disqualified. R and S both begin with "In a…" describing a category that Q
introduces. **Q opens.**
**Chain:** Q announces two categories, so S (elastic) and R (inelastic) follow in the order Q named them.
**Closer:** P's "however" contrasts with R's statement that kinetic energy is not conserved — so P must
follow R.
**Answer: Q S R P.**

**Example 2**

> **P.** This is why the Recruitment Test carries three-quarters of the final weight.
> **Q.** Selection for the post is made in two stages.
> **R.** The marks obtained in it are carried into the final merit list.
> **S.** The first is the Recruitment Test.

**Opener:** P begins "This is why" — disqualified. R says "in it" — dependent. S says "The first" — implies
a set already mentioned. **Q opens.**
**Chain:** S's "The first" points at Q's "two stages", so **Q S** is a mandatory pair. R's "it" refers to
the Recruitment Test named in S, so **S R**.
**Closer:** P concludes with "This is why".
**Answer: Q S R P.**

3. What is a mandatory pair, and why does it save more time than finding the opener?

## 6. Graded Practice — 20 Questions ★★★★★

**Target: 60 to 75 seconds each.** Use the elimination method — opener first, then one mandatory pair.

### 6.1 Level 1 — Four-sentence sets

**Q1.**
P. It was enacted to provide for the institution of provident funds for employees.
Q. The Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act was passed in 1952.
R. Three schemes have been framed under it.
S. These are the Provident Fund Scheme, the Pension Scheme and the Insurance Scheme.
(a) P Q R S (b) Q P S R (c) Q P R S (d) R S Q P

**Q2.**
P. The auditor therefore expresses an opinion rather than issuing a certificate.
Q. An audit is an independent examination of financial information.
R. Its purpose is to determine whether the statements give a true and fair view.
S. Absolute accuracy cannot be guaranteed, because audit evidence is persuasive rather than conclusive.
(a) Q R S P (b) Q S R P (c) R Q S P (d) P Q R S

**Q3.**
P. This makes them unsuitable for use in hard water.
Q. Soaps are the sodium or potassium salts of long-chain fatty acids.
R. Detergents were developed to overcome this limitation.
S. They form an insoluble scum with calcium and magnesium ions.
(a) Q P S R (b) S Q P R (c) Q R S P (d) Q S P R

**Q4.**
P. Momentum, however, is conserved in every collision.
Q. Collisions are classified as elastic or inelastic.
R. In an inelastic collision kinetic energy is not conserved.
S. In an elastic collision both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved.
(a) Q R S P (b) Q S R P (c) S R Q P (d) P Q S R

**Q5.**
P. A provision is therefore created even when the year results in a loss.
Q. A provision is a charge against profit.
R. A reserve, by contrast, is an appropriation of profit.
S. It can be created only if there is a profit.
(a) Q P R S (b) Q R P S (c) R S Q P (d) Q R S P

**Q6.**
P. The pH of seawater has fallen from about 8.2 to about 8.1.
Q. The ocean absorbs a large share of the carbon dioxide emitted by human activity.
R. Seawater nevertheless remains alkaline.
S. Dissolved carbon dioxide forms carbonic acid, releasing hydrogen ions.
(a) Q P S R (b) S Q P R (c) Q S R P (d) Q S P R

**Q7.**
P. Each of these is exactly three binary digits.
Q. The octal system uses the digits 0 to 7.
R. Conversion between binary and octal therefore requires no arithmetic.
S. Grouping the bits in threes from the right is sufficient.
(a) Q R P S (b) Q P R S (c) P Q R S (d) Q S P R

**Q8.**
P. He must issue notice, hear the employer and pass a reasoned order.
Q. An Assistant Provident Fund Commissioner conducts inquiries under Section 7A.
R. That function is therefore described as quasi-judicial.
S. The order so passed is appealable.
(a) Q R P S (b) P Q S R (c) Q P S R (d) Q S P R

**Q9.**
P. The trial balance will nevertheless agree.
Q. An error of principle arises when a capital item is treated as revenue.
R. Both the profit and the assets are understated.
S. The entry is balanced, though wrongly classified.
(a) Q R S P (b) Q S R P (c) R Q S P (d) Q P S R

**Q10.**
P. Its agreement therefore proves only arithmetical accuracy.
Q. A trial balance lists the debit and credit balances of all ledger accounts.
R. An entry may be perfectly balanced and yet posted to entirely the wrong account.
S. It is prepared to test whether total debits equal total credits.
(a) Q R S P (b) S Q R P (c) Q S P R (d) Q S R P

**Q11.**
P. This band deliberately straddles neutral.
Q. Industrial effluent discharged into inland surface water must fall within a permissible pH range.
R. Requiring exact neutrality would be unattainable in practice.

(a) Q P S R (b) Q S P R (c) S Q P R (d) Q R S P

**Q12.**
P. Each node must be connected to every other node.
Q. A mesh topology is the most reliable of the network topologies.

**Q13.**

**Q14.**
P. It must respond within a strict and guaranteed deadline.

## 7. Answers and Explanations

### 7.1 Level 1

| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|:-:|---|
| 1 | c | **Q P R S.** Q alone can open — P says "It was", R says "under it", S says "These are". Then **R S** is a mandatory pair: S's "These are" lists the three schemes R announces |
| 2 | a | **Q R S P.** Q defines; R's "Its purpose" refers to the audit; S gives the reason; P's "therefore" concludes. Two structural facts settle it — "Its" cannot open, and "therefore" cannot come before its cause |
| 3 | d | **Q S P R.** Q names soaps; S's "They" refers to them; P's "This" refers to the scum in S; R's "this limitation" refers to P. A chain of three back-references, each fixing one position |
| 4 | b | **Q S R P.** Q announces the two categories; S and R take them in the order named; P's "however" contradicts R. "However" almost never opens |
| 5 | a | **Q P R S.** Q states the charge; P's "therefore" follows from it; R's "by contrast" introduces the reserve; S's "It" refers to the reserve, so S must follow R |
| 6 | d | **Q S P R.** Q, then S's mechanism, then P's measured consequence, then R's "nevertheless" — the qualification that seawater is still alkaline must come last, because it corrects the impression P leaves |
| 7 | b | **Q P R S.** Q names the digits; P's "Each of these" refers to them; R's "therefore" draws the conclusion; S explains how |

### 7.2 Level 2

| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|:-:|---|
| 8 | c | **Q P S R.** Q introduces the officer; P describes what he must do; S adds that the order is appealable; R's "That function is therefore" concludes from all of it |
| 9 | a | **Q R S P.** Q defines the error; R gives the effect; S explains why the entry balances; P's "nevertheless" states the consequence for the trial balance, which only makes sense after S |
| 10 | d | **Q S R P.** Q defines; S's "It is prepared" continues about the trial balance; R gives the counter-instance; P's "therefore" concludes. Note R must precede P, because P's limitation follows from R's possibility |
| 11 | b | **Q S P R.** Q introduces the range; S's "That range" is a mandatory pair with Q; P's "This band" refers to S; R explains why the band is wide |
| 12 | c | **Q P R S.** Q introduces mesh; P states the requirement; R's "therefore" gives the formula; S's "otherwise" explains the division by two, which only makes sense once the formula is on the table |
| 13 | d | **R P Q S.** R opens — the only sentence with no back-reference. P's "by contrast" must follow R; Q's "Their" refers to the detergents in P; S's "therefore" concludes |
| 14 | a | **Q P R S.** Q makes the negative claim; P states what the definition actually is; R restates it as a contrast; S illustrates. **General before specific** puts the example last |

### 7.3 Level 3

| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|:-:|---|
| 15 | c | **Q.** P begins "This is why", R says "in it", S says "The first stage" — all three depend on something earlier. Only Q stands alone |
| 16 | b | **P.** "This is why" summarises the consequence of everything preceding, and no sentence in the set follows from P. The full order is Q S R P |
| 17 | d | **Q R P S.** Q sets up the puzzle of two similar phrases; R defines the first; P's "on the other hand" defines the second; S comments on the pair. S cannot precede the definitions it comments on |
| 18 | a | **Q P S R.** Q introduces the energy; P's "it" refers to that energy; S accounts for "the remainder", which only exists once P has named a tenth; R's "consequently" concludes |
| 19 | c | **Q S R P.** Q states the engineering principle; S elaborates it; R draws the analogy to accounting; P's "Its" refers to the trial balance in R. Note that R must precede P, because P's "Its" has no referent until R names the trial balance |
| 20 | b | **Q S P R.** Q announces the two kinds; S's "the former" points back to the first, so **Q S** is mandatory; P explains the consequence for that kind; R turns to the second kind |

::: think | THINK LIKE UPSC · THE THREE SHAPES IN THIS BANK
1. **The back-reference chain.** Q3, Q11 and Q18 each contain three sentences that point backwards
   (*they, this, that range, the remainder*). Chains like this are the **easiest** jumbles, because each
   reference fixes one position absolutely. Look for them first
2. **The "former / latter" pair.** Q20. *The former* and *the latter* are the strongest mandatory-pair
   signals in the language — they cannot appear before the pair they refer to
3. **The qualification that must come last.** Q6 and Q9. Where one sentence **corrects a misimpression**
   left by another (*nevertheless remains alkaline*, *will nevertheless agree*), it must follow the
   sentence creating that impression — which usually puts it at the end
:::
