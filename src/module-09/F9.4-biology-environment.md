## 1. Session 1 — The Cell ★★★★☆

The **cell** is the structural and functional unit of life. It was discovered by **Robert Hooke** in
1665, observing cork.

**Prokaryotic** cells have no true nucleus and no membrane-bound organelles — bacteria.
**Eukaryotic** cells have both — plants, animals, fungi.

### 1.1 The organelles and their functions

| Organelle | Function | Nickname |
|---|---|---|
| **Nucleus** | Controls the cell; contains DNA | — |
| **Mitochondrion** | Releases energy as ATP | **Powerhouse of the cell** |
| **Ribosome** | **Protein synthesis** | — |
| **Chloroplast** | **Photosynthesis** — plants only | — |
| **Lysosome** | Contains digestive enzymes | **Suicidal bag** |
| **Golgi apparatus** | Packaging and secretion | — |
| Endoplasmic reticulum | Transport within the cell | — |
| **Vacuole** | Storage; **large and central in plant cells** | — |

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · PLANT VERSUS ANIMAL CELL, AND THE TWO DIVISIONS
**A plant cell HAS a cell wall of cellulose, chloroplasts, and a large central vacuole. An animal cell
has none of these** but does have a centrosome.

The reliable wrong option reverses the cell wall — "a plant cell lacks a cell wall" is false, and it is
offered because the *animal* cell is the one that lacks it.

| | **Mitosis** | **Meiosis** |
|---|---|---|
| Occurs in | Somatic (body) cells | **Reproductive** cells |
| Daughter cells | **Two**, genetically **identical** | **Four** |
| Chromosome number | **Unchanged** | **Halved** — reduction division |
| Purpose | Growth and repair | Formation of **gametes** |

A normal human somatic cell has **46 chromosomes — 23 pairs**. Sex chromosomes: **XX female, XY male**.
DNA's double-helix structure was described by **Watson and Crick**.
:::

## 2. Session 2 — Human Body Systems ★★★★★

### 2.1 Digestion and circulation

| Organ | Function |
|---|---|
| Mouth | **Salivary amylase** begins starch digestion |
| Stomach | **HCl** and **pepsin** — protein digestion |
| **Small intestine** | **Main site of absorption** |
| Large intestine | Absorption of **water** |
| **Liver** | The **largest gland**; secretes **bile** |
| **Pancreas** | Both exocrine (enzymes) and endocrine (**insulin**) |

**Circulation.** The heart has **four chambers** — two atria and two ventricles.

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · ARTERIES, VEINS AND THE TWO EXCEPTIONS
**Arteries carry blood AWAY from the heart; veins carry blood TO the heart.** That is the definition —
direction, not oxygen content.

Oxygen content follows as a general rule with **two exceptions**:

- The **pulmonary artery** carries **deoxygenated** blood, from the heart to the lungs
- The **pulmonary vein** carries **oxygenated** blood, from the lungs to the heart

So a question asking which artery carries deoxygenated blood is answered by the **pulmonary artery**, and
a statement that "all arteries carry oxygenated blood" is wrong.

**Blood:** **RBC** contain **haemoglobin** and live about 120 days; **WBC** provide immunity;
**platelets** enable clotting. **O negative is the universal donor; AB positive the universal
recipient.**
:::

### 2.2 Excretion, nerves and hormones

- **Kidney** — the functional unit is the **nephron**; the nitrogenous waste is **urea**
- **Brain** — the **cerebrum** is the largest part and governs thinking; the **cerebellum** governs
  **balance and coordination**; the **medulla oblongata** controls involuntary functions such as
  heartbeat and breathing. **Reflex action** is mediated by the **spinal cord**, not the brain

| Gland | Hormone | Deficiency or excess |
|---|---|---|
| **Pituitary** | Growth hormone | The **master gland** |
| **Thyroid** | **Thyroxine** — requires **iodine** | Iodine deficiency causes **goitre** |
| **Pancreas** | **Insulin** | Deficiency causes **diabetes mellitus** |
| **Adrenal** | **Adrenaline** | The **emergency hormone** |

An adult human has **206 bones**. The **femur** is the largest; the **stapes**, in the ear, the smallest.

### 2.3 The ear

Outer, middle and inner. The middle ear contains **three bones — malleus, incus and stapes**. In the
inner ear the **cochlea** serves **hearing** and the **semicircular canals** serve **balance**.

### 2.4 The eye — the 2023 item

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE THREE LAYERS OF THE EYEBALL, OUTSIDE INWARDS
This is a 2023 question. The wall of the eyeball has **three coats**, and the order matters:

| Order | Layer | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — outermost** | **Sclera** | Tough, white, protective. Its transparent front part is the **cornea** |
| **2 — middle** | **Choroid** | **Vascular** and pigmented; supplies blood and prevents internal reflection |
| **3 — innermost** | **Retina** | **Light-sensitive**, carrying the **rods and cones** |

Mnemonic for the order: **S-C-R**, outside in — **S**clera, **C**horoid, **R**etina. Alphabetical, which
makes it unusually easy.

The trap is any option placing the **choroid innermost** or the **retina outermost**.

**Inside the retina:** **rods** give vision in **dim light**, in black and white; **cones** give
**colour** vision in bright light. The **blind spot** is where the optic nerve leaves and has neither.

**Other parts:** the **iris** controls the size of the **pupil** and hence the light admitted; the
**lens** focuses; the aqueous and vitreous humours fill the chambers. Defects of vision and their
corrections are in **F9.2 §1.5**.
:::

## 3. Session 3 — Nutrition and Disease ★★★★☆

### 3.1 Vitamins and deficiency diseases

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · FAT-SOLUBLE VERSUS WATER-SOLUBLE, AND THE DEFICIENCY LIST
**Fat-soluble vitamins: A, D, E and K.** Everything else — the **B group and C** — is **water-soluble**.
That four-letter list is asked directly.

| Vitamin | Chemical name | Deficiency disease |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Retinol | **Night blindness**, xerophthalmia |
| **B1** | Thiamine | **Beri-beri** |
| **B3** | Niacin | **Pellagra** |
| **B12** | Cobalamin | Pernicious anaemia |
| **C** | Ascorbic acid | **Scurvy** |
| **D** | Calciferol | **Rickets** in children, osteomalacia in adults |
| **E** | Tocopherol | Sterility |
| **K** | — | Defective **blood clotting** |

**Mineral deficiencies:** **iron » anaemia** · **iodine » goitre** · calcium and vitamin D » rickets.
**Protein deficiency** causes **kwashiorkor** and **marasmus**.
:::

### 3.2 Diseases by causative organism

| Cause | Diseases |
|---|---|
| **Bacteria** | **Tuberculosis**, cholera, typhoid, tetanus, leprosy, plague, diphtheria |
| **Virus** | Influenza, **AIDS**, polio, measles, mumps, rabies, **dengue**, chikungunya, hepatitis |
| **Protozoa** | **Malaria** (*Plasmodium*), amoebic dysentery, kala-azar |
| **Fungus** | Ringworm, athlete's foot |

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE MOSQUITO VECTORS
| Mosquito | Transmits |
|---|---|
| **Anopheles** | **Malaria** |
| **Aedes aegypti** | **Dengue**, chikungunya, yellow fever, Zika |
| **Culex** | **Filariasis** |

And the classification trap: **malaria is protozoan, not bacterial or viral**, while **dengue is
viral, not protozoan**. Both are mosquito-borne, which is exactly why the causative organisms get
swapped.
:::

## 4. Session 4 — Plants ★★★★★

### 4.1 Photosynthesis and transport

**Photosynthesis** occurs in the **chloroplast** using **chlorophyll** and sunlight: carbon dioxide and
water become glucose and oxygen. It is **endothermic** — it absorbs energy, which is F9.3 §5.1.

**Transport:** **xylem** carries **water and minerals upwards**; **phloem** carries **food**, and it
moves in **both directions**.

### 4.2 Modified roots — the 2023 item

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE FOUR MODIFIED ROOTS, WITH THEIR PLANTS
This is a 2023 question, and the four are constantly interchanged.

| Modification | Purpose | Plant |
|---|---|---|
| **Prop roots** | Aerial roots growing **downwards from branches** to support heavy spreading branches | **BANYAN** |
| **Stilt roots** | Growing **obliquely from the lower stem** into the soil for support | **Maize, sugarcane, screwpine** |
| **Pneumatophores** (respiratory roots) | Growing **upwards out of waterlogged soil** for gas exchange | **MANGROVES** |
| **Storage roots** | Storing food | **Carrot**, sweet potato, radish |

The distinction that decides the question is **direction**:

- **Prop roots grow DOWN, from the branches** — banyan
- **Stilt roots grow DOWN and outward, from the lower stem** — sugarcane
- **Pneumatophores grow UP, out of the mud** — mangrove

So "aerial roots hanging from the branches of a large tree" is unambiguously the **banyan**, and
"roots growing vertically upward from waterlogged soil" is the **mangrove**.
:::

**Modified stems**, for contrast, since they are offered as distractors: **potato** is a *tuber*,
**ginger** a *rhizome*, **onion** a *bulb*.

**Nitrogen fixation:** leguminous plants carry **root nodules** containing ***Rhizobium***.

### 4.3 Plant hormones and tropisms

| Hormone | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Auxin** | Cell elongation; responsible for **phototropism** |
| Gibberellin | Stem elongation |
| Cytokinin | Cell division |
| **Abscisic acid** | Stress response, closing stomata; growth **inhibitor** |
| **Ethylene** | **Ripening** of fruit |

**Tropisms:** photo (light), geo (gravity), hydro (water), thigmo (touch), chemo (chemicals).

## 5. Session 5 — Ecology and Environment ★★★★★

### 5.1 Ecosystem structure

An **ecosystem** has **biotic** components — **producers**, **consumers** and **decomposers** — and
**abiotic** components.

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE TEN PER CENT LAW AND UNIDIRECTIONAL FLOW
**Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level passes to the next** — Lindeman's ten per cent law.
The rest is lost as heat and in metabolism.

Two consequences that are examined:

1. **Energy flow through an ecosystem is UNIDIRECTIONAL**, not cyclic. Nutrients cycle; **energy does
   not**. An option saying energy flow is bidirectional or cyclic is wrong
2. Food chains are therefore **short** — usually four or five links — because so little energy survives

**Biomagnification** is the increasing concentration of **non-biodegradable** substances such as DDT and
mercury at successively higher trophic levels. The word to check in the option is
**non-biodegradable** — biodegradable substances do not biomagnify.
:::

### 5.2 Pollution

| Type | Key indicators and facts |
|---|---|
| **Air** | PM2.5 and PM10, sulphur dioxide, oxides of nitrogen, carbon monoxide |
| **Water** | **BOD** — biochemical oxygen demand. **Higher BOD means more organic pollution** |
| **Eutrophication** | Nutrient enrichment by **nitrates and phosphates** » **algal bloom** » oxygen depletion » fish die |

Note the link back to F9.3 §4.1: **phosphates from detergents** are a classic contributor to
eutrophication, which is part of why detergent biodegradability matters.

**Acid rain** comes from oxides of **sulphur and nitrogen** forming sulphuric and nitric acids.

### 5.3 Ocean acidification — the 2023 item

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · WHAT ACIDIFICATION MEANS, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
The ocean absorbs a large share of the carbon dioxide humans emit. Dissolved **carbon dioxide forms
carbonic acid**, which releases hydrogen ions and **lowers the pH of seawater**.

**Consequence:** less carbonate is available for shell and skeleton formation, so the organisms harmed
are the **calcifying** ones — **corals**, molluscs and shelled plankton. Coral bleaching and reef
weakening follow.

**And now the point the exam is most likely to test.** Ocean pH has fallen from roughly **8.2 to about
8.1** since pre-industrial times. So:

**Seawater is still ALKALINE. "Acidification" means moving TOWARDS acidic, not having become acidic.**

Any statement that the ocean has become acidic, or that its pH is below 7, is **wrong** — and it is the
intuitive reading of the word. The cause is **carbon dioxide**, not sulphur dioxide, which is the agent
of acid *rain*.
:::

### 5.4 Climate, ozone and the conventions

**Greenhouse gases:** carbon dioxide, **methane**, nitrous oxide, water vapour, CFCs. Methane has a
higher global warming potential per molecule than carbon dioxide.

**Ozone.** Stratospheric ozone absorbs **ultraviolet** radiation and is beneficial; tropospheric ozone is
a **pollutant**. Depletion is caused by **CFCs**, and the ozone hole appears over **Antarctica**.

| Agreement | Year | Subject |
|---|---|---|
| **Ramsar** Convention | **1971** | **Wetlands** |
| **CITES** | 1973 | Trade in endangered species |
| **Montreal** Protocol | **1987** | **Ozone-depleting substances** |
| **UNFCCC** and **CBD** | 1992 | Climate change; biological diversity |
| **Kyoto** Protocol | 1997 | Emission reduction commitments |
| **Paris** Agreement | **2015** | Climate change |

::: trap | COMMON UPSC TRAP · MONTREAL VERSUS KYOTO VERSUS PARIS
Three protocols, three different subjects, routinely swapped:

- **Montreal 1987 » OZONE**
- **Kyoto 1997 » climate, binding emission targets**
- **Paris 2015 » climate, nationally determined contributions**

**Montreal is the ozone one.** If the stem mentions the ozone layer or CFCs and the options include
Kyoto or Paris, they are there to catch you. And **Ramsar is wetlands**, not forests.
:::

**India:** **Project Tiger** began in **1973**, **Project Elephant** in 1992. Protected areas comprise
national parks, wildlife sanctuaries and biosphere reserves.
