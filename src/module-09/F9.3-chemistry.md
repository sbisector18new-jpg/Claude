## 1. Session 1 — Atomic Structure and the Mole ★★★☆☆

| Particle | Charge | Location |
|---|---|---|
| **Proton** | Positive | Nucleus |
| **Neutron** | Neutral | Nucleus |
| **Electron** | Negative | Shells around the nucleus |

- **Atomic number (Z) = number of protons.** It defines the element
- **Mass number (A) = protons + neutrons**
- **Valency** — the combining capacity, determined by the outermost shell and the **octet rule**

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · ISOTOPES, ISOBARS AND THE MOLE
| Term | Same | Different |
|---|---|---|
| **Isotopes** | **Atomic number** (protons) | **Mass number** (neutrons) |
| **Isobars** | **Mass number** | Atomic number |
| **Isotones** | Number of neutrons | Both Z and A |

Isotopes have the **same chemical** properties (same electron arrangement) but different **physical**
properties. Examples: protium, deuterium and tritium; carbon-12 and carbon-14; uranium-235 and
uranium-238.

**One mole contains 6.022 × 10²³ particles — Avogadro's number.** That figure is asked directly, and its
usual distractors are the speed of light (3 × 10⁸) and the electronic charge (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹).
:::

## 2. Session 2 — Periodic Table and Bonding ★★★☆☆

### 2.1 The periodic table

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE LAW, AND THE GROUP NAMES
**The modern periodic law: the properties of elements are a periodic function of their ATOMIC NUMBER.**
Mendeleev's earlier law used **atomic mass** — that swap is the standard question.

There are **18 groups and 7 periods**. The group names that get asked:

| Group | Name |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Alkali metals** |
| **2** | **Alkaline earth metals** |
| **17** | **Halogens** |
| **18** | **Noble or inert gases** |

The commonest error is calling group 18 the halogens. **17 halogens, 18 noble gases.**

**Trends.** Across a period, left to right: atomic size **decreases**, electronegativity **increases**,
metallic character **decreases**. Down a group: atomic size **increases**, metallic character
**increases**, electronegativity **decreases**.
:::

### 2.2 Bonding

| Bond | Mechanism | Properties | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ionic** (electrovalent) | **Transfer** of electrons | **High** melting point; conducts when molten or in solution | NaCl |
| **Covalent** | **Sharing** of electrons | Low melting point; generally poor conductor | H₂O, CH₄ |
| **Metallic** | Delocalised electrons | Conducts, malleable, ductile | Iron, copper |
| **Hydrogen bond** | Weak attraction involving H and an electronegative atom | Raises boiling point | Why **ice floats** and water boils high |

## 3. Session 3 — Acids, Bases, Salts and pH ★★★★★

**A 2023 topic.** Read this section carefully.

### 3.1 Acids and bases

| | **Acid** | **Base** |
|---|---|---|
| Taste | Sour | Bitter, and feels soapy |
| Litmus | Turns **blue litmus red** | Turns **red litmus blue** |
| In water | Releases **H⁺** ions | Releases **OH⁻** ions |
| Examples | HCl, H₂SO₄, citric, acetic | NaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)₂ |

**Neutralisation:** acid + base » **salt + water**, and it is **exothermic**.

**Indicators:** litmus; methyl orange; **phenolphthalein — colourless in acid, PINK in base**; universal
indicator for the whole range.

### 3.2 The pH scale

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE SCALE, AND THE FACTOR OF TEN
The **pH scale runs from 0 to 14**. pH = −log[H⁺].

- **pH 7 — neutral** (pure water at 25 °C)
- **Below 7 — acidic.** The lower the number, the stronger the acid
- **Above 7 — basic or alkaline**

**Each unit of pH is a factor of TEN in hydrogen ion concentration.** So a solution of pH 4 has
**100 times** the hydrogen ion concentration of one at pH 6 — two units, so 10², not two times. That
multiplication is a favourite numerical.

Values worth carrying:

| Substance | pH |
|---|---|
| Gastric juice | about 1.5 to 3.5 |
| Pure water | **7** |
| **Human blood** | about **7.4** — slightly **alkaline**, and tightly regulated |
| Milk | about 6.6 |
:::

### 3.3 Effluents and pH — the 2023 item

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE DISCHARGE STANDARD
Under the general standards for the **discharge of industrial effluents into inland surface water**, the
permissible **pH range is about 5.5 to 9.0**.

The logic is worth holding rather than the number alone: discharge is permitted across a **band either
side of neutral**, not at exactly 7, because requiring precise neutrality would be unattainable — but
strongly acidic or strongly alkaline effluent destroys aquatic life and corrodes infrastructure.

So the wrong options are "exactly 7.0", and any band lying wholly on one side of neutral such as 1 to 5
or 9 to 12. The correct answer **straddles 7**.
:::

Related environmental chemistry, which connects to F9.4: **acid rain** is caused by oxides of sulphur and
nitrogen forming sulphuric and nitric acids; **ocean acidification** is caused by dissolved carbon
dioxide forming carbonic acid.

### 3.4 Salts and water hardness

| Common name | Chemical name |
|---|---|
| Common salt | Sodium chloride, NaCl |
| **Baking soda** | **Sodium hydrogen carbonate**, NaHCO₃ |
| Washing soda | Sodium carbonate decahydrate |
| Bleaching powder | Calcium oxychloride |
| **Quicklime** | Calcium oxide, CaO |
| **Slaked lime** | Calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂ |
| Plaster of Paris | Calcium sulphate hemihydrate |
| Caustic soda | Sodium hydroxide, NaOH |

**Hardness of water:**

| Type | Caused by | Removed by |
|---|---|---|
| **Temporary** | **Bicarbonates** of calcium and magnesium | **Boiling** |
| **Permanent** | **Sulphates and chlorides** of calcium and magnesium | Washing soda, ion exchange |

## 4. Session 4 — Soaps, Detergents and Everyday Substances ★★★★★

**A 2023 topic.**

### 4.1 Soaps and detergents

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE DEFINITIONS, AND THE HARD-WATER DIFFERENCE
**Soap is the sodium or potassium salt of a long-chain fatty acid.** It is made by **saponification** —
the hydrolysis of fats or oils with an alkali such as sodium hydroxide.

**A detergent is the sodium salt of a long-chain benzene sulphonic acid or sulphate.**

Now the difference that generates the question:

| | **Soap** | **Detergent** |
|---|---|---|
| Works in **hard water**? | **No** — forms an insoluble **scum** | **Yes** |
| Why | Its calcium and magnesium salts are **insoluble** | Its calcium and magnesium salts are **soluble** |
| Biodegradability | **Biodegradable** | Often **less** biodegradable — branched chains cause foaming in rivers |

Note the direction carefully, because reversing it is the standard wrong option: **it is SOAP that fails
in hard water**, not the detergent. Detergents were developed precisely to solve that problem — and the
environmental cost was reduced biodegradability.
:::

**How soap cleans — the micelle.** A soap molecule has a **hydrophilic (water-loving) ionic head** and a
**hydrophobic (water-repelling) hydrocarbon tail**. In water the molecules cluster into a **micelle**
with the tails pointing inwards, trapping oil and grease in the centre while the heads face the water —
so the dirt is carried away in suspension.

### 4.2 Everyday substances worth recognising

| Name | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Dry ice** | **Solid carbon dioxide** |
| **Laughing gas** | **Nitrous oxide**, N₂O |
| **Marsh gas** | **Methane**, CH₄ |
| Vinegar | Dilute **ethanoic (acetic) acid**, about 5 to 8% |
| **Aqua regia** | A mixture of **concentrated HCl and concentrated HNO₃**, which dissolves gold |
| Producer gas, water gas | Fuel gas mixtures |
| Urea | A **nitrogenous** fertiliser |

## 5. Session 5 — Enthalpy and Types of Reaction ★★★★★

**A 2023 topic.**

### 5.1 Exothermic and endothermic

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE SIGN OF ΔH, AND THE TWO LISTS
| | **Exothermic** | **Endothermic** |
|---|---|---|
| Heat | **Released** | **Absorbed** |
| **ΔH** | **Negative** | **Positive** |
| Surroundings | Warm up | Cool down |

**The sign is the trap.** Heat coming *out* of the system makes the enthalpy change **negative**, which
feels backwards to beginners — you are measuring the system's enthalpy, not the room's. An option saying
an endothermic reaction has a negative ΔH is wrong.

**Exothermic:** combustion of fuels · **respiration** · **neutralisation** of an acid by a base ·
quicklime added to water · rusting (slow).

**Endothermic:** **photosynthesis** · **thermal decomposition**, such as calcium carbonate to calcium
oxide and carbon dioxide · dissolving ammonium chloride in water · electrolysis.

The clean pair to remember: **respiration is exothermic, photosynthesis is endothermic.** They are the
reverse of one another, which is why they carry opposite signs.
:::

### 5.2 Types of reaction

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Combination** | Two or more reactants form one product |
| **Decomposition** | One reactant splits — thermal, electrolytic or photochemical |
| **Displacement** | A more reactive element displaces a less reactive one |
| **Double displacement** | Ions are exchanged; includes precipitation and neutralisation |
| **Redox** | Oxidation and reduction occur together |

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · OIL RIG
**Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain — of ELECTRONS.**

| | Oxidation | Reduction |
|---|---|---|
| Electrons | **Lost** | **Gained** |
| Oxygen | Gained | Lost |
| Hydrogen | Lost | Gained |

The modern definition is in terms of **electrons**, which is what "best defined as" questions are testing.
The oxygen definition is older and narrower.

**Corrosion:** rusting of iron requires **both oxygen AND moisture** — either alone will not do, which is
why an option naming only one is wrong. Prevented by painting, oiling, **galvanisation** (zinc coating)
and alloying.

**Rancidity:** oxidation of fats and oils, retarded by **antioxidants** and by flushing packaging with
**nitrogen**.
:::

::: check | CHECK YOURSELF · SESSION 5

3. Which of respiration and photosynthesis is endothermic?
4. Define oxidation in terms of electrons. What does OIL RIG stand for?

:::

## 6. Session 6 — Metals, Non-metals and Carbon ★★★★☆

### 6.1 Metals and non-metals

| | **Metals** | **Non-metals** |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | Lustrous | Dull, brittle |
| Conductivity | **Good** | **Poor** — except **graphite** |
| Oxides formed | **Basic** | **Acidic** |
| Exceptions | **Mercury** is liquid; sodium and potassium are soft | **Bromine** is liquid; **graphite** conducts; **diamond** is hardest |

**Reactivity series**, most reactive first:

> **K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > (H) > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au**

**Alloys** worth knowing:

| Alloy | Composition |
|---|---|
| **Brass** | Copper + **zinc** |
| **Bronze** | Copper + **tin** |
| Steel | Iron + carbon |
| **Stainless steel** | Iron + **chromium + nickel** |
| **Solder** | Lead + tin |
| Duralumin | Aluminium + copper + magnesium + manganese |
| Amalgam | Any alloy containing **mercury** |

### 6.2 Carbon

**Carbon shows tetravalency and catenation** — it forms four bonds and long chains, which is why organic
chemistry exists.

| Allotrope | Property |
|---|---|
| **Diamond** | **Hardest** natural substance; does **not** conduct electricity |
| **Graphite** | **Conducts** electricity; soft; used as a lubricant and in pencils |
| **Fullerene** | Cage-like molecules, such as C-60 |

**Hydrocarbons:** alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (saturated) · alkenes CₙH₂ₙ · alkynes CₙH₂ₙ₋₂.

**Functional groups:** alcohol −OH · aldehyde −CHO · carboxylic acid −COOH.

::: trap | COMMON UPSC TRAP · DIAMOND AND GRAPHITE
Both are pure carbon, and the exam relies on you knowing which property belongs to which.

**Graphite conducts electricity; diamond does not.** Graphite has delocalised electrons between its
layers; diamond's electrons are all locked into covalent bonds. And **diamond is the hardest** natural
substance while graphite is soft enough to write with.

Same element, opposite properties — because the difference is structure, not composition.
:::

**EFFLUENT DISCHARGE pH 5.5 TO 9.0** — a band **straddling** neutral · acid rain from oxides of sulphur
and nitrogen · ocean acidification from dissolved **CO₂**

**HARDNESS** temporary = **bicarbonates**, removed by **boiling** · permanent = **sulphates and
chlorides**, removed by washing soda or ion exchange

**SOAP** = sodium or potassium salt of a **long-chain fatty acid** · made by **SAPONIFICATION** ·
**FAILS in hard water**, forms insoluble **scum** · **biodegradable**
**DETERGENT** = sodium salt of a long-chain **benzene sulphonic acid** · **works in hard water** ·
**less biodegradable**, causes foaming
**MICELLE** hydrophilic **head** out, hydrophobic **tail** in, trapping oil

**ENTHALPY** exothermic = heat **released**, **ΔH NEGATIVE** — combustion, **respiration**,
neutralisation, quicklime in water · endothermic = heat **absorbed**, **ΔH POSITIVE** —
**photosynthesis**, thermal decomposition, electrolysis

**OIL RIG** **O**xidation **I**s **L**oss, **R**eduction **I**s **G**ain — of **electrons**

**RUSTING NEEDS OXYGEN AND MOISTURE, BOTH** · prevented by painting, oiling, **galvanisation** (zinc),
alloying · **rancidity** retarded by antioxidants and nitrogen flushing

**METALS** basic oxides, good conductors, **mercury liquid**
**NON-METALS** **acidic** oxides, poor conductors **except graphite**, **bromine** liquid

**REACTIVITY** K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > **Zn** > Fe > Pb > (H) > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au

**ALLOYS** brass = Cu + **Zn** · bronze = Cu + **Sn** · stainless steel = Fe + **Cr + Ni** · solder = Pb +
Sn · amalgam contains **mercury**

**CARBON** tetravalency and catenation · **diamond hardest, non-conductor** · **graphite conducts**, soft,
lubricant · fullerene C-60

**NAMES** dry ice = **solid CO₂** · laughing gas = **N₂O** · marsh gas = **CH₄** · aqua regia =
**concentrated HCl + concentrated HNO₃** · vinegar = dilute ethanoic acid
