| Assertion-Reason items | 0 | 0 | **5** |

:::

::: note | NOTE · WHY THE COUNT AND ASSERTION-REASON LOADING IS THE RIGHT WAY TO MAKE A MOCK HARDER
There are two ways to harden a paper. You can ask **obscurer facts**, or you can ask **the same facts in
formats that require complete rather than partial knowledge.** The first produces a paper that does not
resemble the examination and teaches nothing. The second produces exactly the pressure the real paper
applies.

F11.4 §3.1 explains the mathematics: in the older *1 and 2 only* format, confident knowledge of two
statements often determined the answer, whereas the count format requires you to adjudicate **every**
statement. **One uncertain statement destroys the item.** Seven such items here is 17.5 marks that cannot be
half-won.

So the facts in this paper are not exotic. **The formats are.** That is the design.
:::

**Passage for Questions 15 to 18.**

There is a temptation, when a body of law is consolidated, to treat the consolidation as the reform. It is
not. Replacing twenty-nine enactments with four is an act of drafting, and drafting is the cheapest part of
any reform programme. What determines whether a labour code improves the position of a worker is not the
elegance of its definitions but the density of the administrative machinery available to enforce them, and
that machinery is not created by a gazette notification.

The point can be put more sharply. A statute that extends an entitlement to a worker who has no registered
employer has extended nothing until somebody can identify the worker, identify the person liable, and
collect from that person. Each of those three steps is an administrative capability rather than a legal one.
This is why the sceptical question to ask of any social security reform is not whether the entitlement is
generous but whether the obligation is collectible. Generosity on paper is free; collection is expensive,
and it is where reform programmes are quietly abandoned.

None of which is an argument against consolidation. Simplification has genuine value: a single registration
is cheaper for the employer than six, and a single definition of wages removes an avenue of avoidance. But
these are gains in **compliance cost**, not in coverage, and the two are routinely conflated by those
announcing the reform and by those opposing it.
