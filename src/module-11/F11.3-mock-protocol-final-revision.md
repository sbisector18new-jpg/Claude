Every other chapter in this manual adds coverage. **This one adds nothing and is worth more than most of

retrieval matters more than coverage.

the schedule. §2 is the discipline that makes mocks worth sitting. **§5 is the protocol for the day, and it

## 1. Session 1 — When a Mock Is Worth Taking ★★★★★

### 1.1 The five conditions

A mock is a **measuring instrument**, and an instrument used carelessly gives a reading worse than no
reading, because it is believed.

| Condition | Why |
|---|---|
| **1 Full 120 minutes, uninterrupted** | The paper's difficulty *is* the time constraint. A mock taken in two sittings measures knowledge, which you already know you have |
| **2 One sitting, no breaks, no phone** | Twenty minutes of the real paper will be spent tired. That has to be practised |
| **3 Unseen questions** | A question you have met before measures memory of the answer, not retrieval of the reasoning |
| **4 At the same time of day as the real paper** | If the RT is a morning paper, do not take mocks at midnight |
| **5 Scored honestly, including the negative marking** | A score computed without the penalty flatters you by exactly the amount you most need to see |

**Condition 3 is the one that limits how many genuine mocks are available**, and §3 addresses it directly and
honestly.

### 1.2 How many, and when

| Mock | When | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Mock 1** (F11.2) | **Week 8** — F8.0's calendar places it there | **Diagnostic.** Establishes the baseline and the weak parts |
| **Mock 2** | Week 10 | Tests whether the week 8 diagnosis has been acted on |
| **Mock 3** | Week 12 | Mid-point check under improved conditions |
| **Mock 4** | Two weeks before the paper | Full dress rehearsal, same time of day |
| **Mock 5** | **One week before** | Final calibration. **Not later** |

::: trap | TRAP · DO NOT SIT A MOCK IN THE LAST THREE DAYS
A poor score three days before the paper does damage out of all proportion to its information value: there
is no longer time to act on it, and the loss of confidence is carried into the hall.

**The last mock goes one week out.** The final three days are for **wall sheets, the error log and sleep** —
§5.

Equally, **do not sit mocks more often than fortnightly.** A mock consumes unseen material and produces
about ninety minutes of analysis; sitting them weekly means either skipping the analysis, which wastes the
mock, or exhausting the material, which leaves nothing for the dress rehearsal.
:::

## 2. Session 2 — The Post-Mock Protocol ★★★★★

### 2.1 The ninety minutes after

Do these five things in order, on the same day.

| Step | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| **1 Score it honestly** | 10 min | Including the **−0.8333**. Count blanks separately from wrongs |
| **2 Complete the analysis table** | 10 min | F11.2 §15 — accuracy, attempt rate, weakest part, marks lost to penalty |
| **3 Read EVERY explanation** | 40 min | Including for questions you answered correctly. A right answer for the wrong reason is a future error |
| **4 Log every error** | 20 min | §2.3's format. **This is the step that compounds** |
| **5 Write one line** | 5 min | *What will I do differently in the next fortnight?* One sentence, specific |

**Step 3 is the one candidates skip and the one that matters most after step 4.** Roughly one right answer
in six at this level is a **lucky guess or correct reasoning applied to the wrong feature** — and you cannot
tell which without reading the explanation.

### 2.2 Reading the four numbers

F11.2 §15 gives the table. The diagnosis is mechanical:

| Symptom | Diagnosis | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy **below 75%** | Guessing too loosely | **Attempt fewer.** Re-read F11.1 §1.2 and apply the elimination rule strictly |
| Attempt rate **below 80%** | Too slow, or too cautious | Speed drills on the **cheap formats** — vocabulary at 25 seconds (F8.1), recall at 20 |
| Accuracy fine, attempt rate fine, total still low | **A specific subject** | Re-read the weakest part's chapter and redo its bank |
| Marks lost to penalty **above 15** | Elimination discipline failing | Practise **naming the eliminated option** before answering |

**The distinction that matters:** *speed*, *accuracy* and *subject knowledge* are three different faults with
three different treatments, and a single total score conceals which you have. **A candidate who treats a
speed problem by reading more chapters will not improve.**

### 2.3 The error log

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE FOURTH COLUMN IS THE WHOLE POINT
F8.0 §5 introduced this and it is restated here because in the last four weeks the error log **becomes the
primary study material**.

| Date | Source | What I chose | Correct | **Why I got it wrong — in my own words** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 Oct | Mock 1 Q77 | ₹25,000 | ₹15,000 | Took a reported proposal as notified law |
| 12 Oct | Mock 1 Q32 | 38% | 20% | Used 2024 as the base when the stem said 2025 |
| 12 Oct | Mock 1 Q71 | (a) | (d) | Read past the word "not" in the stem |

**"Silly mistake" is not a diagnosis and teaches nothing.** The entry must be specific enough to act on:
*"I applied the percentage before the deduction"*, *"I used the slant height as the vertical height"*, *"I did
not check which year the stem named"*.

**Because when you have thirty entries you will find that four or five root causes produce most of your
errors.** F8.0 §5 predicts the top three: **missing the word "not"**, **unit and conversion failures**, and
**formula swaps within a matched pair**. Mock 1's own analysis in F11.2 §16 shows all three present.

**Four or five root causes is a tractable problem. "Get better at the paper" is not.**

**The weekly review, fifteen minutes:** re-attempt every error of the week without looking; **star** any you
repeat; re-test starred items every week until clean; count root causes and name the top three.
:::

## 3. Session 3 — Assembling Mocks 2 to 5 ★★★★★

::: important | IMPORTANT · THE HONEST POSITION ON MOCKS 2 TO 5
**Mock 1 in F11.2 is 120 fresh questions. Mocks 2 to 5 are not written out in this manual, and the reason
should be stated plainly rather than disguised.**

Four further papers would be **480 more questions with explanations**. Authoring them to the standard of
F11.2 in one sitting is not something I can do honestly, and a padded set of low-quality items would be
**worse than none** — you would be calibrating against questions that do not resemble the paper. This is the
same judgement F8.0 §0 records about its calendar: *it schedules existing questions rather than pretending to
contain 72 days of fresh ones.*

**What this section gives you instead is an assembly recipe.** The manual contains **994 practice questions**
across 35 chapters. Drawn correctly, they make a paper of the right shape, and — critically — **questions you
answered weeks ago and have not seen since function as substantially unseen** at retrieval. Spaced
re-testing is not a poor substitute for fresh questions; it is what the last four weeks should consist of
anyway.

**Where the recipe cannot reach:** Modules 1, 2, 3 and 5 are **not in this repository** (register §9), so
**44 of the 120 questions in each mock must come from your own material or from the previous papers.**
That gap is the strongest practical reason to supply those modules and the two papers into `pyq/`.

## 4. Session 4 — The Final Four Weeks ★★★★★

### 4.1 The principle

> **In the last four weeks, retrieval beats coverage.** Nothing new is read. Everything is recalled, timed,
> or corrected.

F8.0 §3.3 already applies this to the evening drill — from week 9 its calendar stops introducing material and
runs only error-log work and timed sections. §4.2 extends it to the morning slot.

### 4.2 The schedule

**Two hours a day: one hour of module revision in the morning, thirty minutes of F8.0 drill in the evening,
thirty minutes of error log.** Saturdays carry the mocks.

| Week | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **−4** | M1 wall sheets + bank | M2 wall sheets + bank | M3 wall sheets + bank | M6 wall sheets + bank | Error log, whole | **Mock 4** (dress rehearsal) |
| **−3** | M8 wall sheets, all 9 | M9 wall sheets, all 7 | M5 wall sheets + bank | M4 wall sheets, all 5 | **F10.1 §4 sweep** (2 hrs) | Timed sections: English + Quant |
| **−2** | Error log, English half | Error log, Quant half | M2 and M3 again — **the codes and EPS 2026** | M1 again | Mixed 30, timed | **Mock 5** (final) |
| **−1** | Mock 5 analysis | Weakest two modules only | Wall sheets, all 34 | Error log, starred only | Wall sheets + F11.1 §1 | **Rest. Nothing new** |

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE THREE THINGS THIS SCHEDULE DOES DELIBERATELY
**1 It revises by WALL SHEET, not by chapter.** Every chapter but F8.0 ends with a five-minute wall sheet, and
**34 of them is about three hours for the entire manual**. In the last month you should be able to recover a
chapter from its wall sheet; if you cannot, that is the signal to re-open the chapter — and it is a far
cheaper diagnostic than re-reading everything.

**2 It puts the F10.1 §4 verification sweep at week −3.** Not earlier, because figures move; not later,
because you need time to absorb the twelve answers. **Diarise it now.**

**3 It revisits Modules 2 and 3 twice**, at −4 and again at −2. They are **52 planning marks**, they are the
subject matter of the post, and they are the two modules most exposed to the labour codes and EPS 2026. If
your material for them is old, the second pass is where you correct it.

**And note what the last week does NOT contain:** no mock, no new chapter, no new source. **Wall sheets, the
starred error log, and rest.**
:::

### 4.3 The spaced-revision queue

For anything you have starred twice in the error log, use expanding intervals: **re-test after 1 day, then 3,
then 7, then 14.** An item cleanly recalled at 14 days is safe. An item failed at any interval goes back to
the beginning of the queue.

**This applies to about thirty or forty items, not to the syllabus.** It is a scalpel, not a schedule.

## 5. Session 5 — The Day Before, and the Day Of ★★★★★

### 5.1 The day before

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Read the **wall sheets** — three hours for all 34, or the twelve you are weakest on | **Do not** sit a mock |
| Re-read the **starred** error log entries only | **Do not** open a chapter you have not read before |
| Check the **admit card, photo identity and the reporting time**; plan the journey | **Do not** attempt to learn a new topic |
| Re-read **F11.1 §1** — the marking arithmetic and the three passes | **Do not** discuss the paper with other candidates |
| **Sleep normally.** Set two alarms | **Do not** stay up late "finishing" anything |

### 5.2 The morning

1. **Eat.** Two hours of sustained attention is a physical task
2. **Arrive early.** Reporting time, not start time
3. **Carry only what is permitted.** Admit card, identity proof, the pens specified
4. **In the last five minutes before the paper**, read nothing. Do not open a book in the hall — a
   half-remembered fact glimpsed at the door displaces a well-remembered one

### 5.3 In the hall

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE SIX RULES, IN ORDER OF WHEN THEY APPLY
**1 First minute — write the time budget on the rough sheet.** *Pass 1 ends at T+45. Pass 2 ends at
T+100.* You will not calculate reliably under pressure later.

**2 Pass 1, 45 minutes — answer only what you know immediately.** Anything requiring calculation or
deliberation is skipped. Mark skipped questions with a clear symbol.

**3 Pass 2, 55 minutes — the calculable ones.** Mensuration, DI, arithmetic. You now know how many remain
and how long you have for each.

**4 Pass 3, 20 minutes — eliminate and guess.** Fill every bubble where you can rule out **one** option. A
blind guess is worth zero; a one-option elimination is worth **+0.28** — F11.1 §1.2.

**5 Throughout — underline the word "not".** About a fifth of the paper is negatively framed, and
misreading one costs a **3.33-mark swing on a question you knew**.

**6 Do not leave early.** There is no advantage to it, and pass 3 is worth several marks. Use all 120
minutes.

**And one rule about the paper's psychology:** if a question looks familiar and an option feels obviously
right, that is the moment to slow down for five seconds. The paper's dominant trap is a **correct fact about
something adjacent** — F11.1 §3.2. Familiarity is exactly the sensation that trap produces.
:::

### 5.4 Afterwards

The RT is **75% of the final weight**; the interview carries the rest. **Do not attempt a post-mortem with
other candidates outside the hall** — it is unreliable and demoralising in equal measure.

Keep your **rough sheet and your recollection of any question you were unsure of**. If a further stage
follows, those are the topics to revisit — and if the paper is analysed for a future sitting, that record is
the raw material for a PYQ update, which register §9 and F11.1 §0 both record as the manual's outstanding
need.
