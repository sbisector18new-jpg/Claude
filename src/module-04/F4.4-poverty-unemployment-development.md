::: must-master | MUST MASTER · IN THIS CHAPTER, LEARN THE DEFINITIONS AND NOT THE NUMBERS
Poverty and unemployment are the parts of Module 4 where candidates waste the most time, because the
statistics are voluminous, they change every survey round, and they are the least likely thing to be asked.

**What is asked is the machinery of measurement.** Which committee used consumption expenditure rather than
calories. What the unemployment rate is a percentage *of*. Which three dimensions the Human Development
Index combines and by what kind of average. Whether a rising Gini means more or less inequality.

Those have permanent answers. **A current unemployment rate does not**, and it is quoted in this chapter
only in a dated callout with a source, per the convention set in F4.1 §0.

So: learn §1's committee sequence, §2's four unemployment concepts and three rate definitions, and §3's
index compositions. Read the numbers once and let them go.

## 1. Session 1 — Poverty ★★★★★

### 1.1 The two concepts

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Absolute poverty** | Deprivation measured against a **fixed line** — a specified basket or calorie norm — the same standard regardless of what others have |
| **Relative poverty** | Deprivation judged against the **standard of living prevailing in that society**. A relatively poor person in a rich country may be absolutely better off than an average person in a poor one |

**Developing countries generally measure absolute poverty; developed countries generally measure relative
poverty**, often as a fraction of median income. The distinction is the commonest conceptual question in the
topic.

### 1.2 The committees, in order

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE SEQUENCE, AND WHAT EACH CHANGED
The examiner asks either **who used what basis** or **in what order they reported**. Both are answered by
this table.

| Committee / Group | Year | What it did |
|---|---|---|
| **Dandekar and Rath** | 1971 | Early estimation based on **calorie norms** |
| **Alagh Task Force** | **1979** | Fixed the poverty line on **calorie intake: 2,400 kcal rural, 2,100 kcal urban** |
| **Lakdawala Committee** | **1993** | State-specific poverty lines; used **CPI-IW and CPI-AL** to update them |
| **Tendulkar Committee** | **2009** | Moved away from calories to **Monthly Per Capita Consumption Expenditure (MPCE)**, a **mixed reference period**, and included **health and education spending** |
| **Rangarajan Committee** | **2014** | Reverted to separate rural and urban baskets; produced **higher** poverty estimates than Tendulkar |

**The two facts most often tested:** the **calorie norms are 2,400 rural and 2,100 urban** — note that the
**rural** figure is the higher one, because rural work is more physically demanding, which is the
counter-intuitive part — and **Tendulkar is the committee that abandoned the calorie basis for consumption
expenditure**.

**The order is Alagh (1979) » Lakdawala (1993) » Tendulkar (2009) » Rangarajan (2014).**
:::

### 1.3 The measures

| Measure | What it captures |
|---|---|
| **Head count ratio** | The **proportion** of the population below the line. Says nothing about how far below |
| **Poverty gap index** | The **average shortfall** from the line — the depth of poverty |
| **Squared poverty gap** | Weights larger shortfalls more heavily — the **severity** of poverty |

**Why more than one is needed:** if a transfer moves a person from just below the line to just above it, the
head count improves markedly while the poorest are untouched. The gap measures are what prevent that from
looking like success.

### 1.4 The Multidimensional Poverty Index

| Feature | |
|---|---|
| **Global MPI** | Published by **UNDP and the Oxford Poverty and Human Development Initiative (OPHI)** |
| **National MPI** | Published by **NITI Aayog** |
| Method | The **Alkire-Foster** method |
| **Three dimensions** | **Health, education, and standard of living** |
| Indicators | Twelve in the national index, drawn from the National Family Health Survey |

**The point of a multidimensional measure** is that income is a means and not the deprivation itself: a
household above an income line but without sanitation, schooling or clean fuel is poor in a sense the income
line cannot see. **The three dimensions are health, education and standard of living** — note that
**income is not one of them**, which is exactly what a question will test.

## 2. Session 2 — Unemployment ★★★★★

### 2.1 The types

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Open** | Willing and able to work, no work available at all |
| **Disguised** | **More people employed than are needed** — removing some would not reduce output. **Characteristic of Indian agriculture** |
| **Seasonal** | Work available only in part of the year — again agriculture, and tourism |
| **Structural** | A **mismatch** between workers' skills or location and the jobs available. Long-lasting |
| **Cyclical** | Caused by a **downturn in aggregate demand**; disappears in recovery |
| **Frictional** | Short-term, while moving between jobs — a search phenomenon, not a shortage |
| **Technological** | Displacement by mechanisation or automation |
| **Educated / underemployment** | Qualified persons in work below their qualification, or working fewer hours than they wish |

::: trap | TRAP · DISGUISED, STRUCTURAL AND CYCLICAL ARE THE THREE THAT GET SWAPPED
- **Disguised** — the work is *shared* among too many. Output would not fall if some left. **Agriculture**
- **Structural** — the jobs exist but the **skills or location do not match**. A textile worker in a
  district where only software firms are hiring
- **Cyclical** — the **jobs have temporarily disappeared** because demand fell. A construction worker in a
  recession

The distinguishing question is: **is the problem too many people for the work (disguised), the wrong people
for the work (structural), or not enough work for now (cyclical)?** Each is offered as a distractor for the
others.
:::

### 2.2 How India measures it

| Approach | Reference period |
|---|---|
| **Usual Status (principal + subsidiary)** | The **preceding 365 days** — the *long-term* measure, gives the **lowest** unemployment rate |
| **Current Weekly Status** | The preceding **7 days** |
| **Current Daily Status** | Each **half-day** of the reference week — the most exacting, gives the **highest** rate |

**The three rates, and what each is a percentage of:**

> **Labour Force Participation Rate (LFPR) = labour force ÷ population**
> **Worker Population Ratio (WPR) = employed persons ÷ population**
> **Unemployment Rate (UR) = unemployed persons ÷ LABOUR FORCE**

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE UNEMPLOYMENT RATE IS A SHARE OF THE LABOUR FORCE, NOT OF THE POPULATION
This is the single most examined definition in the topic, and getting it wrong makes every related
calculation wrong.

**The labour force = the employed + the unemployed who are actively seeking work.** Someone who is not
looking for work — a student, a homemaker, a retired person — is **outside the labour force entirely** and
appears in **neither** the numerator nor the denominator of the unemployment rate.

**Two consequences that are asked:**

1. **The unemployment rate can FALL because people stop looking for work.** They leave the labour force, so
   the denominator shrinks and the numerator shrinks — an improvement in the rate that reflects
   discouragement rather than employment. This is why the **LFPR must be read alongside the UR**
2. **India's female LFPR is far below the male figure**, which is the largest single reason overall LFPR is
   low. A low female LFPR *reduces* the measured unemployment rate, because women not seeking work are not
   counted as unemployed

**The survey:** the **Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS)**, conducted by the **National Statistical Office
under MoSPI**, which began in 2017-18 and is **now released monthly** as well as quarterly and annually — a
change from the earlier annual-only design, and worth knowing because older material describes it as an
annual survey.
:::

**female LFPR is roughly a third**, well under half the male rate. *Content rephrased from the sources for

### 2.3 The rural employment guarantee — MGNREGA replaced

::: high-yield | HIGH YIELD · THE GUARANTEE IS NOW 125 DAYS, NOT 100  `[AS AT 16 AUG 2026]`
**This is the most important correction in Module 4, and almost all existing study material gets it wrong.**

The **Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005 has been REPLACED** by the **Viksit
Bharat — Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) Act, 2025**, known as **VB-G RAM G**.

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Passed by Parliament | **18–19 December 2025** |
| **In force across India** | **1 July 2026** |
| **The guarantee** | **125 days** of wage employment per rural household per financial year — **up from 100** |
| Who is entitled | Every rural household whose adult members volunteer for **unskilled manual work** |
| Design | Links employment to **durable rural infrastructure** and climate resilience; aligned to **Viksit Bharat @2047** |
| Contested | A greater share of funding on **States**; **top-down** allocation; **face-authentication** attendance |

Sources: [PRS Bill Track](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-viksit-bharat-%E2%80%93-guarantee-for-rozgar-and-ajeevika-mission-gramin-vb-%E2%80%93-g-ram-g-bill-2025) ·
[PIB on the 125-day guarantee](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/may/doc2026511867601.pdf) ·
[Economic Times explainer](https://m.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/economy/policy/explained-what-is-the-new-vb-g-ram-g-act-and-how-will-it-replace-mgnrega/articleshow/131013803.cms)

**What carries over.** The architecture is recognisably the same: a **legal guarantee** rather than a scheme,
an **unemployment allowance** if work is not provided within **15 days**, **one-third participation by
women**, **social audit** by the Gram Sabha, and a **demand-driven** design in which the work follows the
application rather than an allocation.

**What to hold for the paper:** **100 becomes 125**, passage is **December 2025**, and commencement is
**1 July 2026**. An option offering 100 days is the **superseded** answer; an option offering December 2025
as the date the guarantee changed confuses passage with commencement. F10.3 §2.2 carries the fuller record.
*Content rephrased from the sources for compliance with licensing restrictions.*
:::

## 3. Session 3 — Human Development and Inequality ★★★★☆

### 3.1 The Human Development Index

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THREE DIMENSIONS, FOUR INDICATORS, AND A GEOMETRIC MEAN
| Feature | |
|---|---|
| **Published by** | **UNDP**, in the **Human Development Report**, **first in 1990** |
| **Conceived by** | **Mahbub ul Haq**, with **Amartya Sen** |
| **Dimension 1 — health** | **Life expectancy at birth** |
| **Dimension 2 — education** | **Mean years of schooling** (adults) and **expected years of schooling** (children) |
| **Dimension 3 — standard of living** | **Gross national income per capita, at purchasing power parity** |
| **Aggregation** | The **GEOMETRIC mean** of the three dimension indices |
| Range | **0 to 1** |

**Three things get asked, and all three are in that table:**

1. **What is NOT in the HDI.** Unemployment, inequality, poverty, gender and environmental quality are all
   **absent**. The HDI is deliberately austere — three dimensions, nothing else — and separate indices
   exist for the rest
2. **The geometric mean, not the arithmetic.** This was a change made in 2010, and it matters
   substantively: a geometric mean **punishes imbalance**, so a country cannot compensate for very low life
   expectancy with very high income. An arithmetic mean would let it
3. **GNI per capita, not GDP** — and **at PPP**, not market exchange rates
:::

### 3.2 The related indices

| Index | What it adds |
|---|---|
| **IHDI** — Inequality-adjusted HDI | Discounts the HDI for **inequality** in each dimension |
| **GDI** — Gender Development Index | Ratio of female to male HDI |
| **GII** — Gender Inequality Index | Reproductive health, empowerment, labour market |
| **MPI** | Multidimensional poverty — §1.4 |
| **PQLI** — Physical Quality of Life Index | **Morris D Morris**: literacy, **infant mortality**, and **life expectancy at age one**. **Excludes income entirely** |

::: trap | TRAP · PQLI EXCLUDES INCOME; HDI INCLUDES IT
The **Physical Quality of Life Index** was an earlier attempt at a welfare measure and it uses **three
indicators with no income component at all** — literacy, infant mortality and life expectancy at age one.
The **HDI includes income** as one of its three dimensions.

So a question asking which composite index **omits income** is asking for the **PQLI**. Note also that PQLI
uses life expectancy **at age one**, not at birth, precisely because infant mortality is counted separately
and would otherwise be double counted.
:::

### 3.3 Measuring inequality

| Tool | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Lorenz curve** | A graph of the **cumulative share of income against the cumulative share of population**. The **45-degree line is perfect equality**; the further the curve sags below it, the greater the inequality |
| **Gini coefficient** | The area between the Lorenz curve and the line of equality, as a proportion of the whole triangle. **0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality** |
| **Kuznets curve** | The hypothesis that inequality **rises then falls** as an economy develops — an inverted U |

> **A RISING Gini coefficient means RISING inequality.** The direction is the whole question, and the
> reversed statement is a standard "not correct" option.

:::

## 4. Session 4 — The Welfare Architecture ★★★★☆

This section overlaps Module 3 and can be read as revision. It is included because a *"which scheme does
what"* question is among the most likely six marks in Module 4.

| Scheme | What it provides |
|---|---|
| **VB-G RAM G Act, 2025** (replacing MGNREGA, 2005) | **125 days** of guaranteed rural wage employment, from **1 July 2026** — §2.3 |
| **National Food Security Act, 2013** | Subsidised foodgrain as a **legal entitlement**; priority households and Antyodaya |
| **Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY** | Health cover of **₹5 lakh per family per year** for secondary and tertiary care |
| PM Awas Yojana | Housing, rural and urban |
| PM-KISAN | Income support to landholding farmers |
| **Atal Pension Yojana** | A **guaranteed pension** of ₹1,000 to ₹5,000 a month, for the unorganised sector |
| **PMJJBY / PMSBY** | Low-premium **life** and **accident** insurance — see F7.1 |
| **e-Shram** | The national **database of unorganised workers** |
| **Code on Social Security, 2020** | Brings **gig and platform workers** within a contributory framework — F8.3's fourth passage |

::: know | KNOW · WHY THIS TABLE MATTERS TO THIS PARTICULAR POST
An Assistant Provident Fund Commissioner administers **contributory** social insurance. The schemes above
are mostly **non-contributory assistance**, funded from general revenues.

That distinction — the one F8.3's first passage is built on — is the organising idea of the whole Indian
social security system, and it explains the coverage gap the **Code on Social Security, 2020** was written
to address: contributory schemes reach those with an identifiable employer, and assistance schemes reach the
rest.

**Expect it in the interview as well as the paper.** The strong answer is not a list of schemes but the
observation that India runs two parallel systems on different financing principles, and that the growth of
platform work sits exactly on the boundary between them.
:::

## 7. One-Minute Revision

::: check | ONE-MINUTE REVISION · CHAPTER F4.4
**LEARN DEFINITIONS, NOT NUMBERS.** The machinery of measurement is permanent; the statistics change every
