::: important | IMPORTANT · THE WINDOW, THE CUT-OFF, AND WHAT THAT MEANS
This chapter and F10.4 together are the **exhaustive twelve-month dossier**: events from **August 2025 to
the cut-off of 1 August 2026**. Every item carries a source link.

**Three conventions govern the whole dossier, and they matter more than any single fact in it.**

**1 The cut-off is applied strictly.** Where an event straddles 1 August 2026 it is included and the
straddle is **stated** — the Commonwealth Games closed on 2 August 2026, one day outside, and F10.4 §5 says
so rather than quietly rounding. Where an item falls days *before* the window opens it is marked as a
preceding anchor, as NISAR's launch of 30 July 2025 is in F10.4 §3.

**2 Weighting follows F10.1 §1.2, not the news cycle.** An EPFO paper's eight current affairs marks are not
distributed like a general knowledge quiz. **§1 — labour, social security and the EPFO — is the longest
section in the dossier and should be read first and twice.** Sport, awards and obituaries are compact by
deliberate decision, and F10.4 §5 records why.

**3 The dossier does not replace F10.1's system; it starts it.** F10.1 §3 prescribes a monthly register the
candidate keeps himself. This chapter supplies the **twelve months already elapsed**, so that the register
begins with a full year behind it rather than from zero. **From today, you maintain it.** F10.1 §4's
twelve-item sweep still applies to every figure here.
:::

### 1.2 What the final rules did, and did not, do

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| **Minimum wage calculation formula** | **Dropped** from the final rules |
| **Floor wage** | Provided for, to be fixed on **minimum living standards**; the rules do **not** specify detailed norms |
| **National floor level minimum wage** | Remains **advisory, not binding** — about **₹176 to ₹178 a day**, unrevised since 2017–2019 |
| **Definition of wages** | **Basic pay plus dearness allowance must be at least 50% of total remuneration**; any excess above 50% is **added back to wages** |

**The 50% rule is the provision with the largest practical effect**, and it is the one F10.2 §1.2 identifies
as the change that most affects EPFO administration: it prevents an employer from structuring pay to keep
the wage component — and therefore the provident fund contribution — artificially small.

::: high-yield | HIGH YIELD · EPFO OFFICERS, INCLUDING APFCs, NOTIFIED UNDER THE SOCIAL SECURITY CODE
**This is the single most directly relevant item in the entire dossier to the post you are applying for.**

The Government has notified **EPFO officers as authorities under Chapter III of the Code on Social
Security, 2020**, to operationalise enforcement, recovery and compliance under the Code. The categories
named are:

- **Central Provident Fund Commissioners**
- **Additional Central Provident Fund Commissioners**
- **Regional Provident Fund Commissioners**
- **Assistant Provident Fund Commissioners**
- **Enforcement Officers**

They have been **appointed as Recovery Officers** for the recovery of dues and enforcement of provident
fund obligations under the Code.

Source: [Taxmann on the notification of EPFO officers under the Code](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/government-authorises-epfo-officers-under-social-security-code)

**Why this matters twice over.** First, it is an obviously examinable fact about the organisation. Second,
and more usefully, **it tells you what the job now is**: the statutory powers an Assistant Provident Fund
Commissioner exercises — inquiry, determination, recovery — now flow from the **Code on Social Security,
2020** rather than from the 1952 Act directly, because the 1952 Act has been subsumed into it (F10.2 §1.2).

**For the interview**, that is a far better answer than reciting Section 7A: the powers are the same in
substance, but their source has moved, and the officer's authority now sits inside a consolidated code
covering provident fund, insurance, gratuity, maternity benefit and gig workers together. *Content
rephrased from the source for compliance with licensing restrictions.*
:::

### 1.5 The India–UK Double Contributions Convention

::: high-yield | HIGH YIELD · A SOCIAL SECURITY AGREEMENT THE EPFO ITSELF ADMINISTERS
| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | A **Double Contributions Convention (DCC)** — a type of **Social Security Agreement** coordinating the *payment of contributions* |
| **In force** | **15 July 2026**, simultaneously with the India–UK trade agreement |
| **What it does** | Prevents **double social security contributions** for workers temporarily posted between the two countries |
| **Detachment exemption** | Up to **60 months — five years** |
| **Mechanism** | A **certificate of coverage**: the posted employee remains under **home country** legislation and is exempt in the host country |
| **What it does NOT do** | It does **not** govern access to benefits such as the State Pension |
| **EPFO's role** | EPFO has issued the **procedure for claiming exemption** |

Sources: [UK Government DCC explainer](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-india-trade-deal-double-contributions-convention-explainer/uk-india-double-contributions-convention-dcc-explainer) ·
[Ministry of Commerce note on CETA and the DCC entering into force](https://www.commerce.gov.in/files/d-m-y/1.pdf) ·
[Business Today on the EPFO's exemption procedure and the 60-month limit](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/story/epfo-issues-procedure-for-exemption-under-india-uk-social-security-agreement-543474-2026-07-16)

**Why an APFC candidate should know this cold.** Social Security Agreements are administered **through the
EPFO**, which issues certificates of coverage to Indian workers going abroad and processes exemption claims
for foreign nationals working in India. The DCC is the newest and largest such agreement, and the
**60-month exemption** is its headline number.

**The distinction to be precise about:** a DCC coordinates **contributions**, not **benefits**. An option
saying it gives Indian workers access to the UK State Pension is wrong. *Content rephrased from the sources
for compliance with licensing restrictions.*

### 2.2 The VB-G RAM G Act, 2025 — MGNREGA replaced

::: high-yield | HIGH YIELD · THE RURAL EMPLOYMENT GUARANTEE IS NOW 125 DAYS, NOT 100
The **Viksit Bharat — Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) Act, 2025**, universally
abbreviated **VB-G RAM G**, **replaces the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005**.

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Introduced in Lok Sabha | **16 December 2025** |
| Passed by Parliament | **18–19 December 2025** |
| Presidential assent | December 2025, by President **Droupadi Murmu** |
| **In force across India** | **1 July 2026** |
| **The guarantee** | **125 days** of wage employment in a financial year per rural household — **up from 100** |
| Who is entitled | Every rural household whose adult members volunteer for **unskilled manual work** |
| Design | Links wage employment to **durable rural infrastructure** across four priority areas; climate resilience; village-level planning; aligned to **Viksit Bharat @2047** |
| Contested features | A greater share of the funding burden on **States**; **top-down** resource allocation; digitisation and **face-authentication attendance** |

Sources: [PRS Bill Track and summary](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-viksit-bharat-%E2%80%93-guarantee-for-rozgar-and-ajeevika-mission-gramin-vb-%E2%80%93-g-ram-g-bill-2025) ·
