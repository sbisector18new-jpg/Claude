## 1. Session 1 — The Four Labour Codes ★★★★★

### 1.1 The central fact

::: high-yield | HIGH YIELD · IN FORCE FROM 21 NOVEMBER 2025, REPLACING 29 CENTRAL ACTS  `[AS AT 16 AUG 2026]`
| Code | Year of enactment |
|---|---|
| **Code on Wages** | **2019** |
| **Industrial Relations Code** | **2020** |
| **Code on Social Security** | **2020** |
| **Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code** | **2020** |

All four were **brought into force with effect from 21 November 2025**, and they **replace 29 central labour
laws**. The **central rules** giving them full effect were notified in 2026, the Government having considered
objections to the draft rules published in December 2025.

Sources: [The Hindu on operationalisation and the 29 Acts](https://www.thehindu.com/business/labour-reforms-government-fully-operationalises-four-new-codes-by-publishing-rules/article70958372.ece) ·
[Economic Times on the commencement date](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/labour-law-overhaul-done-centre-notifies-rules-for-all-4-labour-codes-new-wage-social-security-norms-kick-in/articleshow/130975018.cms) ·
[EY alert on the codes taking effect](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/alerts-hub/2025/11/new-labour-codes-implemented-across-the-country-effective-21-november-2025.pdf)

**Note the gap between enactment and commencement.** The Code on Wages was passed in **2019** and came into
force in **2025** — six years later. A question giving a *year* is probably asking about enactment; a question
giving a *date* is asking about commencement. F10.1 §7's second shape. *Content rephrased from the sources
for compliance with licensing restrictions.*
:::

### 1.2 Which Acts went into which code

| Code | Principal Acts subsumed |
|---|---|
| **Code on Wages, 2019** | **Payment of Wages Act 1936**, **Minimum Wages Act 1948**, Payment of Bonus Act 1965, Equal Remuneration Act 1976 |
| **Industrial Relations Code, 2020** | **Industrial Disputes Act 1947**, Trade Unions Act 1926, Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act 1946 |
| **Code on Social Security, 2020** | **Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act 1952**, ESI Act 1948, **Payment of Gratuity Act 1972**, Maternity Benefit Act 1961, Employees' Compensation Act 1923 |
| **OSH Code, 2020** | **Factories Act 1948**, Mines Act 1952, Contract Labour Act 1970, Inter-State Migrant Workmen Act 1979 |

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE ONE THAT MATTERS MOST TO THIS POST
**The Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952 is subsumed into the Code on Social
Security, 2020.** So is the ESI Act, gratuity, maternity benefit and workmen's compensation.

That single mapping is the most examinable fact in this session for an APFC candidate, because it means the
statute the post is built on now sits inside a code. **Four Acts to remember per code, and the social security
code's four are the ones to know cold.**

**Two definitional changes worth holding:**

1. **A uniform definition of "wages" applies across all four codes** — previously each Act defined it
   differently, which is what produced decades of litigation over what counted for provident fund purposes.
   This is the change with the largest practical effect on EPFO administration
2. **"Gig worker" and "platform worker" are now statutory categories**, defined for the first time — §2.5
:::

### 1.3 Gig and platform workers under the Code on Social Security

::: high-yield | HIGH YIELD · THE AGGREGATOR'S OBLIGATION  `[AS AT 16 AUG 2026]`
| Feature | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Aggregator's contribution** | **Between 1% and 2% of annual turnover**, subject to a **ceiling of 5% of the amount paid to gig and platform workers** |
| **Registration of workers** | Aggregators must upload worker details to a central portal **within 45 days** |
| **Interest on delayed contributions** | **12% per annum** |
| Record-keeping | **Real-time** recording of engagements and exits |
| Eligibility (draft rules) | Engagement of **not less than 90 days** with one aggregator, or **120 days** across multiple aggregators, in the last financial year |

Sources: [Livemint on the 1–2% turnover formula](https://www.livemint.com/opinion/online-views/why-gig-workers-social-security-contribution-formula-rejigged-insurance-gratuity-pf-epfo-11786526451559.html) ·
[Economic Times on the 45-day registration and 12% interest](https://m.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/india/govt-mandates-aggregators-to-register-gig-workers-on-portal-within-45-days/articleshow/130982123.cms) ·
[Livemint on the 90/120-day eligibility condition](https://www.livemint.com/news/india/new-labour-codes-centres-new-draft-rules-float-social-security-for-gig-workers-but-conditions-apply-90-days-of-11767329902512.html)

**Why the contribution is on TURNOVER rather than on wages** is the conceptual point, and it is the same one
F8.3's fourth passage makes: a platform worker has no wage an employer can be made to contribute against,
so the levy attaches to the only reliably identifiable base — the platform's revenue. *Content rephrased from

### 2.2 The wage ceiling — a proposal, not yet law

::: trap | TRAP · THE CEILING IS STILL ₹15,000. THE ₹25,000 FIGURE IS A PROPOSAL  `[AS AT 16 AUG 2026]`
This is the item most likely to be got wrong, because the proposed figure has been widely reported and reads
like a decided fact.

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| **Statutory wage ceiling for mandatory EPF coverage** | **₹15,000 a month — unchanged since 2014** |
| **Mandatory employee contribution at the ceiling** | **₹1,800 a month** (12% of ₹15,000); contributions above that are **voluntary** |
| The proposal | The Department of Expenditure has **approved a proposal to raise the ceiling to ₹25,000**, **subject to Cabinet approval and notification** |
| Status | **Not yet in effect** |
| Background | The **Supreme Court** asked the Government to reassess the ₹15,000 limit, unchanged since 2014 |

Sources: [Livemint on the Department of Expenditure approval](https://www.livemint.com/money/personal-finance/epf-wage-ceiling-may-rise-to-25-000-heres-how-it-could-boost-your-eps-pension-and-affect-pf-contributions-11785899225819.html) ·
[Economic Times on the ₹1,800 cap and voluntary excess](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/news/story/epfo-new-rules-2026-mandatory-pf-capped-at-rs1800-extra-savings-now-voluntary-540438-2026-07-02) ·
[Economic Times on the Supreme Court direction](http://economictimes.indiatimes.com/wealth/save/epfo-wage-ceiling-change-supreme-court-asks-provident-fund-body-to-increase-rs-15000-limit-what-it-means-for-you/articleshow/126389775.cms)

**The discipline this teaches is general and worth more than the fact.** Current affairs reporting does not
distinguish clearly between **proposed, approved, notified and in force**, and an examiner does. Four stages:

> **proposed » approved by a department » Cabinet approval » notified and in force**

**Only the last is law.** Whenever you record a change in your monthly sheet (F10.1 §3.2), **write which of
the four stages it has reached.** Q27 turns on exactly this. *Content rephrased from the sources for
compliance with licensing restrictions.*
:::

### 2.3 The Employees' Pension Scheme, 2026

::: high-yield | HIGH YIELD · EPS 2026 HAS REPLACED EPS-95  `[AS AT 16 AUG 2026]`
| Feature | Position |
|---|---|
| **New scheme** | **Employees' Pension Scheme, 2026**, effective **29 June 2026** |
| **What it replaced** | **EPS-95** (and the older EPS-71 family scheme arrangements) |
| **Unchanged** | The **pension formula** and the **contribution structure** |
| **Minimum monthly pension** | **₹1,000 — unchanged, and in force since 1 September 2014** |
| **New: claim settlement** | Pension claims to be settled within **20 days** |

| Omitted | The sub-paragraph giving a one-year window to opt to contribute on salary above the ceiling |
| Higher pension option | Arises from the **Supreme Court's judgment of 4 November 2022**; applications still being processed |

Sources: [Economic Times on EPS 2026 replacing EPS-95](https://m.economictimes.indiatimes.com/wealth/invest/eps-2026-replaces-eps-71-eps-95-what-has-changed-in-new-epfo-pension-scheme-and-what-remains-the-same/articleshow/132108888.cms) ·
[Economic Times on the ₹1,000 minimum being unchanged](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/wealth/invest/epfo-eps-2026-scheme-formula-has-the-minimum-pension-been-increased-from-rs-1000-explained/articleshow/132133026.cms) ·
[The Hindu on the omitted higher-pension clause](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/new-eps-rules-leave-out-clause-on-higher-pension/article70731399.ece)

**Two things to be precise about**, because both are widely misreported:

1. **The minimum pension has NOT been increased.** It remains **₹1,000**, as it has since 2014, despite
   sustained demands for ₹7,500. An option asserting an increase is wrong
2. **The formula has not changed.** EPS 2026 is largely a **consolidation and a service-standards reform** —
   the 20-day settlement and the 12% penalty on the EPFO are the substantive novelties

::: trap | TRAP · THE COVER AMOUNTS ARE ALL DIFFERENT AND ALL ROUND NUMBERS

as a triple — **scheme, amount, and unit** — because the unit is what most often distinguishes the right
option: PM-JAY's ₹5 lakh is **per family per year**, not per person, and the EPFO's ₹5 lakh is an
**auto-settlement threshold**, not a benefit.

   because reporting blurs the four stages and an examiner does not. When you record a change, record its
   stage
2. **The same number in two roles.** Q16 — ₹15,000 is both the ELI **benefit cap** and the EPF **wage

   distinct dates for one statute — introduction, enactment, commencement. **Dates cluster in a reform
   period and are offered for one another**
4. **The widely demanded figure.** Q8 — ₹7,500 is so often reported as a demand that it reads as a fact. **A
   number that appears constantly in headlines as an aspiration is a natural distractor**, and the answer is
