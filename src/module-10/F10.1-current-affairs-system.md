::: important | IMPORTANT · THE HONEST POSITION ON THIS MODULE, STATED FIRST
Module 10 is **20.0 planning marks over 12 hours** — eight questions — and it is the only module in this
manual that **cannot be finished**. Every other module is a closed body of material. This one acquires new
content every week until the day of the paper.

**So a chapter that is merely a list of facts is the wrong deliverable.** A list compiled in August 2026 for
a paper written in November or December 2026 is wrong about everything that happens in between, and worse,
it is **confidently** wrong — the failure mode F4.1 §0 introduced the dated-figure convention to prevent.

**What this chapter is instead:** a **collection system** (§3), a **verification protocol** (§4), and a
**watchlist of the specific things this examination asks about** (§5). It also contains the part of current
affairs that does **not** decay — §2's institutional attributions, which are what "current affairs"
questions in this examination overwhelmingly test.

**F10.2 is the dossier**: the facts as they stood on 16 August 2026, dated and sourced, to be re-verified
against §4's checklist before the paper.

That division — the method here, the perishable content there, clearly separated — is the only honest way to
put current affairs in a printed manual. It is the same principle as F8.0's calendar declaring that it
schedules existing questions rather than pretending to contain new ones.
:::

## 1. Session 1 — What Is Actually Being Tested ★★★☆☆

### 1.1 The three kinds of "current affairs" question

| Kind | Example | Decays? |
|---|---|---|
| **1 Institutional attribution** | Which body publishes the Human Development Report? | **No** — permanent |
| **2 A recent, definite change** | GST now has how many main slabs? What replaced EPS-95? | **Slowly** — stays askable for years |
| **3 A current reading** | What is the repo rate? The unemployment rate? | **Fast** — months |

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · MOST OF THIS MODULE'S MARKS ARE IN KINDS 1 AND 2, NOT KIND 3
This is the judgement that decides how to spend the twelve hours.

**Kind 3 — the current reading — is what candidates fear and what they spend their time on.** It is also the
**least** of the three in yield, because a paper set months before it is sat cannot safely ask for a number
that moves weekly, and because the setter knows it.

**Kind 1 is permanently askable and permanently learnable.** Who publishes the World Development Report has
been the same answer for forty years and will be the same answer in November. §2.2's table is therefore the
highest-value page in this module, and it can be finished in an evening.

**Kind 2 is the sweet spot** — a definite change, recent enough that older material is wrong, stable enough
to be a fair question. **The four labour codes coming into force, GST's two slabs, the Income-tax Act 2025,
the Employees' Pension Scheme 2026.** These are what F10.2 is built around, and F4.3's opening callout shows
the pattern.

**So spend the twelve hours roughly: four on §2's attributions, six on F10.2's kind-2 changes, and two on
the §4 sweep in the fortnight before the paper.** Not twelve on chasing the news.
:::

### 1.2 Where the marks actually sit for this examination

Blueprint §4.4's record of the 2023 paper shows current affairs appearing **inside other modules** rather
than as a labelled block — a question on a labour statute's amendment reads as labour law; a question on the
Budget reads as economy.

**That is what "integrated" in the module's name means**, and it has a practical consequence: **a current
affairs item is only worth learning if it attaches to something in the static syllabus.** The attachment
points, in order of yield for this post, are:

1. **Labour law and social security** — Modules 2 and 3
2. **The economy** — Module 4
3. Polity and governance — Module 1
4. Science and technology — Module 9

Sport, entertainment, obituaries and general trivia are **not** on that list. They are the bulk of a
commercial current affairs magazine and close to zero of this paper.

## 2. Session 2 — The Attributions That Do Not Decay ★★★★★

### 2.1 Why this is the best-value section in the module

A question asking **who publishes what** has a permanent answer, is easy to set, is unambiguous, and
separates prepared candidates from unprepared ones. Every examiner likes all four properties. Learn the
table and a predictable share of the eight marks becomes safe.

### 2.2 Reports and their publishers

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE REPORT-TO-PUBLISHER TABLE
| Report / Index | Published by |
|---|---|
| **Human Development Report**, HDI, IHDI, GII | **UNDP** |
| **Global Multidimensional Poverty Index** | **UNDP with OPHI** (Oxford) |
| **National Multidimensional Poverty Index**, SDG India Index | **NITI Aayog** |
| **World Development Report**, Human Capital Index | **World Bank** |
| **World Economic Outlook**, Fiscal Monitor | **IMF** |
| **Global Competitiveness Report**, **Global Gender Gap Report** | **World Economic Forum** |
| **Global Innovation Index** | **WIPO** — the World Intellectual Property Organization |
| **Corruption Perceptions Index** | **Transparency International** |
| **Global Hunger Index** | **Concern Worldwide and Welthungerhilfe** |
| **World Happiness Report** | **UN Sustainable Development Solutions Network** |
| **Press Freedom Index** | Reporters Without Borders (RSF) |
| **State of World Population** | **UNFPA** |
| State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World | **FAO** (with WFP, WHO, UNICEF, IFAD) |
| **World Employment and Social Outlook**, Global Wage Report | **ILO** |
| **Economic Survey** | **Department of Economic Affairs**, under the **Chief Economic Adviser** |
| **GDP, CPI, IIP, PLFS** | **NSO, under MoSPI** |
| **Wholesale Price Index** | **Office of the Economic Adviser, Ministry of Commerce and Industry** |
| **CPI-IW** | **Labour Bureau** |
| Annual Status of Education Report (**ASER**) | **Pratham Education Foundation** — a private body, not government |
| National Family Health Survey (**NFHS**) | Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, through IIPS |
| Sample Registration System (**SRS**) | **Registrar General of India** |

**The three traps in that table:**

1. **World Bank against IMF.** *Development* and *Human Capital* are World Bank; *Outlook* and *Fiscal
   Monitor* are IMF. The two are both Bretton Woods institutions in Washington, which is why they are
   offered for each other — F4.5 §4.3
2. **WEF against UNDP.** The **Gender Gap** report is the **World Economic Forum**; the **Gender Inequality
   Index** is **UNDP**. Two gender indices, two different bodies, and the names are close enough to swap
3. **The private publishers.** **ASER is Pratham**, not the government. The **Global Hunger Index** is two
   European NGOs, not the FAO. Candidates assume an official-sounding index must be official

**And one discontinued report worth knowing:** the World Bank **stopped publishing the Ease of Doing
Business rankings** in 2021. A question asserting that it still does is wrong, and it is the sort of stale
fact that survives in coaching material for years.
:::

### 2.3 The bodies most relevant to this post

| Body | Facts worth holding |
|---|---|
| **ILO** | Founded **1919**, under the Treaty of Versailles; the **oldest** UN specialised agency; became a UN agency in 1946; **headquarters Geneva**; **tripartite** — governments, employers and workers; India a **founder member** |
| **EPFO** | Administers the 1952 Act's schemes; apex body the **Central Board of Trustees (CBT)**, **chaired by the Union Minister for Labour and Employment**; also tripartite |
| **ESIC** | Employees' State Insurance Corporation — medical and cash benefits |
| **e-Shram** | The national **database of unorganised workers** |
| **NPCI** | Operates UPI — relevant because EPFO 3.0 settlement runs on it |

::: know | KNOW · THE ILO'S TRIPARTISM IS THE MOST EXAMINABLE THING ABOUT IT
The ILO is **unique among UN specialised agencies in its tripartite structure**: each member State's
delegation comprises **two government delegates, one employers' delegate and one workers' delegate**, and
the employer and worker delegates vote **independently of their government**.

That structure is mirrored in India's own **Central Board of Trustees**, which likewise seats government,
employer and employee representatives — and it is the institutional expression of the idea that labour
standards are negotiated rather than imposed.

**For the interview:** tripartism explains why labour law reform in India moves slowly and why the four
labour codes took from 2019 to 2025 to come into force. It is a better answer than "bureaucratic delay".
:::

## 3. Session 3 — The Collection System ★★★★★

### 3.1 The sources, in priority order

| Priority | Source | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **PIB (pib.gov.in) press releases** | The primary source. Every scheme, appointment and Cabinet decision appears here first and in official wording |
| **2** | **One national newspaper**, read for editorials and the economy pages | *The Hindu* or *Indian Express*. **One**, daily — not three, weekly |
| **3** | **Ministry of Labour and EPFO circulars** | Directly on this examination's subject. Circulars on the EPFO site are the single most targeted source available |
| **4** | **PRS Legislative Research (prsindia.org)** | Bill and Act summaries, Budget analysis, committee reports — the fastest way to get a statute's substance |
| **5** | **RBI and MoSPI press releases** | For the policy rates and the statistics |
| 6 | Economic Survey and Budget documents | Once a year, read the highlights |

**Not on the list, deliberately:** current affairs YouTube channels, daily quiz apps, and monthly compilation
magazines. Blueprint §22 permits coaching material as *supplementary*; it should not be the primary channel,
because it optimises for volume of items rather than for the attachment points §1.2 identifies.

### 3.2 The daily habit — fifteen minutes

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · ONE PAGE A MONTH, IN FOUR COLUMNS
The failure mode in current affairs is not insufficient reading. It is **reading without recording**, which
produces a sense of familiarity and no retrievable knowledge.

**The format — one sheet per month, four columns:**

| Date | Item | **Which module it attaches to** | Why it might be asked |
|---|---|---|---|
| 21 Nov 2025 | Four labour codes brought into force | **M2, M3** | Replaces 29 Acts; makes older answers wrong |
| 22 Sep 2025 | GST to two slabs, 5% and 18% | **M4** | Definite, recent, contradicts every textbook |
| 29 Jun 2026 | EPS 2026 replaces EPS-95 | **M3** | Directly on this post's subject matter |

**The third column is the one that does the work.** An item that cannot be attached to a module is an item
to **discard** — that is the operational meaning of "integrated", and it is what keeps the register to one
page a month instead of ten.

**Fifteen minutes a day:** ten reading PIB and the paper, five recording. Any item you cannot summarise in
one line does not go in.
:::

### 3.3 The monthly consolidation — thirty minutes

At the end of each month:

1. **Re-read the month's sheet** and strike out anything that has not turned into a durable fact
2. **Move surviving items** into the relevant module's revision notes — a labour item goes to Module 2's
   notes, not into a separate current affairs pile
3. **Write five questions** on the month's items, in the paper's four-option format, and add them to your
   error log's drill

Step 2 is what makes the module *integrated* rather than parallel. **By the end you should not have a
current affairs file at all** — you should have Modules 1 to 9, updated.

3. How long is the daily habit, and how is it split?

## 4. Session 4 — The Verification Protocol ★★★★★

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · THE FORTNIGHT-BEFORE SWEEP, ITEM BY ITEM
Every figure in this manual marked **`[AS AT 16 AUG 2026]`** must be re-checked before you sit the paper.
This is the checklist. It takes about **two hours** and it is the highest-return two hours in the module,
because it converts a set of possibly-stale facts into a set of certainly-current ones.

| # | Check | Where | Chapter to update |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Repo rate**, SDF, MSF, stance | RBI latest MPC statement | **F4.2 §2.3** |
| **2** | **CPI inflation**, latest and projected | RBI policy statement, MoSPI | F4.2 §3.3 |
| **3** | **GDP growth**, latest estimate and which estimate it is | MoSPI press release | **F4.1 §3.1** |
| **4** | **Fiscal deficit** target, current year | Budget at a Glance | **F4.3 §2.2** |
| **5** | **EPF interest rate** for the current year, and whether ratified | EPFO / PIB | **F10.2 §2** |
| **6** | **EPF wage ceiling** — still ₹15,000, or revised? | EPFO / PIB | **F10.2 §2** |
| **7** | **Any new EPFO scheme or circular** | EPFO website | F10.2 §2 |
| **8** | **Labour code rules** — further notifications | Ministry of Labour | **F10.2 §1** |
| **9** | **Unemployment rate**, LFPR | MoSPI monthly PLFS | F4.4 §2.2 |
| **10** | **Census 2027** progress and any date change | censusindia.gov.in | F4.5 §2.3 |
| **11** | **GST** — any further rate change | GST Council press release | F4.3 §3.2 |
| **12** | Office-holders: RBI Governor, Labour Minister, CEA | PIB | F4.2, F10.2 |

**How to record the result.** Do not annotate the PDF. Write the twelve answers on **one sheet**, dated, and
keep it with your wall sheets. If a figure has not changed, write *"unchanged"* — you need the positive
confirmation, because "I did not find a change" and "I did not look" feel identical a week later.
:::

### 4.1 The two-sentence rule for anything you cannot verify

If you cannot confirm a figure from a primary source, **do not carry it into the hall.** An unverified number
is worse than no number, because of the marking scheme: F8.0 §6 records that a blind guess has an expected
value of exactly zero, while an answer given confidently from a stale fact is a **negative** 0.8333 dressed
up as knowledge.

**Where a figure is uncertain, fall back on the structure.** You may not remember whether the repo rate is
5.25% or 5.50%, but you will remember that **the MSF is 25 basis points above it and the SDF 25 below** — and
questions about the corridor's *structure* are more common than questions about its level.

## 5. Session 5 — The EPFO Watchlist ★★★★★

This is the section that makes Module 10 worth twelve hours rather than four. **The eight current affairs
marks in an EPFO paper are not distributed like a general knowledge quiz** — they weight heavily towards
this organisation, its statutes and its schemes.

### 5.1 The standing watchlist

| Watch | Why it matters | Status at compilation |
|---|---|---|
| **The four labour codes** | Replace **29** central Acts; make pre-2025 labour material obsolete | **In force from 21 Nov 2025**; central rules notified 2026 |
| **EPF interest rate** | Declared annually by the **CBT**, ratified by Government | **8.25% for 2025-26** |
| **EPF wage ceiling** | ₹15,000 since 2014; a revision is under consideration | **Still ₹15,000** — a proposal to raise it is **not yet notified** |
| **Employees' Pension Scheme, 2026** | **Replaced EPS-95** | In force from **29 June 2026** |
| **EPFO 3.0** | UPI and ATM withdrawal, higher auto-settlement | Rolling out through 2026 |
| **ELI Scheme** | EPFO-administered employment incentive | Effective from **1 Aug 2025** |
| **Gig and platform workers** | Aggregator contributions under the Code on Social Security | Rules notified 2026 |
| Higher EPS pension litigation | The 2022 Supreme Court option, and pending applications | Ongoing |

F10.2 carries the detail and the sources for each.

::: important | IMPORTANT · A CURRENCY RISK IN THIS MANUAL'S OWN BACK CATALOGUE
Two of the items above are not merely new facts. **They invalidate content.**

1. **The four labour codes came into force on 21 November 2025, replacing 29 central labour laws** —
   including the Industrial Disputes Act 1947, the Payment of Wages Act 1936, the Minimum Wages Act 1948 and
   the Factories Act 1948
2. **The Employees' Pension Scheme, 2026 has replaced EPS-95** with effect from 29 June 2026

**Modules 2 and 3 were completed before this manual's build pipeline existed and are not in `src/`** — see
register §9. If they were written before late 2025, they teach the repealed Acts and EPS-95 **as live law**,
which is a serious defect in the two modules that together carry about **52 planning marks**.

**This cannot be checked from inside the repository, because the sources are not here.** It is now the
strongest reason to re-supply them: not merely so the master book can be built, but so that they can be
**read against the codes and corrected**. Recorded in register §9 as the top of the queue.
:::

### 5.2 How to read a change like the labour codes

When a statute is replaced, the examinable content is rarely the fine print. It is:

1. **What replaced what, and from when** — four codes, 29 Acts, 21 November 2025
2. **Which Acts were subsumed into which code** — wages into the Code on Wages; ID Act, Trade Unions Act and
   Standing Orders Act into the Industrial Relations Code; EPF, ESI, gratuity, maternity benefit into the
   Code on Social Security; Factories Act and Mines Act into the OSH Code
3. **The definitions that changed** — notably a **uniform definition of "wages"** across all four codes, and
   the new statutory categories of **gig worker** and **platform worker**
4. **The new numbers** — thresholds, time limits, contribution rates

**That is four facts per code, not four hundred.** Learn those and the codes are covered to the depth this
paper requires.

::: think | THINK LIKE UPSC · THE THREE SHAPES IN THIS BANK
1. **The plausible institution.** Every wrong option in §6.1 is a real body that publishes real reports.

2. **Enactment against commencement.** Q19. A statute has a year of enactment and a date of coming into

3. **The discontinued or superseded institution.** Q8's Planning Commission, Q16's Ease of Doing Business,

   distractor**, because familiarity outlives existence
