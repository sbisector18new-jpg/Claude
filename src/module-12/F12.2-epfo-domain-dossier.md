## 1. The Post — Answer This One Perfectly

::: must-master | MUST MASTER · WHAT AN ASSISTANT PROVIDENT FUND COMMISSIONER ACTUALLY DOES
An APFC is a **statutory officer of the Employees' Provident Fund Organisation** with **quasi-judicial**
and **recovery** powers. It is not a clerical or purely administrative post, and the board will want to
know that you understand that distinction.

The work divides into five things.

| Function | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Compliance and coverage** | Identifying establishments liable to be covered, bringing them within the fold, and ensuring returns and contributions are filed |
| **Quasi-judicial inquiry** | Determining the moneys due from an employer, on evidence, by a reasoned and appealable order. This is adjudication, and it must satisfy natural justice |
| **Recovery** | Enforcing determined dues. APFCs have been notified as **Recovery Officers** under Chapter III of the Code on Social Security |
| **Benefit administration** | Settlement of claims — provident fund withdrawals, pension, and the death-linked insurance benefit — within service standards |
| **Grievance redressal and outreach** | Handling member and employer grievances, and the employer-facing work of persuading rather than only enforcing |
:::

::: know | KNOW · THE FRAME, FORTY SECONDS
*"It is a statutory post, sir, with two powers that define it. First it is **quasi-judicial** — an
Assistant Provident Fund Commissioner determines what an employer owes, on evidence, by a reasoned order
that is appealable, so the duty is to apply the law to facts fairly rather than to maximise recovery.
Second it is a **recovery** function, and APFCs have been notified as Recovery Officers under Chapter III
of the Code on Social Security. Around those two sit coverage and compliance work, claim settlement against
service standards, and grievance redressal. The part I find most interesting is that the same officer both
enforces against an employer and serves the member, and those two roles need different temperaments."*

**Why that last sentence earns its place:** it is a genuine observation about the job rather than a
description of it, and it invites a follow-up you can answer.
:::

### 1.1 The follow-ups this invites

**"What does quasi-judicial mean?"** — That the officer exercises a power to decide rights between parties,
must follow the principles of natural justice, must give reasons, and is subject to appeal; but is not a
court and is not bound by the full rigour of the Evidence Act.

**"What would you do if an employer simply does not pay?"** — Establish liability by a proper inquiry
first; then interest and damages for the period of default; then recovery, including as an arrear of land
revenue, with attachment as the last step. **Frame it as a ladder, and say that persuasion and facilitation
come before coercion**, because most default is cash-flow rather than fraud — and because a board wants to
hear proportionality.

**"Is your job to protect the worker or to regulate the employer?"** — A genuine tension, and the honest
answer is that the statutory duty runs to the **member**, but the practical route to it runs through a
functioning employer. An establishment driven under does not pay arrears. Say both.

**"Would you be comfortable passing an order against a large company?"** — Yes, and the reason is that the
order is reasoned and appealable. That is what makes the power exercisable without either fear or
arbitrariness. Do not perform toughness; describe the safeguard.

## 2. The Organisation

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| **EPFO** | A **statutory** body under the Ministry of Labour and Employment. **Not constitutional** — F11.4 T-12 |
| **Central Board of Trustees** | The apex **tripartite** body: government, employers and employees |
| Chairperson of the Board | The **Union Minister for Labour and Employment** |
| Vice-chairperson | The Minister of State for Labour and Employment |
| Member-Secretary | The **Central Provident Fund Commissioner**, ex officio |
| Field structure | Zonal offices under Additional Central PF Commissioners, then Regional Offices, then District Offices |
| Interest rate | Recommended by the Board, and requires the concurrence of the **Ministry of Finance** before notification |
| Sister organisation | **ESIC**, also statutory, also chaired by the Union Labour Minister, administering health insurance |

::: trap | TRAP · THREE THINGS BOARDS PROBE HERE
**1 "Who declares the interest rate?"** The Board **recommends**; the Government, through the Ministry of
Finance, **concurs** before notification. Candidates say the Board decides. It does not, finally.

**2 "What does tripartite mean, and why does it matter?"** Not merely that three parties sit on the Board,
but *why*: contributions come from employers and members, so a body governing their money has both
represented alongside government. If asked whether it works, the honest answer notes that tripartism gives
legitimacy and slows decisions, and that both are real.

**3 "Is EPFO a regulator?"** Careful. EPFO **administers** schemes and **enforces** compliance; it is not a
sectoral regulator like SEBI or IRDAI, and it is **not** regulated by PFRDA, which governs the National
Pension System. That last distinction is a favourite.
:::

### 2.1 EPFO against the alternatives

A board may test whether you understand your organisation's place in the system.

| Comparison | The discriminating line |
|---|---|
| **EPFO and ESIC** | Both statutory, both tripartite, both under the Labour Ministry. EPFO does **retirement savings, pension and death-linked insurance**; ESIC does **health insurance and medical care**. Different contingencies, different thresholds — 20 employees against 10, ₹15,000 against ₹21,000 |
| **EPS and the National Pension System** | EPS is a **defined-benefit**, employment-linked, mandatory scheme for covered employees. NPS is **defined-contribution**, market-linked, individual-account, and regulated by PFRDA. The risk sits with the fund in the first and with the subscriber in the second |
| **EPFO and PFRDA** | Different statutes, different schemes. PFRDA does **not** regulate EPFO's schemes |
| **Contributory and non-contributory security** | EPFO is **contributory** — an earned entitlement from wages. Schemes like PM-JAY or the old-age pension components are largely **non-contributory and tax-financed**. The distinction matters for who can be covered |

## 3. The Law and the Scheme Architecture

### 3.1 The instrument position, said correctly

::: know | KNOW · THE FRAME, THIRTY SECONDS, AND THE HIGHEST-VALUE THIRTY SECONDS IN THE INTERVIEW
*"The governing law is now the **Code on Social Security, 2020**, which came into force with the other three
Codes on **21 November 2025** and subsumed nine enactments including the 1952 Act. Under it, three schemes
were notified on **29 June 2026** — the Employees' Provident Funds Scheme 2026, the Employees' Pension
Scheme 2026, and the Employees' Deposit Linked Insurance Scheme 2026 — replacing the 1952, 1995 and 1976
schemes respectively, and the 2026 pension scheme also replaced the Family Pension Scheme of 1971. The
important point is that **most of the parameters were carried over** — the contribution rates, the pension
formula, the ₹15,000 ceiling and the ₹1,000 minimum pension are unchanged — so the change is one of
instrument rather than of substance."*

**That final sentence is the one that distinguishes you.** It shows you know not merely *that* the law
changed but *what* changed, which is the question a board actually cares about.
:::

### 3.2 The three schemes

| Scheme | Funded by | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Provident Fund** | Employee 12%, employer 3.67% | Lump-sum retirement savings with interest, and permitted advances |
| **Pension** | 8.33% diverted from the employer, plus **1.16%** from the Central Government | Monthly pension on superannuation, and survivor benefits |
| **Deposit Linked Insurance** | **Employer alone, 0.50%** | Lump-sum assurance on death in service, at no cost to the member |

**The point worth making aloud:** the member contributes **only** to the provident fund. Pension is funded
by diversion from the employer's share plus a government contribution, and insurance is funded wholly by
the employer. *"The member receives three benefits and pays into one"* is a sentence a board will remember.

### 3.3 The APFC's powers, and where they now come from

::: important | IMPORTANT · SAY *CHAPTER III OF THE CODE*, NOT *SECTION 7A*
Under the repealed 1952 Act, determination of dues sat in **Section 7A**, interest in **7Q**, damages in
**14B**, and recovery in **8** and **8B to 8G**. Those numbers are worth knowing, as history and because
they still appear in older material.

But the operative position, per **F10.3 §1.3**, is that **EPFO officers including APFCs have been notified
under Chapter III of the Code on Social Security and appointed Recovery Officers**, so present powers of
inquiry and recovery flow from **the Code**.

**In the room:** describe the **function** — determination, interest, damages, recovery — and attribute it
to the **Code**. If pressed for the old section numbers, give them and say they belonged to the 1952 Act.
That sequence demonstrates currency and depth at once, and it is the opposite of the error F11.4 §5 warns
about.
:::

The functional ladder, which is the answer to *what happens when an employer defaults*:

1. **Determination** of the amount due, by inquiry, on evidence, by a reasoned and appealable order
2. **Interest** for the period of delay — compensatory, and not discretionary
3. **Damages** for default — penal, and **graded by how long the default ran**
4. **Recovery** — including as an arrear of land revenue, through the Recovery Officer, with attachment
5. **Prosecution**, in the appropriate case

**And the protections that sit around the fund**, which are worth naming because they show you understand
whose money it is: the accumulations are **protected from attachment** by the member's creditors, provident
fund dues have **priority** over other debts of the employer, and an employer may **not reduce wages** to
offset the liability.

## 4. The Numbers

::: know | KNOW · THE FIGURES A BOARD MAY REASONABLY EXPECT
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Coverage threshold | **20** or more persons |
| Wage ceiling | **₹15,000** — a rise to ₹25,000 is **approved but not notified** |
| Contribution | **12%** employee and **12%** employer |
| Employer's split | **8.33%** pension, **3.67%** provident fund |
| Central Government share in pension | **1.16%** of wages |
| Deposit-linked insurance | **0.50%**, employer only; maximum assurance **₹7 lakh** |
| Minimum monthly pension | **₹1,000** |
| Superannuation age | **58**; reduced pension from **50** |
| Minimum service for pension | **10** years of eligible service |
| Interest rate, 2025-26 | **8.25%**, a third consecutive year |
| Claim settlement standard | **20 days** under the 2026 pension scheme, with **12%** interest where EPFO delays |
| ESI, for contrast | **10** employees, **₹21,000** ceiling, **3.25%** and **0.75%** |
:::

::: trap | TRAP · THE FOUR FIGURES TO HANDLE WITH CARE
**₹25,000.** Approved, **not notified**. Say so explicitly — it is a chance to demonstrate the
*proposed / approved / notified* discipline from F10.2 §2.2, which is exactly the discrimination a
statutory officer is paid for.

**The insurance minimum.** Figures of ₹2 lakh and ₹2.5 lakh both circulate because they belong to different
notifications. **Give the maximum of ₹7 lakh, which is stable, and decline the minimum** unless you have
verified it that week.

**The interest rate.** Declared annually. Verify before you sit.

**Anything about numbers of subscribers, claims settled or corpus size.** These move constantly. Give an
order of magnitude if you must, flag it as approximate, or decline. **Never a precise figure you have not
verified.**
:::

## 5. Policy — Where the Marks Are

Factual questions establish competence. **Policy questions distinguish candidates**, because they reveal
whether you understand what the organisation is *for*. Each item below gives the tension and a position.
F12.3 §1 develops the frame; here is the substance.

### 5.1 The central problem: coverage

::: know | KNOW · THE ONE STRUCTURAL FACT THAT FRAMES EVERYTHING
The overwhelming majority of India's workforce is **informal**, and formal social security historically
attached to the **registered enterprise** rather than to the worker — because the enterprise is where a
contribution could be **deducted and enforced**.

The consequence is uncomfortable and worth stating: **the schemes reached the workers who needed them least
intensely and missed those who needed them most.**

**The frame:** *"The binding constraint has never been generosity, sir, it has been collectibility. Extending
an entitlement to a worker with no registered employer extends nothing until somebody can identify the
worker, identify who is liable, and collect from them — and each of those is an administrative capability
rather than a legal one. That is why I think worker-level registration and the aggregator obligation matter
more than the entitlement language."*
:::

### 5.2 The contested questions, with positions

| Question | The tension, and a defensible position |
|---|---|
| **Are the labour Codes good for workers?** | They raise thresholds for standing orders and retrenchment permission from 100 to 300, which reduces protection for workers in mid-sized firms; and they extend social security to gig and platform workers, which expands it for millions previously outside. **Both are true.** A defensible position: the Codes trade **depth of protection for a minority** against **breadth of coverage for a majority**, and whether that was worth it depends on enforcement, which is not yet visible |
| **Why has India not ratified Conventions 87 and 98?** | Freedom of association and collective bargaining. The stated difficulties concern government servants and existing restrictions. It sits at the centre of trade union objections to the IR Code. **Describe the debate; do not take a partisan side** |
| **Should the wage ceiling be raised to ₹25,000?** | For: it extends coverage and raises contributions and eventual pensions. Against: it raises employer cost, may push marginal firms towards informality, and increases EPFO's liability. Note it is **approved but not notified** |
| **Is ₹1,000 an adequate minimum pension?** | Plainly low against any cost-of-living measure. But the scheme is **contributory and defined-benefit**, so raising the floor without raising contributions transfers the cost to the corpus or the taxpayer. **Name the trade-off rather than the grievance** |
| **Gig workers — will the aggregator model work?** | The obligation is on the aggregator, expressed as one to two per cent of turnover subject to a cap. The design is sound because it puts liability where the payment record exists. The risk is classification disputes and platforms restructuring to avoid the definition |
| **Should EPFO funds be invested in equity?** | Higher long-run returns against higher volatility, on money that is a member's retirement security and carries an assured-return expectation. A measured position: a **capped** equity allocation is defensible; the difficulty is that members bear the downside without having chosen the risk |
| **EPS or NPS — which is better?** | Not a better-or-worse question but a **risk-allocation** question. Defined benefit puts investment risk on the scheme; defined contribution puts it on the subscriber. Say that, and you have answered better than a preference would |
| **Universal social security — is it achievable?** | The Code's architecture points that way. The obstacles are administrative and fiscal rather than legal: identification, portability across States and employers, and who contributes for a worker with no employer |
| **Higher pension on actual salary** | The litigation was resolved with a further limited window for eligible members to exercise the joint option. **Give the shape and resist any clean absolute** — the actual position is heavily qualified, and a board will know it |
| **The India-UK Double Contributions Convention** | In force **15 July 2026**, a **60-month** detachment exemption, coordinating **contributions and not benefits**, and **EPFO administers it**. Good evidence of currency, and an opening to social security agreements generally |

### 5.3 EPFO's own reform agenda

Worth having ready, because it is the answer to *what would you change*:

**EPFO 3.0** — UPI and ATM withdrawal, auto-settlement raised to **₹5 lakh**, up to **75%** of balance by
UPI, thirteen withdrawal categories reduced to **three**, automated transfers on changing jobs, and a stated
aim of **24 to 48 hour** settlement.

::: action | ACTION · THE ANSWER TO *WHAT WOULD YOU IMPROVE*
Do not propose a change of law; you are applying for an administrative post, and boards mark down
candidates who legislate.

**Propose something in your own gift.** Three defensible answers:

1. **Reduce the inquiry-to-recovery cycle time.** Determination is quasi-judicial and cannot be rushed, but
   listing, adjournment discipline and reasoned orders written promptly are within the officer's control
2. **Employer facilitation before enforcement**, especially for small establishments crossing the threshold
   for the first time, where non-compliance is usually ignorance rather than evasion
3. **Grievance closure quality rather than volume** — the useful measure is whether the member's problem
   ended, not whether the ticket did

Each is modest, administrative and within the post. That is why each works.
:::
