NOTE · THIS CHAPTER CLOSES THE 2023 SCIENCE LIST

The last three of the ten 2023 General Science items are here: the layers of the eyeball (§2.4), banyan prop roots (§4.2) and ocean acidification (§5.3).

Unlike physics, biology is not recall for a Mechanical Engineer — it is closer to genuine new learning, and it is fact-dense rather than formula-based. So this chapter is built as tables of facts organised by system, with the three PYQ-evidenced sections given the fullest treatment.

Note also what the 2023 selection reveals: two of the three biology items were plant morphology and environmental chemistry, not human physiology. Candidates who study only the human body are aiming at the wrong third of the subject.

1. Session 1 — The Cell

The cell is the structural and functional unit of life. It was discovered by Robert Hooke in 1665, observing cork.

Prokaryotic cells have no true nucleus and no membrane-bound organelles — bacteria. Eukaryotic cells have both — plants, animals, fungi.

MUST MASTER · PLANT VERSUS ANIMAL CELL, AND THE TWO DIVISIONS

A plant cell HAS a cell wall of cellulose, chloroplasts, and a large central vacuole. An animal cell has none of these but does have a centrosome.

The reliable wrong option reverses the cell wall — "a plant cell lacks a cell wall" is false, and it is offered because the animal cell is the one that lacks it.

A normal human somatic cell has 46 chromosomes — 23 pairs. Sex chromosomes: XX female, XY male. DNA's double-helix structure was described by Watson and Crick.

2. Session 2 — Human Body Systems

2.1 Digestion and circulation

Circulation. The heart has four chambers — two atria and two ventricles.

MUST MASTER · ARTERIES, VEINS AND THE TWO EXCEPTIONS

Arteries carry blood AWAY from the heart; veins carry blood TO the heart. That is the definition — direction, not oxygen content.

Oxygen content follows as a general rule with two exceptions:

  • The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood, from the heart to the lungs
  • The pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood, from the lungs to the heart

So a question asking which artery carries deoxygenated blood is answered by the pulmonary artery, and a statement that "all arteries carry oxygenated blood" is wrong.

Blood: RBC contain haemoglobin and live about 120 days; WBC provide immunity; platelets enable clotting. O negative is the universal donor; AB positive the universal recipient.

2.2 Excretion, nerves and hormones

  • Kidney — the functional unit is the nephron; the nitrogenous waste is urea
  • Brain — the cerebrum is the largest part and governs thinking; the cerebellum governs

balance and coordination; the medulla oblongata controls involuntary functions such as heartbeat and breathing. Reflex action is mediated by the spinal cord, not the brain

An adult human has 206 bones. The femur is the largest; the stapes, in the ear, the smallest.

2.3 The ear

Outer, middle and inner. The middle ear contains three bones — malleus, incus and stapes. In the inner ear the cochlea serves hearing and the semicircular canals serve balance.

2.4 The eye — the 2023 item

MUST MASTER · THE THREE LAYERS OF THE EYEBALL, OUTSIDE INWARDS

This is a 2023 question. The wall of the eyeball has three coats, and the order matters:

OrderLayerNature
1 — outermostScleraTough, white, protective. Its transparent front part is the cornea
2 — middleChoroidVascular and pigmented; supplies blood and prevents internal reflection
3 — innermostRetinaLight-sensitive, carrying the rods and cones

Mnemonic for the order: S-C-R, outside in — Sclera, Choroid, Retina. Alphabetical, which makes it unusually easy.

The trap is any option placing the choroid innermost or the retina outermost.

Inside the retina: rods give vision in dim light, in black and white; cones give colour vision in bright light. The blind spot is where the optic nerve leaves and has neither.

Other parts: the iris controls the size of the pupil and hence the light admitted; the lens focuses; the aqueous and vitreous humours fill the chambers. Defects of vision and their corrections are in F9.2 §1.5.

4. Session 4 — Plants

4.1 Photosynthesis and transport

Photosynthesis occurs in the chloroplast using chlorophyll and sunlight: carbon dioxide and water become glucose and oxygen. It is endothermic — it absorbs energy, which is F9.3 §5.1.

Transport: xylem carries water and minerals upwards; phloem carries food, and it moves in both directions.

4.2 Modified roots — the 2023 item

MUST MASTER · THE FOUR MODIFIED ROOTS, WITH THEIR PLANTS

This is a 2023 question, and the four are constantly interchanged.

ModificationPurposePlant
Prop rootsAerial roots growing downwards from branches to support heavy spreading branchesBANYAN
Stilt rootsGrowing obliquely from the lower stem into the soil for supportMaize, sugarcane, screwpine
Pneumatophores (respiratory roots)Growing upwards out of waterlogged soil for gas exchangeMANGROVES
Storage rootsStoring foodCarrot, sweet potato, radish

The distinction that decides the question is direction:

  • Prop roots grow DOWN, from the branches — banyan
  • Stilt roots grow DOWN and outward, from the lower stem — sugarcane
  • Pneumatophores grow UP, out of the mud — mangrove

So "aerial roots hanging from the branches of a large tree" is unambiguously the banyan, and "roots growing vertically upward from waterlogged soil" is the mangrove.

Modified stems, for contrast, since they are offered as distractors: potato is a tuber, ginger a rhizome, onion a bulb.

Nitrogen fixation: leguminous plants carry root nodules containing Rhizobium.

5. Session 5 — Ecology and Environment

5.1 Ecosystem structure

An ecosystem has biotic components — producers, consumers and decomposers — and abiotic components.

MUST MASTER · THE TEN PER CENT LAW AND UNIDIRECTIONAL FLOW

Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level passes to the next — Lindeman's ten per cent law. The rest is lost as heat and in metabolism.

Two consequences that are examined:

  1. Energy flow through an ecosystem is UNIDIRECTIONAL, not cyclic. Nutrients cycle; energy does not. An option saying energy flow is bidirectional or cyclic is wrong
  2. Food chains are therefore short — usually four or five links — because so little energy survives

Biomagnification is the increasing concentration of non-biodegradable substances such as DDT and mercury at successively higher trophic levels. The word to check in the option is non-biodegradable — biodegradable substances do not biomagnify.

5.2 Pollution

TypeKey indicators and facts
AirPM2.5 and PM10, sulphur dioxide, oxides of nitrogen, carbon monoxide
WaterBOD — biochemical oxygen demand. Higher BOD means more organic pollution
EutrophicationNutrient enrichment by nitrates and phosphates » algal bloom » oxygen depletion » fish die

Note the link back to F9.3 §4.1: phosphates from detergents are a classic contributor to eutrophication, which is part of why detergent biodegradability matters.

Acid rain comes from oxides of sulphur and nitrogen forming sulphuric and nitric acids.

5.3 Ocean acidification — the 2023 item

MUST MASTER · WHAT ACIDIFICATION MEANS, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT

The ocean absorbs a large share of the carbon dioxide humans emit. Dissolved carbon dioxide forms carbonic acid, which releases hydrogen ions and lowers the pH of seawater.

Consequence: less carbonate is available for shell and skeleton formation, so the organisms harmed are the calcifying ones — corals, molluscs and shelled plankton. Coral bleaching and reef weakening follow.

And now the point the exam is most likely to test. Ocean pH has fallen from roughly 8.2 to about 8.1 since pre-industrial times. So:

Seawater is still ALKALINE. "Acidification" means moving TOWARDS acidic, not having become acidic.

Any statement that the ocean has become acidic, or that its pH is below 7, is wrong — and it is the intuitive reading of the word. The cause is carbon dioxide, not sulphur dioxide, which is the agent of acid rain.

5.4 Climate, ozone and the conventions

Greenhouse gases: carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, water vapour, CFCs. Methane has a higher global warming potential per molecule than carbon dioxide.

Ozone. Stratospheric ozone absorbs ultraviolet radiation and is beneficial; tropospheric ozone is a pollutant. Depletion is caused by CFCs, and the ozone hole appears over Antarctica.

AgreementYearSubject
Ramsar Convention1971Wetlands
CITES1973Trade in endangered species
Montreal Protocol1987Ozone-depleting substances
UNFCCC and CBD1992Climate change; biological diversity
Kyoto Protocol1997Emission reduction commitments
Paris Agreement2015Climate change

COMMON UPSC TRAP · MONTREAL VERSUS KYOTO VERSUS PARIS

Three protocols, three different subjects, routinely swapped:

  • Montreal 1987 » OZONE
  • Kyoto 1997 » climate, binding emission targets
  • Paris 2015 » climate, nationally determined contributions

Montreal is the ozone one. If the stem mentions the ozone layer or CFCs and the options include Kyoto or Paris, they are there to catch you. And Ramsar is wetlands, not forests.

India: Project Tiger began in 1973, Project Elephant in 1992. Protected areas comprise national parks, wildlife sanctuaries and biosphere reserves.

THINK LIKE UPSC · THE FOUR SHAPES IN THIS CHAPTER

  1. The ordered list. Q11 and Q21 both test the sclera–choroid–retina order. Any three-layer structure invites a scrambled-order question, so learn the sequence with its direction
  2. The word taken literally. Q22. "Acidification" sounds like "has become acidic". It means moving towards acidic. Where a term implies a state, check whether it actually describes a trend
  3. The vector-versus-organism swap. Q7 and Q26. Malaria and dengue share mosquito transmission but differ completely in causative organism — protozoan versus virus
  4. The reversed possession. Q25's statement 3 and Q28's option (a). The examiner takes a true fact about one thing and attaches it to its opposite. Cell wall belongs to the plant; unidirectional flow to energy

ONE LINE FOR THE INTERVIEW Ocean acidification is a good example of a term that misleads by sounding absolute: seawater is still alkaline at about pH 8.1, but the shift of roughly 0.1 units since pre-industrial times represents about a thirty per cent rise in hydrogen ion concentration, which is already enough to impair shell formation in calcifying organisms.
