1. Session 1 — Atomic Structure and the Mole

ParticleChargeLocation
ProtonPositiveNucleus
NeutronNeutralNucleus
ElectronNegativeShells around the nucleus
  • Atomic number (Z) = number of protons. It defines the element
  • Mass number (A) = protons + neutrons
  • Valency — the combining capacity, determined by the outermost shell and the octet rule

MUST MASTER · ISOTOPES, ISOBARS AND THE MOLE

TermSameDifferent
IsotopesAtomic number (protons)Mass number (neutrons)
IsobarsMass numberAtomic number
IsotonesNumber of neutronsBoth Z and A

Isotopes have the same chemical properties (same electron arrangement) but different physical properties. Examples: protium, deuterium and tritium; carbon-12 and carbon-14; uranium-235 and uranium-238.

One mole contains 6.022 × 10²³ particles — Avogadro's number. That figure is asked directly, and its usual distractors are the speed of light (3 × 10⁸) and the electronic charge (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹).

2. Session 2 — Periodic Table and Bonding

2.1 The periodic table

MUST MASTER · THE LAW, AND THE GROUP NAMES

The modern periodic law: the properties of elements are a periodic function of their ATOMIC NUMBER. Mendeleev's earlier law used atomic mass — that swap is the standard question.

There are 18 groups and 7 periods. The group names that get asked:

GroupName
1Alkali metals
2Alkaline earth metals
17Halogens
18Noble or inert gases

The commonest error is calling group 18 the halogens. 17 halogens, 18 noble gases.

Trends. Across a period, left to right: atomic size decreases, electronegativity increases, metallic character decreases. Down a group: atomic size increases, metallic character increases, electronegativity decreases.

3. Session 3 — Acids, Bases, Salts and pH

A 2023 topic. Read this section carefully.

3.1 Acids and bases

AcidBase

LitmusTurns blue litmus redTurns red litmus blue

ExamplesHCl, H₂SO₄, citric, aceticNaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)₂

Neutralisation: acid + base » salt + water, and it is exothermic.

Indicators: litmus; methyl orange; phenolphthalein — colourless in acid, PINK in base; universal indicator for the whole range.

3.2 The pH scale

MUST MASTER · THE SCALE, AND THE FACTOR OF TEN

The pH scale runs from 0 to 14. pH = −log[H⁺].

  • pH 7 — neutral (pure water at 25 °C)
  • Below 7 — acidic. The lower the number, the stronger the acid
  • Above 7 — basic or alkaline

Each unit of pH is a factor of TEN in hydrogen ion concentration. So a solution of pH 4 has 100 times the hydrogen ion concentration of one at pH 6 — two units, so 10², not two times. That multiplication is a favourite numerical.

Values worth carrying:

SubstancepH
Gastric juiceabout 1.5 to 3.5
Pure water7
Human bloodabout 7.4 — slightly alkaline, and tightly regulated
Milkabout 6.6

3.3 Effluents and pH — the 2023 item

MUST MASTER · THE DISCHARGE STANDARD

Under the general standards for the discharge of industrial effluents into inland surface water, the permissible pH range is about 5.5 to 9.0.

The logic is worth holding rather than the number alone: discharge is permitted across a band either side of neutral, not at exactly 7, because requiring precise neutrality would be unattainable — but strongly acidic or strongly alkaline effluent destroys aquatic life and corrodes infrastructure.

So the wrong options are "exactly 7.0", and any band lying wholly on one side of neutral such as 1 to 5 or 9 to 12. The correct answer straddles 7.

Related environmental chemistry, which connects to F9.4: acid rain is caused by oxides of sulphur and nitrogen forming sulphuric and nitric acids; ocean acidification is caused by dissolved carbon dioxide forming carbonic acid.

4. Session 4 — Soaps, Detergents and Everyday Substances

A 2023 topic.

4.1 Soaps and detergents

MUST MASTER · THE DEFINITIONS, AND THE HARD-WATER DIFFERENCE

Soap is the sodium or potassium salt of a long-chain fatty acid. It is made by saponification — the hydrolysis of fats or oils with an alkali such as sodium hydroxide.

A detergent is the sodium salt of a long-chain benzene sulphonic acid or sulphate.

Now the difference that generates the question:

SoapDetergent
Works in hard water?No — forms an insoluble scumYes
WhyIts calcium and magnesium salts are insolubleIts calcium and magnesium salts are soluble
BiodegradabilityBiodegradableOften less biodegradable — branched chains cause foaming in rivers

Note the direction carefully, because reversing it is the standard wrong option: it is SOAP that fails in hard water, not the detergent. Detergents were developed precisely to solve that problem — and the environmental cost was reduced biodegradability.

How soap cleans — the micelle. A soap molecule has a hydrophilic (water-loving) ionic head and a hydrophobic (water-repelling) hydrocarbon tail. In water the molecules cluster into a micelle with the tails pointing inwards, trapping oil and grease in the centre while the heads face the water — so the dirt is carried away in suspension.

5. Session 5 — Enthalpy and Types of Reaction

A 2023 topic.

5.1 Exothermic and endothermic

MUST MASTER · THE SIGN OF ΔH, AND THE TWO LISTS

ExothermicEndothermic
HeatReleasedAbsorbed
ΔHNegativePositive
SurroundingsWarm upCool down

The sign is the trap. Heat coming out of the system makes the enthalpy change negative, which feels backwards to beginners — you are measuring the system's enthalpy, not the room's. An option saying an endothermic reaction has a negative ΔH is wrong.

Exothermic: combustion of fuels · respiration · neutralisation of an acid by a base · quicklime added to water · rusting (slow).

Endothermic: photosynthesis · thermal decomposition, such as calcium carbonate to calcium oxide and carbon dioxide · dissolving ammonium chloride in water · electrolysis.

The clean pair to remember: respiration is exothermic, photosynthesis is endothermic. They are the reverse of one another, which is why they carry opposite signs.

5.2 Types of reaction

TypeDescription
CombinationTwo or more reactants form one product
DecompositionOne reactant splits — thermal, electrolytic or photochemical
DisplacementA more reactive element displaces a less reactive one
Double displacementIons are exchanged; includes precipitation and neutralisation
RedoxOxidation and reduction occur together

MUST MASTER · OIL RIG

Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain — of ELECTRONS.

OxidationReduction
ElectronsLostGained
OxygenGainedLost
HydrogenLostGained

The modern definition is in terms of electrons, which is what "best defined as" questions are testing. The oxygen definition is older and narrower.

Corrosion: rusting of iron requires both oxygen AND moisture — either alone will not do, which is why an option naming only one is wrong. Prevented by painting, oiling, galvanisation (zinc coating) and alloying.

Rancidity: oxidation of fats and oils, retarded by antioxidants and by flushing packaging with nitrogen.

6.2 Carbon

Carbon shows tetravalency and catenation — it forms four bonds and long chains, which is why organic chemistry exists.

AllotropeProperty
DiamondHardest natural substance; does not conduct electricity
GraphiteConducts electricity; soft; used as a lubricant and in pencils
FullereneCage-like molecules, such as C-60

Hydrocarbons: alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (saturated) · alkenes CₙH₂ₙ · alkynes CₙH₂ₙ₋₂.

Functional groups: alcohol −OH · aldehyde −CHO · carboxylic acid −COOH.

COMMON UPSC TRAP · DIAMOND AND GRAPHITE

Both are pure carbon, and the exam relies on you knowing which property belongs to which.

Graphite conducts electricity; diamond does not. Graphite has delocalised electrons between its layers; diamond's electrons are all locked into covalent bonds. And diamond is the hardest natural substance while graphite is soft enough to write with.

Same element, opposite properties — because the difference is structure, not composition.

THINK LIKE UPSC · THE FOUR SHAPES IN THIS CHAPTER

  1. The reversal. Q21 and Q28 both work by swapping which of a pair has a property — soap versus detergent, metal versus non-metal. Whenever two things are contrasted, check the direction
  2. The logarithm. Q24. pH differences are powers of ten, never arithmetic. Two units means 100 times
  3. The sign of ΔH. Q7 and Q22. Exothermic is negative, which feels backwards until you remember you are measuring the system, not the room
  4. The concentration qualifier. Q20 offers the right acids at the wrong concentration. Read the adjectives in chemistry options, not just the formulas
