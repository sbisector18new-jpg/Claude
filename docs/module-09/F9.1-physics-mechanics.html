1.3 Scalars and vectors

Scalars have magnitude only: mass, speed, distance, work, energy, power, time, temperature, density, pressure, electric charge.

Vectors have magnitude and direction: displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum, weight, impulse, torque.

MUST MASTER · THE THREE PAIRS THE EXAM USES

  1. Work and energy are SCALARS, even though work is defined using force, which is a vector. The dot product of two vectors is a scalar. This is the single most common wrong option in the topic
  2. Speed is scalar, velocity is vector. Likewise distance scalar, displacement vector
  3. Mass is a scalar and is constant. Weight is a vector and varies with location, because W = mg and g varies

Pressure is also a scalar, which surprises people, because although force has direction, pressure at a point acts equally in all directions.

MUST MASTER · THE UNIT CONVERSION THAT DECIDES NUMERICALS

To convert km/h into m/s, multiply by 5/18. To go the other way, multiply by 18/5.

So 72 km/h = 20 m/s, and 36 km/h = 10 m/s. Nearly every kinematics numerical in this examination is set in km/h and must be solved in SI, and the 2023 paper's question on the uniform acceleration of a train is exactly this shape.

Do the conversion first, before you choose an equation. The commonest error is not the physics — it is solving correctly in the wrong units.

Worked, in the 2023 style: a train starting from rest reaches 72 km/h in 20 s under uniform acceleration. 72 km/h = 72 × 5/18 = 20 m s⁻¹. Then a = (v − u)/t = (20 − 0)/20 = 1 m s⁻²

3. Session 3 — Laws of Motion and Collisions

3.1 Newton's three laws

LawStatementWhat it gives us
FirstA body continues at rest or in uniform motion unless acted on by an external forceDefines inertia, and defines force qualitatively
SecondForce equals the rate of change of momentum; F = ma when mass is constantDefines force quantitatively, and gives the newton
ThirdTo every action there is an equal and opposite reactionPairs of forces, and conservation of momentum

MUST MASTER · TWO POINTS ABOUT THE THIRD AND SECOND LAWS

  1. Action and reaction act on DIFFERENT bodies, and therefore never cancel each other. A statement that they cancel, or that they act on the same body, is wrong. This is the single most examinable subtlety in the laws of motion
  2. The general form of the second law is F = dp/dt, force equals the rate of change of momentum. F = ma is the special case where mass is constant. So an option saying the second law applies only when mass is constant is wrong — it is F = ma that carries that restriction, not the law

Three kinds of inertia are sometimes asked by name: inertia of rest, of motion, and of direction.

Momentum p = mv, a vector, unit kg m s⁻¹. Impulse = F × t = change in momentum, unit N s.

3.2 Collisions — the 2023 pattern

MUST MASTER · MOMENTUM IS CONSERVED IN EVERY COLLISION

Linear momentum is conserved in all collisions. Kinetic energy is conserved only in an elastic collision.

That one sentence answers most collision questions, and the 2023 paper's inelastic-collision item is built on it. The trap is an option asserting that momentum is not conserved in an inelastic collision — it always is, because conservation of momentum follows from Newton's third law and holds regardless of whether energy is lost to heat, sound or deformation.

Coefficient of restitution e = (relative velocity of separation) / (relative velocity of approach). e = 1 perfectly elastic, e = 0 perfectly inelastic.

Worked, perfectly inelastic: 2 kg at 5 m s⁻¹ strikes a stationary 3 kg body and they move together. Momentum before = 2 × 5 = 10 kg m s⁻¹. After, (2 + 3) v = 10, so v = 2 m s⁻¹. Check the energy: before = ½ × 2 × 25 = 25 J; after = ½ × 5 × 4 = 10 J. 15 J lost — which is exactly why it is inelastic.

3.3 Circular motion

Centripetal force = mv²/r, directed towards the centre — it is the real force that keeps a body on a circular path.

Centrifugal force is a pseudo (fictitious) force, apparent only in a rotating frame, directed away from the centre. Calling it a real force is a standard wrong option.

4. Session 4 — Work, Energy and Power

4.1 Work

W = F s cos θ, where θ is the angle between force and displacement. Unit: joule. A scalar.

θcos θWork
0°1Maximum, positive
90°0Zero
180°−1Negative

MUST MASTER · WHEN WORK IS ZERO

Work is zero when the force is perpendicular to the displacement, because cos 90° = 0.

Three standard illustrations, each of which has appeared in one form or another:

  • Carrying a bucket while walking on level ground — the force is vertical, the displacement

horizontal, so the work done against gravity is zero

  • A satellite in a circular orbit — the gravitational force is towards the centre, the displacement

tangential, so gravity does no work

  • A coolie carrying a load on a level platform — the same situation as the bucket

Note the counter-intuitive framing: the man is certainly getting tired, but the physics definition of work is zero. The exam relies on you confusing effort with work.

5. Session 5 — Gravitation and Fluids

5.1 Gravitation

F = G m₁ m₂ / r² and g = GM/R²

FactDetail
g variesDecreases with altitude and with depth; zero at the centre of the Earth
g is maximum at the poles, minimum at the equatorBecause the Earth bulges at the equator and rotates
Escape velocity from EarthAbout 11.2 km s⁻¹
Orbital velocity near the surfaceAbout 7.9 km s⁻¹
RelationshipEscape velocity = sqrt2 × orbital velocity

MUST MASTER · WEIGHTLESSNESS IS NOT THE ABSENCE OF GRAVITY

An astronaut in an orbiting satellite feels weightless because the astronaut and the satellite are both in free fall — they accelerate towards the Earth at the same rate, so there is no contact force between them.

Gravity is very much present; indeed it is what keeps the satellite in orbit. The option "there is no gravity in space" is wrong, and it is the intuitive answer.

The state is properly called apparent weightlessness, and the same thing happens in a lift whose cable is cut.

5.2 Fluids — four principles

PrincipleStatementApplication
Pascal's lawPressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally in all directionsHydraulic press, hydraulic brakes, hydraulic lift
Archimedes' principleThe upthrust equals the weight of the fluid displacedFlotation, ships, submarines, hydrometer
Bernoulli's principleWhere the velocity of a fluid is high, its pressure is lowAerofoil lift, atomiser, spin of a ball, Venturi meter
Law of flotationA floating body displaces its own weight of fluidShips, icebergs

Pressure = Force / Area, unit pascal. Atmospheric pressure about 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa, measured by a barometer.

COMMON UPSC TRAP · WHICH PRINCIPLE EXPLAINS WHICH DEVICE

The three fluid principles are constantly swapped in matching questions. Fix them by the device:

  • Hydraulic brakes and presses » Pascal — transmission of pressure
  • Ships, submarines and hydrometers » Archimedes — upthrust
  • Aeroplane lift, atomiser, swinging cricket ball » Bernoulli — velocity and pressure

If the device multiplies force, it is Pascal. If it floats or sinks, it is Archimedes. If something moves through or past a fluid, it is Bernoulli.

THINK LIKE UPSC · THE FOUR SHAPES IN THIS CHAPTER

  1. The unit-conversion trap. Q21 offers 3.6 as an option precisely because 72 ÷ 20 = 3.6. Convert km/h to m/s before you touch an equation
  2. The scalar-vector reversal. Q2 and Q23 are the same fact. Work and energy are scalars; momentum and weight are vectors
  3. The slope-versus-area swap. Q15. Slope of a velocity–time graph is acceleration; area is displacement
  4. The intuitive-but-wrong explanation. Q16 and Q27. Weightlessness is not the absence of gravity, and carrying a load is not work. Where an option matches everyday intuition, check the definition
