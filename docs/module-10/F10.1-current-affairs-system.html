IMPORTANT · THE HONEST POSITION ON THIS MODULE, STATED FIRST

Module 10 is 20.0 planning marks over 12 hours — eight questions — and it is the only module in this manual that cannot be finished. Every other module is a closed body of material. This one acquires new content every week until the day of the paper.

So a chapter that is merely a list of facts is the wrong deliverable. A list compiled in August 2026 for a paper written in November or December 2026 is wrong about everything that happens in between, and worse, it is confidently wrong — the failure mode F4.1 §0 introduced the dated-figure convention to prevent.

What this chapter is instead: a collection system (§3), a verification protocol (§4), and a watchlist of the specific things this examination asks about (§5). It also contains the part of current affairs that does not decay — §2's institutional attributions, which are what "current affairs" questions in this examination overwhelmingly test.

F10.2 is the dossier: the facts as they stood on 16 August 2026, dated and sourced, to be re-verified against §4's checklist before the paper.

That division — the method here, the perishable content there, clearly separated — is the only honest way to put current affairs in a printed manual. It is the same principle as F8.0's calendar declaring that it schedules existing questions rather than pretending to contain new ones.

1. Session 1 — What Is Actually Being Tested

1.1 The three kinds of "current affairs" question

KindExampleDecays?
1 Institutional attributionWhich body publishes the Human Development Report?No — permanent

MUST MASTER · MOST OF THIS MODULE'S MARKS ARE IN KINDS 1 AND 2, NOT KIND 3

This is the judgement that decides how to spend the twelve hours.

Kind 3 — the current reading — is what candidates fear and what they spend their time on. It is also the least of the three in yield, because a paper set months before it is sat cannot safely ask for a number that moves weekly, and because the setter knows it.

Kind 1 is permanently askable and permanently learnable. Who publishes the World Development Report has been the same answer for forty years and will be the same answer in November. §2.2's table is therefore the highest-value page in this module, and it can be finished in an evening.

Kind 2 is the sweet spot — a definite change, recent enough that older material is wrong, stable enough to be a fair question. The four labour codes coming into force, GST's two slabs, the Income-tax Act 2025, the Employees' Pension Scheme 2026. These are what F10.2 is built around, and F4.3's opening callout shows the pattern.

So spend the twelve hours roughly: four on §2's attributions, six on F10.2's kind-2 changes, and two on the §4 sweep in the fortnight before the paper. Not twelve on chasing the news.

1.2 Where the marks actually sit for this examination

Blueprint §4.4's record of the 2023 paper shows current affairs appearing inside other modules rather than as a labelled block — a question on a labour statute's amendment reads as labour law; a question on the Budget reads as economy.

That is what "integrated" in the module's name means, and it has a practical consequence: a current affairs item is only worth learning if it attaches to something in the static syllabus. The attachment points, in order of yield for this post, are:

  1. Labour law and social security — Modules 2 and 3
  2. The economy — Module 4
  3. Polity and governance — Module 1
  4. Science and technology — Module 9

Sport, entertainment, obituaries and general trivia are not on that list. They are the bulk of a commercial current affairs magazine and close to zero of this paper.

The three traps in that table:

  1. World Bank against IMF. Development and Human Capital are World Bank; Outlook and Fiscal Monitor are IMF. The two are both Bretton Woods institutions in Washington, which is why they are offered for each other — F4.5 §4.3
  2. WEF against UNDP. The Gender Gap report is the World Economic Forum; the Gender Inequality Index is UNDP. Two gender indices, two different bodies, and the names are close enough to swap
  3. The private publishers. ASER is Pratham, not the government. The Global Hunger Index is two European NGOs, not the FAO. Candidates assume an official-sounding index must be official

And one discontinued report worth knowing: the World Bank stopped publishing the Ease of Doing Business rankings in 2021. A question asserting that it still does is wrong, and it is the sort of stale fact that survives in coaching material for years.

KNOW · THE ILO'S TRIPARTISM IS THE MOST EXAMINABLE THING ABOUT IT

The ILO is unique among UN specialised agencies in its tripartite structure: each member State's delegation comprises two government delegates, one employers' delegate and one workers' delegate, and the employer and worker delegates vote independently of their government.

That structure is mirrored in India's own Central Board of Trustees, which likewise seats government, employer and employee representatives — and it is the institutional expression of the idea that labour standards are negotiated rather than imposed.

For the interview: tripartism explains why labour law reform in India moves slowly and why the four labour codes took from 2019 to 2025 to come into force. It is a better answer than "bureaucratic delay".

Not on the list, deliberately: current affairs YouTube channels, daily quiz apps, and monthly compilation magazines. Blueprint §22 permits coaching material as supplementary; it should not be the primary channel, because it optimises for volume of items rather than for the attachment points §1.2 identifies.

3.2 The daily habit — fifteen minutes

MUST MASTER · ONE PAGE A MONTH, IN FOUR COLUMNS

The failure mode in current affairs is not insufficient reading. It is reading without recording, which produces a sense of familiarity and no retrievable knowledge.

The format — one sheet per month, four columns:

DateItemWhich module it attaches toWhy it might be asked
21 Nov 2025Four labour codes brought into forceM2, M3Replaces 29 Acts; makes older answers wrong
22 Sep 2025GST to two slabs, 5% and 18%M4Definite, recent, contradicts every textbook
29 Jun 2026EPS 2026 replaces EPS-95M3Directly on this post's subject matter

The third column is the one that does the work. An item that cannot be attached to a module is an item to discard — that is the operational meaning of "integrated", and it is what keeps the register to one page a month instead of ten.

Fifteen minutes a day: ten reading PIB and the paper, five recording. Any item you cannot summarise in one line does not go in.

3.3 The monthly consolidation — thirty minutes

At the end of each month:

  1. Re-read the month's sheet and strike out anything that has not turned into a durable fact
  2. Move surviving items into the relevant module's revision notes — a labour item goes to Module 2's notes, not into a separate current affairs pile
  3. Write five questions on the month's items, in the paper's four-option format, and add them to your error log's drill

Step 2 is what makes the module integrated rather than parallel. By the end you should not have a current affairs file at all — you should have Modules 1 to 9, updated.

If a figure has not changed, write "unchanged" — you need the positive confirmation, because "I did not find a change" and "I did not look" feel identical a week later.

4.1 The two-sentence rule for anything you cannot verify

If you cannot confirm a figure from a primary source, do not carry it into the hall. An unverified number is worse than no number, because of the marking scheme: F8.0 §6 records that a blind guess has an expected value of exactly zero, while an answer given confidently from a stale fact is a negative 0.8333 dressed up as knowledge.

Where a figure is uncertain, fall back on the structure. You may not remember whether the repo rate is 5.25% or 5.50%, but you will remember that the MSF is 25 basis points above it and the SDF 25 below — and questions about the corridor's structure are more common than questions about its level.

IMPORTANT · A CURRENCY RISK IN THIS MANUAL'S OWN BACK CATALOGUE

Two of the items above are not merely new facts. They invalidate content.

  1. The four labour codes came into force on 21 November 2025, replacing 29 central labour laws — including the Industrial Disputes Act 1947, the Payment of Wages Act 1936, the Minimum Wages Act 1948 and the Factories Act 1948
  2. The Employees' Pension Scheme, 2026 has replaced EPS-95 with effect from 29 June 2026

Modules 2 and 3 were completed before this manual's build pipeline existed and are not in src/ — see register §9. If they were written before late 2025, they teach the repealed Acts and EPS-95 as live law, which is a serious defect in the two modules that together carry about 52 planning marks.

This cannot be checked from inside the repository, because the sources are not here. It is now the strongest reason to re-supply them: not merely so the master book can be built, but so that they can be read against the codes and corrected. Recorded in register §9 as the top of the queue.

5.2 How to read a change like the labour codes

When a statute is replaced, the examinable content is rarely the fine print. It is:

  1. What replaced what, and from when — four codes, 29 Acts, 21 November 2025
  2. Which Acts were subsumed into which code — wages into the Code on Wages; ID Act, Trade Unions Act and Standing Orders Act into the Industrial Relations Code; EPF, ESI, gratuity, maternity benefit into the Code on Social Security; Factories Act and Mines Act into the OSH Code
  3. The definitions that changed — notably a uniform definition of "wages" across all four codes, and the new statutory categories of gig worker and platform worker
  4. The new numbers — thresholds, time limits, contribution rates

That is four facts per code, not four hundred. Learn those and the codes are covered to the depth this paper requires.

THINK LIKE UPSC · THE THREE SHAPES IN THIS BANK

  1. The plausible institution. Every wrong option in §6.1 is a real body that publishes real reports. None is absurd. Learn the report-to-publisher table as pairs, because recognition of the body is not enough — you must recognise the pairing
  2. Enactment against commencement. Q19. A statute has a year of enactment and a date of coming into force, and for the labour codes they are six years apart. Where a question gives a date rather than a year, check which one is being asked
  3. The discontinued or superseded institution. Q8's Planning Commission, Q16's Ease of Doing Business, and the Employees' Pension Scheme 1995 in F10.2. A body or report that no longer exists is a reliable distractor, because familiarity outlives existence
