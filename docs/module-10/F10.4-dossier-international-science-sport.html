NOTE · WHY THIS CHAPTER GETS SHORTER AS IT GOES ON

F10.3 covered the half of the dossier that bears directly on this post. This chapter covers the rest, and it is arranged in descending order of yield, which means the sections get shorter as you read.

SectionLengthWhy
§1 International, §2 ReportsFullAttribution questions are permanent and cheap — F10.1 §1.1's kind 1
§3 Science, §4 DefenceModerateAttaches to Module 9, and defence items are definite
§5 Awards, sport, personsDeliberately compactThe largest part of a commercial current affairs magazine and close to zero of this paper

§5 is short as a decision, not an oversight. F10.1 §1.2 records the reasoning: sport, entertainment, obituaries and general trivia do not attach to the static syllabus, and an item that attaches to nothing is an item to discard. What §5 contains is the handful of results a general-awareness question would most plausibly use, and nothing more.

If you find yourself spending longer on §5 than on §1 and §2, you are revising the wrong chapter.

TRAP · THE MUTIRÃO DECISION AND THE BELÉM DECLARATION ARE DIFFERENT THINGS

This is the most likely confusion in the whole section, and it is a clean four-option question.

  • The Global Mutirão decision is the formal COP30 outcome, adopted by the conference. It contains the

finance package — and does not commit parties to move away from fossil fuels

  • The Belém Declaration is a separate, voluntary political declaration led by Colombia, signed by

a coalition rather than by the COP, which does address transition away from fossil fuels

So an option stating that COP30 agreed to phase out fossil fuels is wrong, and an option stating that no country committed to it is also wrong. The distinction is between what the conference decided and what a coalition declared alongside it. Content rephrased from the sources for compliance with licensing restrictions.

3. Session 3 — Science, Space and Technology

3.1 NISAR — the preceding anchor

KNOW · LAUNCHED 30 JULY 2025, TWO DAYS BEFORE THE WINDOW OPENS

NISAR — the NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar — was launched on 30 July 2025 aboard GSLV-F16. That is two days before this dossier's window begins, and it is included because it is the most significant Indian space event adjacent to the period and because a paper set in 2026 may still ask about it.

KNOW · THE TWO THAT CONNECT TO MODULE 9, AND THE ONE THAT CONNECTS TO MODULE 4

Nobel questions are usually pure recall, but three of these attach to the static syllabus, which per F10.1 §1.2 is what makes them worth learning:

  • Chemistry — metal-organic frameworks are porous crystalline materials with very large internal surface

area, used for gas storage and separation. This is F9.3's chemistry

  • Physics — macroscopic quantum tunnelling in a superconducting circuit is the physical basis of

superconducting qubits, and therefore of quantum computing. F9.2 and F9.5

  • Economics — innovation-driven growth is endogenous growth theory, which is the theoretical answer to

F4.1 §3.2's distinction between growth and development

László Krasznahorkai is the second Hungarian laureate in Literature, after Imre Kertész in 2002. Content rephrased from the sources for compliance with licensing restrictions.

IMPORTANT · THE GAMES CLOSED ONE DAY AFTER THE CUT-OFF

The Glasgow Commonwealth Games concluded on 2 August 2026 — one day after this dossier's stated cut-off of 1 August 2026.

The Games are included, and the straddle is stated, because §0 of F10.3 commits the dossier to applying the cut-off strictly and declaring rather than concealing the edge cases. The final medal tally is therefore a 2 August figure, even though the great majority of the competition falls inside the window.

If your examination's own cut-off is stated as 1 August 2026, a question on the final tally would be outside it — but questions are rarely that precise, and the tally is worth knowing either way. Content rephrased from the sources for compliance with licensing restrictions.

THINK LIKE UPSC · THE THREE SHAPES IN THIS BANK

  1. The previous edition. Q1 (Baku, Dubai), Q9 (118th), Q18, Q19, Q21 (earlier Nobel citations), Q25 (36 Rafales), Q33 (Birmingham, Gold Coast). Seven of thirty-five questions offer the correct answer to last year's version of the same question. For any recurring event — a COP, an index, a Nobel, a Games — the distractors are its own history
  2. The institutional first against the technical first. Q16 and Q17 are the same satellite asked twice: joint ISRO–NASA and dual-frequency radar. Where an achievement has two distinct claims to novelty, expect both
  3. The component offered as the total. Q28 and Q29 — 131 against 113, and 5 against 13. This is the same pattern as F4.1's Rajya Sabha strength: the examiner offers a real number from the same table
