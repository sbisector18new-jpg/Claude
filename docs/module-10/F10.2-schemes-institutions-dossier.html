1. Session 1 — The Four Labour Codes

1.1 The central fact

CodeYear of enactment

Industrial Relations Code2020

All four were brought into force with effect from 21 November 2025, and they replace 29 central labour laws. The central rules giving them full effect were notified in 2026, the Government having considered objections to the draft rules published in December 2025.

Sources: The Hindu on operationalisation and the 29 Acts · Economic Times on the commencement date · EY alert on the codes taking effect

Note the gap between enactment and commencement. The Code on Wages was passed in 2019 and came into force in 2025 — six years later. A question giving a year is probably asking about enactment; a question giving a date is asking about commencement. F10.1 §7's second shape. MUST MASTER · THE ONE THAT MATTERS MOST TO THIS POST

The Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952 is subsumed into the Code on Social Security, 2020. So is the ESI Act, gratuity, maternity benefit and workmen's compensation.

That single mapping is the most examinable fact in this session for an APFC candidate, because it means the statute the post is built on now sits inside a code. Four Acts to remember per code, and the social security code's four are the ones to know cold.

Two definitional changes worth holding:

  1. A uniform definition of "wages" applies across all four codes — previously each Act defined it differently, which is what produced decades of litigation over what counted for provident fund purposes. This is the change with the largest practical effect on EPFO administration
  2. "Gig worker" and "platform worker" are now statutory categories, defined for the first time — §2.5

2.2 The wage ceiling — a proposal, not yet law

TRAP · THE CEILING IS STILL ₹15,000. THE ₹25,000 FIGURE IS A PROPOSAL [AS AT 16 AUG 2026]

This is the item most likely to be got wrong, because the proposed figure has been widely reported and reads like a decided fact.

ItemPosition
Statutory wage ceiling for mandatory EPF coverage₹15,000 a month — unchanged since 2014
Mandatory employee contribution at the ceiling₹1,800 a month (12% of ₹15,000); contributions above that are voluntary
The proposalThe Department of Expenditure has approved a proposal to raise the ceiling to ₹25,000, subject to Cabinet approval and notification
StatusNot yet in effect
BackgroundThe Supreme Court asked the Government to reassess the ₹15,000 limit, unchanged since 2014

Sources: Livemint on the Department of Expenditure approval · Economic Times on the ₹1,800 cap and voluntary excess · Economic Times on the Supreme Court direction

The discipline this teaches is general and worth more than the fact. Current affairs reporting does not distinguish clearly between proposed, approved, notified and in force, and an examiner does. Four stages:

proposed » approved by a department » Cabinet approval » notified and in force

Only the last is law. Whenever you record a change in your monthly sheet (F10.1 §3.2), write which of the four stages it has reached. Q27 turns on exactly this. Content rephrased from the sources for compliance with licensing restrictions.

Two things to be precise about, because both are widely misreported:

  1. The minimum pension has NOT been increased. It remains ₹1,000, as it has since 2014, despite sustained demands for ₹7,500. An option asserting an increase is wrong
  2. The formula has not changed. EPS 2026 is largely a consolidation and a service-standards reform — the 20-day settlement and the 12% penalty on the EPFO are the substantive novelties

TRAP · THE COVER AMOUNTS ARE ALL DIFFERENT AND ALL ROUND NUMBERS

₹5 lakh (PM-JAY, per family per year), ₹2 lakh (PMJJBY, life), ₹2 lakh (PMSBY, accident), ₹1,000–5,000 (APY, monthly pension), ₹1,000 (minimum EPS pension), ₹5 lakh (EPFO auto-settlement limit), ₹15,000 (EPF wage ceiling and the ELI benefit).

Seven round numbers, and the examiner's whole technique is to attach one to the wrong scheme. Learn each as a triple — scheme, amount, and unit — because the unit is what most often distinguishes the right option: PM-JAY's ₹5 lakh is per family per year, not per person, and the EPFO's ₹5 lakh is an auto-settlement threshold, not a benefit.

THINK LIKE UPSC · THE FOUR SHAPES IN THIS BANK

  1. Proposed against notified. Q10 and Q27. The single most valuable discipline in current affairs, because reporting blurs the four stages and an examiner does not. When you record a change, record its stage
  2. The same number in two roles. Q16 — ₹15,000 is both the ELI benefit cap and the EPF wage ceiling. Q12 — ₹5 lakh is both PM-JAY's annual family cover and EPFO's auto-settlement threshold. Learn scheme, amount and unit as a triple
  3. Two adjacent dates. Q7 (29 June 2026) against the labour codes' 21 November 2025; Q19's three distinct dates for one statute — introduction, enactment, commencement. Dates cluster in a reform period and are offered for one another
  4. The widely demanded figure. Q8 — ₹7,500 is so often reported as a demand that it reads as a fact. A number that appears constantly in headlines as an aspiration is a natural distractor, and the answer is usually the unchanged status quo
