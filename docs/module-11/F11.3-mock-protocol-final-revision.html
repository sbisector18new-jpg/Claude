MUST MASTER · THIS IS THE CHAPTER THAT ACTS ON ALL THE OTHERS

Every other chapter in this manual adds coverage. This one adds nothing and is worth more than most of them, because it governs how the 994 practice questions already written are used in the weeks when retrieval matters more than coverage.

Blueprint §11.3's instruction is unambiguous: surplus time goes to mocks and the spaced-revision queue, never to new syllabus. Register §5 records that with 88% of the paper covered the binding constraint has shifted from coverage to decay — the risk is no longer that you will not reach Module 1, but that Module 1 will have faded by November.

So the last four weeks are not for reading. They are for retrieval, timing and error correction. §4 is the schedule. §2 is the discipline that makes mocks worth sitting. §5 is the protocol for the day, and it is short on purpose.

This chapter has no question bank, for the same reason F8.0 has none: it is a protocol, not a body of examinable content. Its output is a schedule on your wall and a log in your notebook, not marks in a bank.

Read it once, in full, before you sit Mock 1, and then use §4 and §5 as checklists.

1. Session 1 — When a Mock Is Worth Taking

1.1 The five conditions

A mock is a measuring instrument, and an instrument used carelessly gives a reading worse than no reading, because it is believed.

ConditionWhy
1 Full 120 minutes, uninterruptedThe paper's difficulty is the time constraint. A mock taken in two sittings measures knowledge, which you already know you have
2 One sitting, no breaks, no phoneTwenty minutes of the real paper will be spent tired. That has to be practised
3 Unseen questionsA question you have met before measures memory of the answer, not retrieval of the reasoning
4 At the same time of day as the real paperIf the RT is a morning paper, do not take mocks at midnight
5 Scored honestly, including the negative markingA score computed without the penalty flatters you by exactly the amount you most need to see

Condition 3 is the one that limits how many genuine mocks are available, and §3 addresses it directly and honestly.

1.2 How many, and when

MockWhenPurpose
Mock 1 (F11.2)Week 8 — F8.0's calendar places it thereDiagnostic. Establishes the baseline and the weak parts
Mock 2Week 10Tests whether the week 8 diagnosis has been acted on
Mock 3Week 12Mid-point check under improved conditions
Mock 4Two weeks before the paperFull dress rehearsal, same time of day
Mock 5One week beforeFinal calibration. Not later

TRAP · DO NOT SIT A MOCK IN THE LAST THREE DAYS

A poor score three days before the paper does damage out of all proportion to its information value: there is no longer time to act on it, and the loss of confidence is carried into the hall.

The last mock goes one week out. The final three days are for wall sheets, the error log and sleep — §5.

Equally, do not sit mocks more often than fortnightly. A mock consumes unseen material and produces about ninety minutes of analysis; sitting them weekly means either skipping the analysis, which wastes the mock, or exhausting the material, which leaves nothing for the dress rehearsal.

2. Session 2 — The Post-Mock Protocol

2.1 The ninety minutes after

Do these five things in order, on the same day.

StepTimeWhat
1 Score it honestly10 minIncluding the −0.8333. Count blanks separately from wrongs
2 Complete the analysis table10 minF11.2 §15 — accuracy, attempt rate, weakest part, marks lost to penalty
3 Read EVERY explanation40 minIncluding for questions you answered correctly. A right answer for the wrong reason is a future error
4 Log every error20 min§2.3's format. This is the step that compounds
5 Write one line5 minWhat will I do differently in the next fortnight? One sentence, specific

Step 3 is the one candidates skip and the one that matters most after step 4. Roughly one right answer in six at this level is a lucky guess or correct reasoning applied to the wrong feature — and you cannot tell which without reading the explanation.

2.2 Reading the four numbers

F11.2 §15 gives the table. The diagnosis is mechanical:

SymptomDiagnosisTreatment
Accuracy below 75%Guessing too looselyAttempt fewer. Re-read F11.1 §1.2 and apply the elimination rule strictly
Attempt rate below 80%Too slow, or too cautiousSpeed drills on the cheap formats — vocabulary at 25 seconds (F8.1), recall at 20
Accuracy fine, attempt rate fine, total still lowA specific subjectRe-read the weakest part's chapter and redo its bank
Marks lost to penalty above 15Elimination discipline failingPractise naming the eliminated option before answering

The distinction that matters: speed, accuracy and subject knowledge are three different faults with three different treatments, and a single total score conceals which you have. A candidate who treats a speed problem by reading more chapters will not improve.

2.3 The error log

MUST MASTER · THE FOURTH COLUMN IS THE WHOLE POINT

F8.0 §5 introduced this and it is restated here because in the last four weeks the error log becomes the primary study material.

DateSourceWhat I choseCorrectWhy I got it wrong — in my own words
12 OctMock 1 Q77₹25,000₹15,000Took a reported proposal as notified law
12 OctMock 1 Q3238%20%Used 2024 as the base when the stem said 2025
12 OctMock 1 Q71(a)(d)Read past the word "not" in the stem

"Silly mistake" is not a diagnosis and teaches nothing. The entry must be specific enough to act on: "I applied the percentage before the deduction", "I used the slant height as the vertical height", "I did not check which year the stem named".

Because when you have thirty entries you will find that four or five root causes produce most of your errors. F8.0 §5 predicts the top three: missing the word "not", unit and conversion failures, and formula swaps within a matched pair. Mock 1's own analysis in F11.2 §16 shows all three present.

Four or five root causes is a tractable problem. "Get better at the paper" is not.

The weekly review, fifteen minutes: re-attempt every error of the week without looking; star any you repeat; re-test starred items every week until clean; count root causes and name the top three.

3. Session 3 — Assembling Mocks 2 to 5

IMPORTANT · THE HONEST POSITION ON MOCKS 2 TO 5

Mock 1 in F11.2 is 120 fresh questions. Mocks 2 to 5 are not written out in this manual, and the reason should be stated plainly rather than disguised.

Four further papers would be 480 more questions with explanations. Authoring them to the standard of F11.2 in one sitting is not something I can do honestly, and a padded set of low-quality items would be worse than none — you would be calibrating against questions that do not resemble the paper. This is the same judgement F8.0 §0 records about its calendar: it schedules existing questions rather than pretending to contain 72 days of fresh ones.

What this section gives you instead is an assembly recipe. The manual contains 994 practice questions across 35 chapters. Drawn correctly, they make a paper of the right shape, and — critically — questions you answered weeks ago and have not seen since function as substantially unseen at retrieval. Spaced re-testing is not a poor substitute for fresh questions; it is what the last four weeks should consist of anyway.

Where the recipe cannot reach: Modules 1, 2, 3 and 5 are not in this repository (register §9), so 44 of the 120 questions in each mock must come from your own material or from the previous papers. That gap is the strongest practical reason to supply those modules and the two papers into pyq/.

TRAP · CHECK YOUR OWN LABOUR AND SOCIAL SECURITY QUESTIONS BEFORE USING THEM

F10.1 §5.1 records the currency risk and it applies directly here. If your Module 2 and Module 3 notes pre-date 21 November 2025, their questions may test the Industrial Disputes Act, the Payment of Wages Act and EPS-95 as live law — and the answers will be wrong.

Before drawing 21 questions from that material, check three things:

  1. Does it treat the four codes as in force?
  2. Does it give the EPF wage ceiling as ₹15,000 and not describe a revision as effective?
  3. Does it refer to EPS-95 or to the Employees' Pension Scheme, 2026?

A mock built on superseded law trains the wrong answer, which is worse than not sitting it. F10.2 §1 and §2 carry the current position.

3.2 Practical assembly

  1. Write the 120 question references on one sheet — F8.1 Q7, F8.1 Q19, F8.5 Q26… — in a shuffled order, not grouped by subject. This removes F11.2's part scaffolding, per its §0 note
  2. Number them 1 to 120 on your answer sheet
  3. Keep the answer key on a separate sheet, face down
  4. Interleave subjects deliberately. Put a vocabulary question at 95 and a mensuration problem at 12, because that is what the real paper does and it is what makes the three-pass method necessary
  5. Balance the key. Check the letters come out roughly 30/30/30/30; if one letter is short, swap in questions until it is not. A key you can guess is a mock that flatters you

4. Session 4 — The Final Four Weeks

4.1 The principle

In the last four weeks, retrieval beats coverage. Nothing new is read. Everything is recalled, timed, or corrected.

F8.0 §3.3 already applies this to the evening drill — from week 9 its calendar stops introducing material and runs only error-log work and timed sections. §4.2 extends it to the morning slot.

4.2 The schedule

Two hours a day: one hour of module revision in the morning, thirty minutes of F8.0 drill in the evening, thirty minutes of error log. Saturdays carry the mocks.

MUST MASTER · THE THREE THINGS THIS SCHEDULE DOES DELIBERATELY

1 It revises by WALL SHEET, not by chapter. Every chapter but F8.0 ends with a five-minute wall sheet, and 34 of them is about three hours for the entire manual. In the last month you should be able to recover a chapter from its wall sheet; if you cannot, that is the signal to re-open the chapter — and it is a far cheaper diagnostic than re-reading everything.

2 It puts the F10.1 §4 verification sweep at week −3. Not earlier, because figures move; not later, because you need time to absorb the twelve answers. Diarise it now.

3 It revisits Modules 2 and 3 twice, at −4 and again at −2. They are 52 planning marks, they are the subject matter of the post, and they are the two modules most exposed to the labour codes and EPS 2026. If your material for them is old, the second pass is where you correct it.

And note what the last week does NOT contain: no mock, no new chapter, no new source. Wall sheets, the starred error log, and rest.

4.3 The spaced-revision queue

For anything you have starred twice in the error log, use expanding intervals: re-test after 1 day, then 3, then 7, then 14. An item cleanly recalled at 14 days is safe. An item failed at any interval goes back to the beginning of the queue.

This applies to about thirty or forty items, not to the syllabus. It is a scalpel, not a schedule.

5.1 The day before

DoDo not

5.2 The morning

  1. Eat. Two hours of sustained attention is a physical task
  2. Arrive early. Reporting time, not start time
  3. Carry only what is permitted. Admit card, identity proof, the pens specified
  4. In the last five minutes before the paper, read nothing. Do not open a book in the hall — a half-remembered fact glimpsed at the door displaces a well-remembered one

5.3 In the hall

MUST MASTER · THE SIX RULES, IN ORDER OF WHEN THEY APPLY

1 First minute — write the time budget on the rough sheet. Pass 1 ends at T+45. Pass 2 ends at T+100. You will not calculate reliably under pressure later.

2 Pass 1, 45 minutes — answer only what you know immediately. Anything requiring calculation or deliberation is skipped. Mark skipped questions with a clear symbol.

3 Pass 2, 55 minutes — the calculable ones. Mensuration, DI, arithmetic. You now know how many remain and how long you have for each.

4 Pass 3, 20 minutes — eliminate and guess. Fill every bubble where you can rule out one option. A blind guess is worth zero; a one-option elimination is worth +0.28 — F11.1 §1.2.

5 Throughout — underline the word "not". About a fifth of the paper is negatively framed, and misreading one costs a 3.33-mark swing on a question you knew.

6 Do not leave early. There is no advantage to it, and pass 3 is worth several marks. Use all 120 minutes.

And one rule about the paper's psychology: if a question looks familiar and an option feels obviously right, that is the moment to slow down for five seconds. The paper's dominant trap is a correct fact about something adjacent — F11.1 §3.2. Familiarity is exactly the sensation that trap produces.

5.4 Afterwards

The RT is 75% of the final weight; the interview carries the rest. Do not attempt a post-mortem with other candidates outside the hall — it is unreliable and demoralising in equal measure.

Keep your rough sheet and your recollection of any question you were unsure of. If a further stage follows, those are the topics to revisit — and if the paper is analysed for a future sitting, that record is the raw material for a PYQ update, which register §9 and F11.1 §0 both record as the manual's outstanding need.

ONE LINE FOR THE INTERVIEW The value of a mock is not the score but the diagnosis, because a total conceals which of three separate faults produced it — insufficient speed, loose elimination, or a specific gap in knowledge — and those require entirely different remedies in the weeks that remain.
