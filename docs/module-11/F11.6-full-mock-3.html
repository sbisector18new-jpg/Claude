NOTE · WHY THE COUNT AND ASSERTION-REASON LOADING IS THE RIGHT WAY TO MAKE A MOCK HARDER

There are two ways to harden a paper. You can ask obscurer facts, or you can ask the same facts in formats that require complete rather than partial knowledge. The first produces a paper that does not resemble the examination and teaches nothing. The second produces exactly the pressure the real paper applies.

F11.4 §3.1 explains the mathematics: in the older 1 and 2 only format, confident knowledge of two statements often determined the answer, whereas the count format requires you to adjudicate every statement. One uncertain statement destroys the item. Seven such items here is 17.5 marks that cannot be half-won.

So the facts in this paper are not exotic. The formats are. That is the design.

Passage for Questions 15 to 18.

There is a temptation, when a body of law is consolidated, to treat the consolidation as the reform. It is not. Replacing twenty-nine enactments with four is an act of drafting, and drafting is the cheapest part of any reform programme. What determines whether a labour code improves the position of a worker is not the elegance of its definitions but the density of the administrative machinery available to enforce them, and that machinery is not created by a gazette notification.

The point can be put more sharply. A statute that extends an entitlement to a worker who has no registered employer has extended nothing until somebody can identify the worker, identify the person liable, and collect from that person. Each of those three steps is an administrative capability rather than a legal one. This is why the sceptical question to ask of any social security reform is not whether the entitlement is generous but whether the obligation is collectible. Generosity on paper is free; collection is expensive, and it is where reform programmes are quietly abandoned.

None of which is an argument against consolidation. Simplification has genuine value: a single registration is cheaper for the employer than six, and a single definition of wages removes an avenue of avoidance. But these are gains in compliance cost, not in coverage, and the two are routinely conflated by those announcing the reform and by those opposing it.

MUST MASTER · HOW TO READ THE TREND, AND THE ONE PATTERN THAT MEANS TROUBLE

The good trajectory: totals fall a little, accuracy holds, attempts rise, negatives fall. That is a candidate who is converting knowledge into marks and whose elimination is tightening. Score 160 to 175 here with accuracy at or above 78% and you are in contention.

The bad trajectory, and the only one that requires a change of plan: total holds up but accuracy falls while attempts rise. That means you are buying marks with volume and the negatives are eating the purchase. It is the single most common way a well-prepared candidate misses the cut-off, because the total looks acceptable until the negatives are netted.

If that is your pattern, do exactly one thing in the remaining weeks: stop attempting anything on which you cannot name a hard elimination, per F11.4 §1.2. Your total will fall for one mock and then rise past where it was.
