NOTE · WHY THE PAPER IS SET OUT IN LABELLED PARTS

A real paper may interleave subjects, and F11.1 §1.3 explains why that matters — a cheap vocabulary question can sit at number 95.

This mock is nevertheless arranged in labelled parts, for one reason: it makes the score analysis in §15 possible. A per-module score tells you where to spend the remaining weeks; an undifferentiated total of 183 tells you nothing.

So use the parts for diagnosis, but do not let them change how you attempt the paper. Apply the three-pass method across the whole 120, not part by part — skip an expensive question in Part III and come back to it after Part XI, exactly as you would in the hall. Mocks 2 to 5, assembled per F11.3 §3, should be interleaved to remove this scaffolding once the diagnosis has been done.

2.3 Reading comprehension

Questions 16 to 20 relate to the following passage.

The consolidation of twenty-nine central labour statutes into four codes was presented as a simplification, and in a formal sense it is one: an employer who once consulted a dozen Acts now consults four. But the more consequential change is not the reduction in number. It is the adoption of a single definition of wages across all four codes. Under the earlier regime each statute defined the term for its own purposes, and the resulting divergence produced decades of litigation over which allowances counted for provident fund contributions and which did not. A uniform definition removes that question at a stroke, and in doing so it alters the cost of employment rather than merely the paperwork surrounding it. Employers who had structured remuneration so as to keep the wage component small now find that the structure no longer achieves its object. Whether the codes simplify compliance is therefore a smaller question than whether they redistribute cost, and it is the second question that has governed the pace of implementation.
