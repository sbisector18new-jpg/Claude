The eleven parts and their question counts are identical to Mock 1, because both are built to the same planning weightage — and because holding the composition constant is what makes two mocks comparable. Passage for Questions 15 to 18.

Social insurance rests on a proposition that is easy to state and hard to sustain: that risks which are unbearable for one person become manageable when shouldered by many. The arithmetic is the arithmetic of the insurance pool, but the politics is not. A private insurer may decline the applicant most likely to claim; a social insurance scheme is obliged to include precisely that person, and is therefore obliged to compel the participation of those least likely to claim. Compulsion is not a regrettable feature of social security. It is the mechanism.

This explains a pattern that puzzles observers of Indian social security. Coverage has historically been tied to the formal enterprise, not to the worker, because the enterprise is where compulsion could be administered. Where there was a registered employer with a payroll, contributions could be deducted and enforced; where there was not, the worker fell outside, however great the need. The consequence was that the schemes reached the workers who needed them least intensely and missed those who needed them most. Recent architecture attempts to detach entitlement from the enterprise, extending obligations to aggregators and platforms and building worker-level registration. Whether it succeeds turns less on generosity than on administration: whether a contribution can be collected at all where there is no payroll to deduct it from.
