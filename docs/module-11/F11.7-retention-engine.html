MUST MASTER · THE ENEMY IS NO LONGER COVERAGE

This manual now holds around 230 hours of reading and 1,459 practice questions. F11.3 §1 already names the consequence, and Register §5 records it: with the syllabus covered, the binding constraint has moved from coverage to decay. The risk is not that you will fail to reach a module. It is that the module you read in September will have gone by November.

Nothing in the manual fought that. Wall sheets are the right content for the fight and the wrong mechanism — a wall sheet is re-read, and re-reading is the least efficient form of study known, because recognising a fact feels exactly like knowing it.

This chapter supplies the mechanism. decks/ holds 4,403 cards generated from the manual's own sources. §2 is why they are shaped as they are, §4 is the schedule, and §5 is the part most people skip and should not: what to do when a card fails.

0. How This Chapter Works

STUDY SESSION · CHAPTER F11.7

One session, forty minutes, done once. This is a setup chapter, not a study chapter. You read it, you import seven files, you start a daily habit that costs twenty minutes.

Then you never read it again except §5, which you will need in week three when the first cards start failing and you are tempted to do the wrong thing about it.

The decks are built by build/decks.py from src/. They are not a separate body of content to be maintained, and that is deliberate: a fact that changes in a chapter changes in the deck on the next build. Given how much of this syllabus moved in 2025–26, any deck that had to be hand-edited would be wrong within a month.

1. Why Re-reading Fails and Retrieval Does Not

The finding is old, replicated and inconvenient: testing yourself on material produces far more durable memory than reviewing it, and it does so while feeling worse at the time. Re-reading produces fluency — the text runs smoothly, nothing snags — and fluency is mistaken for mastery. Retrieval produces effort, hesitation and the occasional blank, all of which feel like failure and are in fact the mechanism working.

Two consequences for this exam specifically.

First, the wall sheets are being used wrongly by almost everyone. They are excellent — dense, atomic, already stripped to the examinable core. But reading one is recognition practice, and the paper does not test recognition. F11.4 §2 makes the point from the other direction: the whole trap taxonomy works by supplying options that feel familiar. A candidate trained on recognition is trained on precisely the faculty the paper exploits.

Second, spacing beats massing. Reviewing a fact on days 1, 3, 8 and 21 produces stronger retention than reviewing it four times on day 1, at identical total cost. This is why a daily twenty-minute habit outperforms a Sunday three-hour revision block, and why starting the habit late is expensive in a way that cannot be made up by working harder in the last fortnight.

THINK LIKE UPSC · WHY THIS PAPER PUNISHES DECAY UNUSUALLY HARD

Most exams let partial knowledge earn partial credit. This one does not. F11.1 §1.2 shows a wrong answer costs 0.8333 against a correct answer's 2.5 — so a half-remembered fact is not worth a third of a mark, it is worth less than nothing if it produces false confidence.

And F11.4 §3's T-09 shows the specific mechanism: the distractors are superseded values — ₹6,500 for ₹15,000, 1.61% for 1.16%. A decayed memory does not return "I don't know". It returns the neighbouring value with the same feeling of familiarity. That is why decay on this paper converts directly into negative marks, and why the numbers deck is reviewed on the shortest interval of the seven.

2. How the Cards Were Built

Four rules governed generation. They matter because they are the difference between a deck that trains the exam and a deck that trains a feeling of competence.

MUST MASTER · THE FOUR RULES

1. Recognition is not recall. An MCQ's answer is on the page. No card front reproduces options. Every card either deletes the fact from its own sentence, or asks a question whose answer is not visible.

2. One fact to a card. A wall-sheet line reading BRASS = Cu + Zn · BRONZE = Cu + Sn becomes two cards. A card holding two facts fails for the wrong reason and hides which half you lost.

3. Discrimination beats assertion. Knowing ₹15,000 is nearly worthless while ₹6,500 feels equally familiar. So the confusion tables become their own deck, and those cards name the distractor and require you to refuse it.

4. Never fabricate a question. Where a line had no clozeable target and no natural split it was skipped and counted — 440 of them — rather than turned into a vague prompt. The count prints on every build, so the gap stays visible instead of being papered over.

Modules 1, 2, 3 and 5 — polity, labour law, social security and accounting foundations, 97.5 of the 300 planning marks. Their sources are not in src/, so nothing was mined from them.

Labour law and social security are 52 marks and they are the substance of the post. That they are the missing modules is the worst possible distribution of a gap.

incoming/README.md is the upload target. Markdown recovers 100% of available cards; PDF recovers 73%, measured by building the same chapter both ways. The missing quarter is almost entirely Deck 4, because converting a question to free recall needs the answer table to survive as a table.

3. Setting Up, Once

  1. Install Anki — desktop for import, phone for review. The phone is where the twenty minutes actually get done, in queues and between things.
  2. File » Import, select each of the seven files in decks/, click Import. No column mapping: the note type, deck and tag column are declared inside each file.
  3. Set new cards per day to 40 and reviews to unlimited. Forty a day clears 4,403 in about fifteen weeks; if you have less time than that, see §4.3.
  4. Turn off the option that buries siblings, so both cloze deletions of one fact can appear in a day.

CHECK YOURSELF · SETUP

Import is correct if the deck list shows seven subdecks under APFC, the numbers and core-facts decks show as Cloze, and a card from Deck 2 shows a tick line and a cross line on its back. If instead you see one deck called Default with 4,403 notes, the header directives were stripped — re-import the file unmodified.

4. The Schedule

4.1 The daily habit

Twenty minutes, every day, on the phone. Not thirty on some days and none on others: the interval arithmetic assumes daily attendance, and a two-day gap does not cost two days of progress, it collapses the scheduling for every card that came due in the gap.

Order matters. Do due reviews first, always, then new cards with whatever time is left. A backlog of reviews is decay in progress; new cards are only ever an addition.

TRAP · DO NOT ADD NEW CARDS IN THE LAST TWO WEEKS

New cards generate reviews on a 1-, 3-, 8-day rhythm, so a card added fourteen days out is still consuming attention on the morning of the paper, and consuming it at the worst moment — while it is still unstable.

F11.3 §4 sets the last four weeks for retrieval, timing and error correction. Adding cards inside two weeks is coverage work disguised as revision work. Stop at day 14 and let the queue drain.

4.3 If you have less than fifteen weeks

Do not raise the daily new-card count — that inflates reviews quadratically and collapses in week three. Cut scope instead, in this order:

  1. Keep Decks 1, 2 and 6 whole. 1,305 cards, and they are the highest-yield in the set.
  2. Filter Deck 3 by tag to your weakest four modules: tag:syllabus::labour-law and so on.
  3. Drop Deck 4 entirely. Its content is reachable by sitting the mocks, which you are doing anyway.

That floor is about 1,600 cards and clears in six weeks at 40 a day.

5. When a Card Fails

This is the section that decides whether the engine works, and it is where the instinct is wrong.

The instinct is to re-read the chapter. Do not. A single lapse is normal and is the schedule working — the algorithm found the edge of your retention, which is exactly what it is for. Press Again and move on.

Act on the third lapse of the same card, not the first. Three failures is no longer decay, it is a defective card or a genuinely unlearned fact. Then, and only then, do one of these three things.

The cardThe diagnosisThe action
Fails because two facts are tangledYou have a confusion, not a gapFind its partner in Deck 2. If absent, write one card naming both, in the Deck 2 shape
Fails because the fact is isolatedNo structure to hang it onRead that chapter's §, once. This is the only case that justifies going back to prose
Fails because the card is badAmbiguous cloze, missing contextFix or delete it. A bad card is worse than no card — it trains hesitation

THINK LIKE UPSC · A LAPSE IS INFORMATION, AND IT IS THE SAME INFORMATION F11.3 §4.2 WANTS

F11.4 §9.4 builds an error signature from mock answers; F11.3 §4.2 keeps a paper error log. A repeated card lapse is the same signal arriving earlier and cheaper — before it costs 0.8333 in a mock.

So read the two together. If Deck 2 keeps failing on superseded instruments and your mock errors cluster on T-09, that is not two problems. It is one, and it has a name, and F11.4 §5.1 is the fix.

6. What This Does Not Do

Stated plainly, because a deck invites over-confidence.

It does not teach. Every card was generated from a chapter you are expected to have read. A card is a retrieval handle on existing knowledge, and cards on an unread chapter produce memorised strings that collapse the moment the paper rephrases them.

It does not replace mocks. Cards train fact retrieval; the paper additionally demands 60-second pacing, OMR discipline, and the elimination judgment of F11.1 §1.2. F11.2, F11.5 and F11.6 remain the instrument for those. A candidate with 4,403 mature cards and no mocks will still mismanage the two hours.

It does not cover the missing 97.5 marks. See §2.2.

And it does not fix a wrong fact. The cards inherit their sources exactly. Where this manual is out of date the deck will drill the error with perfect efficiency — which is why every figure carries its instrument and vintage, and why decks/ is regenerated rather than hand-maintained.
